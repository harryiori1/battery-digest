## 01 Nio Unveils 60 kWh Battery Pack for Firefly EV
Nio announced that its upcoming Firefly compact crossover will be equipped with a brand‑new 60 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack, slated for launch on 23 October 2026. The larger pack raises the model’s WLTP‑rated range to roughly 550 km (342 mi), a 30 % improvement over the current 45 kWh version. Nio also confirmed that the pack supports its proprietary 800 V fast‑charging architecture, allowing an 80 % charge in just 15 minutes under optimal conditions.

The 60 kWh pack is built in Nio’s Jiangsu “Blue Box” cell‑to‑pack facility, where the company has been scaling its in‑house LFP cell production to reduce reliance on external suppliers. According to Nio, the new pack uses a higher energy‑density cathode formulation (≈165 Wh/kg) and a redesigned thermal‑management module that eliminates the need for active liquid cooling, cutting system weight by 12 kg compared with the previous generation.

Strategically, the move signals Nio’s intent to broaden its portfolio beyond premium sedans and SUVs, targeting the rapidly growing sub‑compact segment in China and potentially in Europe. The larger LFP pack also aligns with China’s policy push for “green” battery chemistries that avoid cobalt and nickel, reducing supply‑chain risk and cost. Analysts estimate the Firefly’s price could start near ¥149,900 (≈ US$21,000), making it one of the most affordable EVs with a 60 kWh battery.

The announcement follows a wave of Chinese OEMs upgrading LFP capacities, a trend driven by recent breakthroughs in electrolyte additives that improve low‑temperature performance. If Nio can maintain its 800 V fast‑charging network, the Firefly could become a benchmark for high‑range, low‑cost EVs in the segment.

## 02 Guangdong‑Hong Kong‑Macao Battery Passport Pilot Launches Data Dashboards
A joint initiative between the Guangdong‑Hong Kong‑Macao Greater Bay Area (GBA) authorities and the industry consortium Zhongchuang Innovation Aviation has entered the operational trial phase of the “Battery Passport” system. The pilot, which began on 14 October 2026, provides a digital record for every battery cell produced or imported into the GBA, capturing data on raw‑material sourcing, manufacturing batch, safety testing, and end‑of‑life recycling pathways.

Two public data dashboards were unveiled simultaneously: one for regulators to monitor compliance with the region’s new safety and recycling standards, and another for OEMs and downstream users to verify cell provenance in real time. The system leverages blockchain‑based immutable ledgers and QR‑code tagging, enabling a smartphone scan to retrieve the full lifecycle history of a cell. Early adopters include CATL, BYD, and several smaller cell makers that have integrated the passport into their production lines.

The pilot aims to address three persistent challenges in the Chinese battery market: (1) counterfeit or sub‑standard cells infiltrating the supply chain, (2) lack of transparency around cobalt and lithium sourcing, and (3) difficulty in tracking batteries for proper recycling. By standardising data capture, the passport is expected to reduce warranty disputes by up to 15 % and improve recycling recovery rates by an estimated 8 % within the first year.

If successful, the GBA plans to expand the passport to all of mainland China by 2028, potentially influencing the forthcoming national “Battery Traceability Regulation” slated for 2029. Industry observers note that the initiative could become a model for other regions grappling with supply‑chain opacity, especially as the global market shifts toward higher‑energy‑density chemistries that demand stricter safety oversight.

## 03 September 2026 China Battery Industry Report Shows Record Production and Doubling of Energy‑Storage Sales
The September 2026 edition of the China Battery Industry Report Card, released by the China Battery Industry Association, reveals that the sector continued its rapid expansion, posting 87 GWh of cell production for the month—up 9 % month‑over‑month and 18 % year‑to‑date. Total battery sales reached 84 GWh, with electric‑vehicle (EV) applications accounting for 71 % and stationary energy‑storage systems (ESS) the remaining 29 %.

Most striking is the ESS segment: installed storage capacity doubled to 12 GWh in September, driven by aggressive utility‑scale projects in the North China Plain and a surge in commercial rooftop installations. The report attributes the growth to falling lithium‑ion costs (now US$95/kWh at pack level) and new government incentives that subsidise storage for grid‑balancing services.

Export volumes also accelerated, climbing 22 % to 5.3 GWh, with the United States, Europe, and Southeast Asia as top destinations. The data suggests that Chinese manufacturers are increasingly targeting overseas markets with higher‑energy‑density NCM and NCA cells, while domestic production remains dominated by LFP chemistry (≈ 62 % of total output).

The report highlights several supply‑chain trends: (1) a 15 % increase in domestic cathode material capacity, largely from new nickel‑hydroxide plants; (2) a 10 % rise in recycled‑material usage, reflecting the maturing of China’s battery‑recycling ecosystem; and (3) a modest but notable uptick in solid‑state cell pilot lines, though commercial volumes remain under 0.5 GWh.

Analysts view the figures as a strong indicator that China’s battery industry is moving from a growth phase to a consolidation and technology‑upgrading phase. The continued scale‑up of ESS capacity, in particular, signals a broader shift toward integrating batteries into grid operations, a trend that could reshape power markets worldwide.
