## 01 Rivian Expands Battery Recycling Deal with Redwood Materials
Rivian and Redwood Materials have deepened their existing recycling collaboration by adding a dedicated material‑recovery line at Rivian’s Normal, Illinois vehicle assembly plant. The new line will process spent lithium‑ion cells from Rivian’s R1 series trucks and SUVs, extracting high‑purity lithium, nickel, cobalt and graphite for reuse in future battery production. Redwood will also evaluate the feasibility of extending the operation to Rivian’s upcoming plant in Georgia and to the company’s battery‑pack testing facility in California.

The partnership, first announced in 2023, now includes a $45 million capital investment from Redwood, funded partly by a $10 million grant from the U.S. Department of Energy’s Battery Materials Initiative. Initial capacity is slated at 5 GWh of recovered material per year, enough to supply roughly 10 % of Rivian’s projected 2027 battery demand. Both companies stress that the closed‑loop system will cut the carbon intensity of Rivian’s supply chain by an estimated 30 percent, aligning with the automaker’s 2030 net‑zero goal.

Industry analysts view the move as a signal that high‑volume EV makers are moving beyond “just‑in‑time” raw‑material sourcing toward circular‑economy models. Redwood’s CEO, JB Straubel, highlighted that the recovered cathode material will be blended with virgin feedstock to meet the stringent performance specifications of Rivian’s NCA‑based packs. The expanded recycling footprint also positions Rivian to meet upcoming U.S. regulations that may require a minimum recycled‑content quota for automotive batteries.

Overall, the deal underscores the accelerating commercial viability of large‑scale battery recycling in the United States, a sector that has struggled to achieve economies of scale. If successful, the Redwood‑Rivian model could become a template for other OEMs seeking to secure supply while reducing environmental impact. ## 02 EcoFlow Launches Tiny Titan, Scalable Residential Battery System
EcoFlow has introduced the “Tiny Titan,” a compact yet modular residential energy‑storage solution aimed at the growing DIY home‑backup market. The base unit delivers 2 kWh of usable capacity in a 12 kg, wall‑mountable enclosure, with an integrated 2 kW inverter and a built‑in battery management system (BMS) that supports up to 10 kWh when stacked with up to four additional modules.

The system is designed around EcoFlow’s proprietary lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) cells, which offer a 3 000‑cycle lifespan and a 10‑year warranty. Fast‑charging capability is a key selling point: each module can be charged from 0 % to 80 % in under 30 minutes using EcoFlow’s 1 kW DC fast‑charge port, or via a standard 240 V AC wall outlet at 1 kW. The Tiny Titan also integrates a smart app that provides real‑time monitoring of state‑of‑charge, load balancing, and grid‑interaction features such as time‑of‑use optimization.

EcoFlow positions the product as a “gateway” for homeowners who want backup power without the expense of a full‑size 10 kWh system. Pricing starts at $1,199 for the 2 kWh unit, with a $300 discount for customers who purchase a two‑module bundle. Early market feedback suggests strong interest in regions prone to grid outages, especially in the southeastern United States where recent storms have highlighted the need for resilient home power.

The launch reflects a broader trend of battery manufacturers moving downstream into end‑user storage, leveraging economies of scale from automotive‑grade cell production. By offering a modular architecture, EcoFlow hopes to capture both entry‑level buyers and power‑hungry households looking to expand capacity over time. ## 03 Nio Unveils 60 kWh Battery Pack for Firefly, Extending Range to 560 km
Chinese EV maker Nio announced a new 60 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack for its Firefly compact crossover, raising the vehicle’s CLTC‑rated range to 560 km (about 348 miles). The pack replaces the previous 48 kWh unit and incorporates a higher energy‑density cell architecture that pushes LFP specific energy to roughly 210 Wh/kg—an improvement of about 15 % over Nio’s earlier cells.

Key technical upgrades include a new “Ultra‑Fast” BMS that supports 350 kW DC charging, enabling a 0‑80 % charge in roughly 15 minutes on compatible 800 V chargers. Thermal management has been refined with a dual‑layer liquid cooling plate that maintains cell temperature within a 5 °C window during high‑power bursts, improving both performance and longevity. Nio also introduced a modular pack design that simplifies replacement and recycling, aligning with the company’s “Battery as a Service” (BaaS) model.

From a market perspective, the larger pack is intended to address consumer concerns about range anxiety in the highly competitive compact SUV segment. Nio projects that the upgraded Firefly will capture an additional 5 % market share in China’s Tier‑2 cities, where daily driving distances often exceed 300 km. The company also hinted that the same 60 kWh architecture could be adapted for its upcoming sedan platform, potentially standardizing LFP across its product line by 2028.

Industry observers note that Nio’s move underscores the rapid maturation of LFP technology, which traditionally lagged behind nickel‑cobalt‑manganese (NCM) chemistries in energy density. By achieving a 560 km range with LFP, Nio demonstrates that cost‑effective, cobalt‑free batteries can now meet mainstream consumer expectations, a development that could reshape supply‑chain dynamics for raw materials.
