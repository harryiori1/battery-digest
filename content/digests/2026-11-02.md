## 01 CATL Partners with Blue Vector to Power SKYLA V30 eVTOL Aircraft
Chinese battery giant CATL announced a strategic partnership with eVTOL developer Blue Vector to supply lithium‑ion battery packs for the SKYLA V30 aircraft, the latest entrant in the urban air‑mobility (UAM) segment. The agreement, disclosed on 2 November 2026, marks CATL’s first dedicated battery contract for a commercial eVTOL platform and underscores the rapid convergence of aviation and battery technology.

The SKYLA V30 is designed for short‑range intra‑city flights, targeting a payload of up to 300 kg and a cruise speed of 250 km/h. Blue Vector has specified a 150 kWh battery system that must deliver a specific energy of at least 300 Wh/kg while maintaining a cycle life of 2,000 full‑charge cycles. CATL will provide its latest NCM‑811 cells, integrated into a modular pack architecture that allows rapid replacement—a key requirement for high‑utilisation UAM operations.

The partnership includes a joint R&D program to optimise thermal management and to certify the pack for aviation safety standards (DO‑160, SAE‑AEP‑53). Production is slated to begin in Q2 2027 at CATL’s new “Aviation Battery” line in Ningde, with an initial delivery of 50 packs to Blue Vector for the pilot fleet. The deal also features a technology‑licensing component, giving Blue Vector access to CATL’s proprietary cell‑balancing algorithms.

Why it matters: The agreement signals that battery manufacturers are moving beyond automotive and stationary storage to address the stringent weight‑and‑safety demands of eVTOLs. With the global UAM market projected to exceed $30 billion by 2035, securing a foothold early could translate into a sizeable new revenue stream for CATL. Moreover, the modular pack design could become a template for other aviation‑grade battery solutions, accelerating certification pathways and reducing overall aircraft operating costs.

The collaboration also highlights the growing importance of supply‑chain localisation; CATL will source critical materials from its existing Chinese mines, while Blue Vector plans to assemble the aircraft in North America, creating a trans‑Pacific value chain that may influence future trade policies on high‑tech battery exports.

## 02 Global EV Battery Market Share Q1‑Q3 2026: CATL Leads with 39.1%, BYD Holds 15.2%
CNEVPost released its quarterly market‑share analysis for January‑September 2026, confirming that Chinese battery makers continue to dominate the global EV battery landscape. CATL retained the top spot with a 39.1 % share of total gigawatt‑hours (GWh) shipped, while BYD held a distant second at 15.2 %. The remaining market was split among South Korean firms (LG Energy Solution 12.4 %, Samsung SDI 9.8 %) and a growing contingent of European and North‑American startups that together accounted for roughly 8 %.

The report attributes CATL’s lead to its aggressive expansion of high‑energy NCM‑811 and LFP lines, as well as the rollout of its “Tener+” 9‑MWh modular storage system in Australia, which has opened new utility‑scale revenue streams. BYD’s share, though lower, reflects the continued popularity of its Blade battery architecture in Chinese domestic models and a modest increase in overseas OEM contracts.

Supply‑chain dynamics also shaped the numbers. Lithium‑hydroxide prices fell 12 % YoY after a surge in Australian and Canadian production, easing cost pressures for NCM chemistries. Conversely, cobalt prices remained volatile, prompting several manufacturers to accelerate LFP transitions. The report notes that sodium‑ion cells, while still a niche for grid storage, contributed an additional 0.6 % of total shipments, up from 0.3 % in the previous quarter.

Implications for the industry are clear: the concentration of capacity in a handful of Chinese firms gives them significant leverage over pricing and technology standards, especially as governments worldwide tighten battery‑passport and recycling regulations. Competitors outside China will need to either secure long‑term raw‑material contracts or differentiate through next‑generation chemistries such as solid‑state or lithium‑sulfur to erode the incumbents’ market share.

The data also serves as a barometer for OEM sourcing strategies. Several European carmakers have announced “dual‑sourcing” policies to mitigate geopolitical risk, but the sheer volume of CATL’s output makes it difficult to replace without substantial investment in new capacity.

## 03 KAIST Unveils New Method to Shrink EV Batteries While Extending Range
Researchers at Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) announced a novel electrode‑design technique that could dramatically reduce the size of lithium‑ion batteries while simultaneously increasing driving range. The breakthrough, detailed in a paper released on 1 November 2026, leverages a three‑dimensional “nanofiber‑woven” scaffold that replaces the conventional flat‑plate electrode architecture.

By arranging active material particles in a porous, interlaced network, the new design achieves a volumetric energy density of 850 Wh/L—about 30 % higher than the best commercial NCM‑811 cells. At the same time, the internal resistance drops by 18 %, enabling faster charge rates (up to 3 C) without excessive heat buildup. The team demonstrated a prototype 60 kWh pack that weighs 350 kg, roughly 15 % lighter than current market equivalents, and delivers a WLTP range of 620 km in a midsize sedan testbed.

The key to the performance gain is the use of a conductive polymer binder that simultaneously provides mechanical strength and ionic pathways, eliminating the need for heavy metallic current collectors. The scaffold is fabricated via a scalable electrospinning process, which the authors claim can be integrated into existing roll‑to‑roll production lines with minimal retooling costs.

Why it matters: Battery size and weight remain the primary constraints on EV range, interior packaging, and vehicle dynamics. If KAIST’s method can be commercialised, automakers could either increase range without enlarging the pack or free up cabin space for additional features. Moreover, the reduced reliance on copper current collectors could lower material costs and improve the overall sustainability profile of the battery.

The research team is already in talks with several Korean OEMs and battery manufacturers to pilot the technology in 2028. Funding from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) will support a pilot‑scale production line slated for early 2027, suggesting a relatively short path from lab to market.
