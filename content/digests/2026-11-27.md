## 01 World’s Largest Electric Bulk Carrier Packs 23.5 MWh Battery  
BRF Froan, the world’s biggest bulk‑cargo ship dedicated to coal, sugar and other commodities, has just been fitted with a staggering 23.5 MWh lithium‑ion battery system – the largest ever installed on a commercial vessel. The battery, supplied by a consortium of European cell manufacturers, occupies roughly 1,200 m³ of the ship’s hull and weighs about 250 tonnes, delivering a continuous power output of 30 MW. This enables the 200‑metre‑long carrier to operate entirely on electricity for voyages up to 300 nautical miles, covering typical short‑haul routes between European ports without emitting a gram of CO₂.  

The project is part of the EU’s “Fit for 55” decarbonisation plan and received €45 million in public funding, matched by private investment from BRF and its battery partner. Engineers had to overcome thermal‑management challenges; the pack uses a liquid‑cooling loop and a fire‑suppression system that meets IMO’s new “Battery‑Powered Ship” standards.  

From a market perspective, the 23.5 MWh pack dwarfs the 10‑MWh batteries currently being trialled on ferries and short‑range container ships, signalling that ultra‑large‑scale energy storage is becoming viable for heavy‑industry shipping. If the technology proves reliable, it could accelerate the shift away from bunker fuel in bulk transport, a sector responsible for roughly 10 % of global maritime emissions. The ship is slated for commercial service in early 2027, with a second unit already on order.

## 02 Prevost Unveils 540 kWh Electric Coach Bus  
Volvo‑owned coach builder Prevost announced the H3‑45 Electric, its first fully battery‑electric motorcoach, at a North‑American launch in Quebec. The vehicle is powered by a 540 kWh lithium‑ion pack supplied by Volvo’s battery division, delivering 450 kW (600 hp) from two independent electric motors. The pack provides a claimed 250‑mile (400 km) range on a single charge, sufficient for most intercity routes and for tour operators that run day‑long itineraries.  

Charging is handled via both CCS‑Type 1 (up to 350 kW) and the proprietary MCS (Motorcoach Charging System) that can deliver 500 kW in under 30 minutes, cutting turnaround time at depots. The bus also features regenerative braking that recovers up to 30 % of kinetic energy, extending range under stop‑and‑go conditions.  

Prevost expects to begin low‑volume production in early 2028, with a target of 1,000 units per year by 2032, primarily for the U.S. and Canadian markets where federal incentives for zero‑emission heavy vehicles are strengthening. The move aligns with a broader industry push: the North‑American coach fleet, which currently runs on diesel, accounts for roughly 2 % of total road‑transport emissions. By replacing diesel with a 540 kWh battery, each coach can avoid up to 150 t of CO₂ annually, assuming an average 150,000‑mile service year.  

The H3‑45 also incorporates a battery‑management system that monitors cell temperature, state‑of‑charge and health in real time, feeding data to fleet operators via a cloud platform for predictive maintenance.

## 03 Zeekr Expands 7X Lineup with New 85 kWh Battery Option  
Chinese premium EV brand Zeekr has rolled out an upgraded battery option for its 7X SUV, adding an 85 kWh pack to the existing 70 kWh configuration. The larger pack, built on Zeekr’s in‑house “X‑Cell” chemistry that blends high‑nickel NMC cathodes with a silicon‑enhanced graphite anode, pushes the WLTC‑rated range to roughly 620 km (385 mi), a 15 % jump over the base model.  

The announcement, made on 27 November via CnEVPost, comes with a limited‑time “Battery Boost” incentive: early buyers receive a free home‑charging wallbox (7 kW) and a complimentary subscription to Zeekr’s battery‑health monitoring service for three years. Pricing for the 85 kWh variant starts at ¥329,900 (≈ $45,000), only ¥20,000 more than the 70 kWh version, reflecting economies of scale from Zeekr’s Gigafactory in Chengdu, which now produces over 150 GWh of cells annually.  

Industry analysts see the move as a direct response to rival offerings from BYD and Nio, which have already introduced 90‑kWh packs in comparable SUVs. By expanding its battery portfolio, Zeekr aims to capture the fast‑growing “mid‑size premium SUV” segment in China, projected to grow 12 % YoY through 2028. The new pack also supports 250 kW DC fast charging, allowing an 80 % charge in under 30 minutes on a 350 kW charger, and is compatible with Zeekr’s upcoming battery‑swap stations in major Chinese cities.  

The upgrade underscores a broader trend: Chinese EV makers are increasingly offering multiple battery capacities within a single model to cater to diverse consumer preferences for range, price and charging speed, while leveraging their vertically integrated supply chains to keep costs competitive.
