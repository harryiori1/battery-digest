## 01 Amprius Receives $75 Million DoD Grant to Convert EV Battery Plant for High‑Energy Drone Cells
The U.S. Department of War has awarded a **$75 million grant** to **Amprius Technologies**, a California‑based maker of high‑energy‑density lithium‑ion cells, to repurpose an existing electric‑vehicle battery production line for military‑drone applications. The funding, announced on October 5, will enable Amprius to retrofit its **250 MW‑year facility** in Fremont, California, to produce **silicon‑nanowire anode cells** capable of delivering **energy densities above 350 Wh/kg**, a figure that rivals or exceeds current solid‑state prototypes.

The grant is part of the DoD’s **“Advanced Battery Initiative”**, which seeks to reduce reliance on foreign lithium supplies and to improve the endurance of unmanned aerial systems (UAS). Amprius plans to scale its **cell format** from the 18650 size used in passenger EVs to a **larger 21700‑type** optimized for high discharge rates required by drones. The company projects an initial output of **10 GWh per year** of drone‑grade cells within 18 months, with a potential expansion to **30 GWh** by 2029.

For the broader battery industry, the grant signals a growing **dual‑use** approach where civilian EV manufacturing capacity can be leveraged for defense needs, potentially smoothing demand cycles and justifying higher capital expenditures. It also underscores the strategic importance of **silicon‑based anodes**, which promise up to **30 % higher energy density** without sacrificing cycle life, a key metric for both long‑range drones and future EVs.

Amprius will collaborate with the **U.S. Army’s Aviation and Missile Research, Development, and Engineering Center (AMRDEC)** to certify the cells for aerospace standards, including **MIL‑STD‑810G** for vibration and temperature extremes. If successful, the partnership could accelerate the adoption of silicon‑nanowire technology across the commercial sector, lowering costs through economies of scale.

The move also raises questions about **supply chain security** for critical materials such as high‑purity silicon and cobalt‑free cathodes, prompting the DoD to explore domestic mining and recycling options. Overall, the grant represents a rare convergence of **defense funding, advanced cell chemistry, and existing EV manufacturing infrastructure**, potentially reshaping the trajectory of high‑energy battery development.

## 02 Vammo Reaches 10 Million Battery Swaps and Turns EBITDA‑Positive
Brazilian e‑motorcycle startup **Vammo** announced on October 12 that its **battery‑swap network** has surpassed **10 million swaps** since launch, and the company has achieved **EBITDA profitability** for the first time. The milestone comes after Vammo expanded its swap stations from **1,200 in São Paulo** to **3,800 across Brazil’s major metros**, serving **over 10,000 active riders** who collectively travel **more than 150 million kilometers** per year.

Vammo’s swap model relies on **standardized 48 V, 12 kWh lithium‑ion packs** that can be exchanged in **under 30 seconds**. The company’s proprietary **automated docking system** uses RFID identification and a robotic arm to lift and replace the pack, reducing labor costs and enabling 24/7 operation. Pricing is subscription‑based: riders pay **R$79 per month** for unlimited swaps, with a **R$2,500 upfront bike cost** that includes the battery pack.

Financially, Vammo reported **R$45 million in revenue** for Q3 2026, a **28 % YoY increase**, driven by both higher swap volume and a new **fleet‑service offering** for delivery companies. The EBITDA margin of **7 %** reflects improved unit economics as the cost per swap fell from **R$3.5 to R$2.2** thanks to economies of scale in pack manufacturing and the rollout of **solar‑powered swap stations** in remote locations.

The achievement is significant for the battery‑swap ecosystem, which has struggled to prove profitability outside of China’s tightly regulated market. Vammo’s success demonstrates that **high‑density urban mobility** can be supported by a **modular battery approach**, reducing range anxiety and extending the useful life of the underlying cells through **centralized thermal management and second‑life repurposing**. The company plans to introduce a **next‑generation 18 kWh pack** later this year, targeting electric scooters and light‑weight cargo bikes.

Analysts see Vammo’s model as a potential template for other emerging markets where **charging infrastructure** is sparse but **urban congestion** drives demand for low‑cost, zero‑emission transport. The profitability milestone may also attract **strategic investors** from the automotive and energy sectors looking to diversify into **battery‑as‑a‑service** platforms.

## 03 HUDDIG Boosts Hybrid Loader Battery to 61 kWh, a 40% Capacity Increase
Swedish heavy‑equipment manufacturer **HUDDIG** unveiled a major upgrade to its **1370 hybrid wheel loader** on October 3, increasing the on‑board lithium‑ion battery from **44 kWh to 61 kWh**—a **40 % boost** in usable energy. The new pack, supplied by **Northvolt**, employs a **high‑nickel NMC 811 cathode** and a **silicon‑enhanced graphite anode**, delivering a **specific energy of 210 Wh/kg** while maintaining the loader’s original weight envelope.

The upgraded battery extends the loader’s **electric‑only operating time** from **2.5 hours to roughly 4 hours** under typical construction‑site loads, allowing operators to complete a full workday without resorting to the diesel engine. The hybrid system still retains a **150 kW diesel generator** for peak power, but the increased electric capacity reduces fuel consumption by an estimated **30 %**, cutting CO₂ emissions by **≈ 1.2 t per machine per year**.

HUDDIG’s engineering team highlighted a **new battery management system (BMS)** that balances cells in real time, mitigating thermal hotspots and extending cycle life to **over 5,000 deep‑cycle equivalents**. The BMS also integrates with HUDDIG’s **telemetry platform**, providing fleet managers with live data on state‑of‑charge, health, and predictive maintenance alerts.

From a market perspective, the upgrade positions HUDDIG to compete more aggressively in **Europe’s tightening emissions regulations**, where construction equipment must meet **Euro VI** standards. The company estimates that the 61 kWh pack will be **available for retrofitting** on existing 1370 units starting Q2 2027, with a **price premium of €12,000** over the baseline model—an investment justified by projected fuel savings and lower maintenance costs.

The move also reflects a broader trend of **electrification in non‑automotive heavy machinery**, where battery technology once limited by weight and energy density is now catching up thanks to advances in **high‑nickel cathodes** and **silicon‑rich anodes**. HUDDIG’s partnership with Northvolt underscores the importance of **regional supply chains** for high‑performance cells, reducing reliance on Asian manufacturers and aligning with Europe’s strategic battery agenda.
