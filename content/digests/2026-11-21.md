## 01 Moment Energy Deploys 500 kWh Storage System Built from Retired EV Batteries
Moment Energy has introduced the **Nova** energy‑storage platform, a 500 kWh system that is assembled almost entirely from **retired electric‑vehicle battery packs**. The company sources used packs from a variety of OEMs, refurbishes them to a “second‑life” performance level, and then integrates them into a modular container that can be stacked or placed side‑by‑side on a commercial site.  

Each Nova unit houses up to **20 refurbished modules**, each delivering roughly 25 kWh, and the system is equipped with a **plug‑and‑play power conversion unit** that can be swapped out without removing the entire container. This design shortens downtime during end‑of‑life (EOL) upgrades and reduces the logistics cost of moving heavy battery packs.  

Moment Energy claims the refurbished packs retain **80 % of their original capacity**, enough to provide **10‑hour peak‑shaving** for a typical medium‑size warehouse or to serve as backup power for data‑center loads. The company also highlights a **30 % lower carbon footprint** compared with building a fresh lithium‑ion system of the same size, because the majority of the embodied energy has already been expended during the first vehicle life.  

The Nova system is already operating at a **logistics hub in Arizona** and a **cold‑storage facility in Texas**, where it has helped shave $120 k per year in electricity costs. Moment Energy plans to scale production to **2 GWh of refurbished capacity by 2028**, targeting the fast‑growing U.S. utility‑scale storage market, which is expected to exceed 1 TWh of installed capacity by 2030.  

The move underscores a broader industry shift toward **circular battery economics**, where manufacturers and recyclers are collaborating to keep lithium‑ion chemistries in service for as long as technically and economically viable. As the EV fleet ages, the supply of usable second‑life packs will increase, potentially creating a new revenue stream for OEMs and a cost‑effective alternative for large‑scale storage developers.

## 02 U.S. Cumulative Battery Storage Surpasses 200 GWh, Driven by Utility‑Scale Projects
The United States has **crossed the 200 GWh threshold** for installed battery‑energy‑storage (BES) capacity, according to data compiled by the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) and industry analysts. The milestone was reached in the first nine months of 2026, with **nearly 50 GWh added** during that period alone—an acceleration that outpaces the previous year’s growth rate by **about 35 %**.  

Utility‑scale projects dominate the new additions, accounting for **≈ 85 %** of the 2026‑to‑date installations. The largest single project is a **150 MW/600 MWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) farm in Texas**, built by a joint venture between **Vistra Energy and CATL**. This plant is designed to provide both **frequency regulation** and **peak‑shaving** services to the ERCOT grid, and it incorporates a **modular container architecture** that allows rapid scaling.  

Corporate and industrial customers are also contributing to the surge. A **500 kWh battery system at a Walmart distribution center in Illinois** and a **1 MWh micro‑grid at a California data‑center campus** were commissioned in Q3, highlighting the expanding role of storage in **load‑management and resiliency** strategies.  

Policy incentives continue to underpin the growth. The **Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)** for storage, extended through 2032, now offers a **30 % credit** for projects that pair storage with renewable generation, while several states have introduced **capacity‑based subsidies** for long‑duration storage (> 4 hours).  

Analysts warn that the rapid build‑out will stress the **lithium‑ion supply chain**, especially for **cathode materials** such as nickel‑manganese‑cobalt (NMC) and LFP. However, the market is responding with **increased domestic gigafactory capacity**—notably **Tesla’s Megapack line in Nevada** and **LG Energy Solution’s new Ohio plant**, both slated to start high‑volume production in 2027.  

The 200 GWh milestone signals that battery storage is moving from a niche ancillary service to a **core grid asset**, essential for integrating the growing share of variable renewable generation and for meeting reliability standards under the **North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC)** framework.

## 03 1967 Porsche 912 Receives EV Swap with Split 300 kWh Battery Pack
A classic‑car enthusiast has completed a **full electric conversion of a 1967 Porsche 912**, installing a **custom split‑pack battery system** that delivers **300 kWh of usable energy** and propels the car to a claimed **250 miles (400 km) of range**. The project, documented on InsideEVs, uses **two 150 kWh lithium‑ion modules** mounted fore and aft of the chassis, preserving the vehicle’s original weight distribution and handling characteristics.  

The conversion employs **high‑energy‑density NMC cells** rated at **250 Wh/kg**, giving the pack a total weight of roughly **1,200 lb (545 kg)**. A **dual‑motor setup**—one on each axle—provides **300 hp** and **300 lb‑ft of torque**, enabling 0‑60 mph in **4.8 seconds**, a performance leap over the original 100 hp flat‑four engine.  

Cooling is managed by a **liquid‑cooled plate system** that circulates through both battery modules and motor housings, maintaining cell temperatures below **35 °C** even during spirited driving. The vehicle’s **regenerative braking** system recovers up to **30 %** of kinetic energy, extending real‑world range to about **300 miles** on mixed‑city/highway cycles.  

Beyond performance, the project showcases a **modular battery architecture** that could be adapted for other low‑volume classic‑car conversions. The split‑pack design allows owners to **swap out a single module** for maintenance or upgrades without dismantling the entire powertrain, a concept that mirrors the **swap‑station model** gaining traction in emerging markets.  

The build cost is estimated at **$120,000**, including cells, motor‑controller kits, custom fabrication, and labor. While the price remains prohibitive for most hobbyists, the project demonstrates the **technical feasibility** of high‑capacity, high‑performance EV conversions for heritage vehicles, a niche that may attract boutique conversion firms as battery costs continue to fall.  

Industry observers note that such conversions could **stimulate demand for second‑life batteries**, as retired EV packs can be repurposed for lower‑power classic‑car applications, further supporting the circular‑economy narrative.
