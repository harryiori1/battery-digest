## 01 Ultra‑class haul truck packs a 4.1 MWh battery and dual‑gun MCS charging
Norsk Hydro Logistics (NHL) unveiled its NTE240E ultra‑class haul truck at a press event in Oslo on 20 November 2026. The centerpiece of the vehicle is a single‑piece 4.1 MWh lithium‑ion battery pack, the largest ever installed on a road‑legal vehicle. The pack is built from 48 kWh modules stacked in a modular “brick” architecture that allows the entire 4 MWh to be swapped in under two hours using a purpose‑built dual‑gun MCS (Multi‑Current‑Source) charger.

The dual‑gun system delivers up to 2 MW per gun, enabling a combined 4 MW charging rate that can replenish 80 % of the pack in roughly 30 minutes under optimal conditions. NHL says the charger can draw power directly from high‑voltage transmission lines, bypassing on‑site transformers, which reduces infrastructure costs for remote mining sites.

From a performance standpoint, the NTE240E can haul 240 tonnes at a maximum speed of 70 km/h, with a claimed range of 600 km on a full charge. The massive pack weighs about 30 tonnes, representing roughly 12 % of the vehicle’s gross vehicle weight. To manage thermal loads, the pack incorporates a liquid‑cooling loop with phase‑change material (PCM) inserts that keep cell temperatures below 35 °C during high‑power discharge.

Industry analysts view the development as a watershed moment for heavy‑duty electrification. The 4 MWh capacity dwarfs the 1.5 MWh packs typically seen on mining trucks, suggesting that future designs could consolidate multiple modules into a single, serviceable unit. The dual‑gun MCS architecture also signals a shift toward ultra‑fast charging solutions that could make electric haul trucks viable in regions where traditional depot charging would be impractical.

NHL plans a limited production run of 50 units for 2027, targeting iron‑ore mines in Western Australia and the Canadian Shield. The company has already secured a partnership with Siemens Energy to co‑develop the high‑power charger, and a pilot installation is slated for a joint venture with Rio Tinto later this year.

The announcement underscores the accelerating race to scale battery size and charging speed for heavy‑industry applications, a segment that has historically lagged behind passenger‑vehicle electrification. If the NTE240E meets its performance targets, it could set a new benchmark for energy‑dense, fast‑charging battery systems in the mining and construction sectors.

## 02 CATL added to Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Tech Index as the sector gains market weight
China’s leading lithium‑ion battery manufacturer Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd. (CATL) and autonomous‑driving sensor firm Hesai were officially added to the Hang Seng Tech Index on 20 November 2026, according to a notice from the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. The index, which tracks the performance of the region’s most innovative technology companies, now includes CATL as its 12th constituent, raising the battery‑sector weighting to 7.3 % of the index.

The inclusion follows a series of strategic moves by CATL to cement its global supply‑chain dominance. In the past twelve months, the company announced a 30 % increase in its annual production capacity, adding 150 GWh of NCM (nickel‑cobalt‑manganese) and LFP (lithium‑iron‑phosphate) cell lines across plants in China, Germany, and the United States. CATL also unveiled a new high‑nickel NCM 811 cathode chemistry that promises an energy density of 260 Wh/kg, a 15 % improvement over its current flagship chemistry.

Investors have responded positively: CATL’s market capitalization rose from HK$1.2 trillion to HK$1.45 trillion in the three weeks after the announcement, lifting its weight in the Hang Seng Composite Index to 1.8 %. Analysts at HSBC note that the index inclusion could attract passive‑fund inflows of up to HK$200 billion, further strengthening CATL’s balance sheet for upcoming R&D projects, including solid‑state prototypes slated for 2028.

The move also reflects broader market dynamics. As the EU and United States tighten battery‑passport regulations, companies with transparent supply‑chain data—like CATL, which recently published a blockchain‑based traceability platform—are better positioned to meet compliance requirements. Moreover, the addition of a battery‑maker to a technology‑focused index signals the sector’s maturation: batteries are no longer a peripheral component but a core technology driving the next wave of electrification across automotive, grid‑storage, and consumer‑electronics markets.

While CATL’s inclusion is a clear vote of confidence, it also raises questions about concentration risk. The index now holds three of the top five global battery manufacturers, potentially amplifying exposure to raw‑material price volatility, especially for nickel and cobalt. Nonetheless, CATL’s aggressive vertical integration—covering everything from mining contracts in Indonesia to recycling facilities in Europe—aims to mitigate such risks.

Overall, the Hang Seng Tech Index addition underscores the strategic importance of battery technology in the broader tech ecosystem and may accelerate capital allocation toward next‑generation cell chemistries and large‑scale manufacturing capacity.
