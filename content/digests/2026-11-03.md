## 01 GM and Peak Energy Accelerate Sodium‑Ion Cells for Grid‑Scale Storage
General Motors has deepened its partnership with Peak Energy, a U.S. startup specializing in sodium‑ion chemistry, by committing a $150 million strategic investment through its GM Ventures arm. The deal, announced on 23 Oct 2026, targets the development of a next‑generation sodium‑ion cell platform optimized for long‑duration grid‑scale storage rather than passenger‑vehicle applications.  

Peak’s prototype cells deliver an energy density of 140 Wh kg⁻¹ at a nominal voltage of 3.2 V, comparable to early‑stage LFP modules but with a cost advantage of roughly 30 % thanks to the abundance of sodium and the elimination of cobalt and nickel. The collaboration will see a 1‑GWh pilot production line built at Peak’s existing facility in Ohio, with first commercial shipments slated for Q2 2027.  

GM plans to integrate the cells into its “Volt‑Grid” portfolio, which already includes lithium‑ion and flow‑battery offerings. The sodium‑ion units are expected to fill a niche for 4‑ to 12‑hour duration storage, supporting renewable‑energy curtailment mitigation and frequency‑regulation services. By diversifying its chemistry mix, GM hopes to reduce exposure to lithium‑price volatility and supply‑chain bottlenecks that have plagued the industry since 2024.  

Analysts at BloombergNEF estimate that sodium‑ion could capture up to 8 % of the global stationary‑storage market by 2035 if cost targets are met. GM’s move signals a broader industry shift toward “multi‑chemistry” strategies, echoing similar announcements from European utilities and Asian manufacturers. The partnership also includes a joint R&D program focused on electrolyte formulations that improve low‑temperature performance—a historic weakness of sodium‑ion technology.  

Overall, the GM‑Peak alliance underscores the growing commercial viability of sodium‑ion cells and could accelerate the deployment of affordable, long‑duration storage across the United States. [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/23/gm-sodium-ion-battery-peak-energy/)

## 02 Nio Hits 10 Million Battery Swaps in 88 Days, Surpassing 130 Million Total
Chinese EV maker Nio announced on 3 Nov 2026 that its Power Swap network completed 10 million battery‑swap transactions in just 88 days, pushing the cumulative total past the 130 million‑swap milestone. The surge was driven by the launch of three new swap stations in Tier‑1 cities—Beijing, Shanghai, and Shenzhen—each equipped with the latest “Swap‑X” robotic system that reduces average swap time to 2.8 minutes, down from the previous 3.5‑minute average.  

Nio’s battery‑as‑a‑service model, introduced in 2021, separates the battery from the vehicle purchase price, allowing customers to pay a monthly subscription while swapping depleted packs for fully charged ones. The latest data shows an average utilization rate of 85 % per pack, compared with 70 % for conventional plug‑in charging, translating into a 30 % reduction in total cost of ownership for Nio owners.  

The company also disclosed that its latest third‑generation 84 kWh swap‑ready pack features a new LFP chemistry with a 5‑year warranty and a 1,200 km (CLTC) range, addressing range‑anxiety concerns that previously limited swap adoption. To support the rapid growth, Nio has secured a 5‑year supply agreement with CATL for 150 GWh of LFP cells, ensuring a stable pipeline for future station expansions.  

Industry observers note that Nio’s swap model could become a template for other high‑density urban markets where charging infrastructure is constrained. The rapid‑swap capability is especially attractive for ride‑hailing fleets, which Nio reports now constitute 42 % of its swap‑station traffic.  

With the 10‑million‑swap burst, Nio demonstrates that battery‑swap networks can achieve scale comparable to traditional charging networks while delivering higher utilization and lower per‑kilometer energy costs. The milestone also reinforces China’s broader push toward battery‑service ecosystems as a means to accelerate EV adoption. [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/03/nio-battery-swaps-130-million/)

## 03 China’s 707 MW Project Brings 29 GWh of LFP Storage Online
A massive lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery farm in northeast China entered commercial operation on 28 Oct 2026, delivering 29 GWh of usable storage capacity across a 707 MW power‑rating. The project, detailed by OFweek on 30 Nov 2026, is the largest single‑site LFP installation in the world and is owned by State Grid Corporation in partnership with domestic battery manufacturer BYD Energy.  

The plant consists of 58 modular “Power‑Box” units, each housing 500 MWh of LFP cells in a containerized format that allows rapid scaling and on‑site maintenance. The cells are based on BYD’s latest “Blade” architecture, offering a volumetric energy density of 210 Wh L⁻¹ and a cycle life exceeding 4,000 full cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge.  

Strategically located near the Liaoning‑Hebei interconnection corridor, the storage farm provides frequency regulation, peak‑shaving, and renewable‑energy firming services for a regional grid that sees over 30 % of its generation from wind and solar. Early performance data indicate a round‑trip efficiency of 92 % and an average dispatch cost of $45 MWh, undercutting comparable lithium‑ion projects by roughly 15 %.  

The project also incorporates a “green‑loop” recycling system: spent modules are automatically routed to a nearby BYD recycling hub where cathode material is recovered at a 96 % rate, supporting China’s circular‑economy targets for battery waste.  

Analysts at Wood Mackenzie project that similar LFP megafarms could collectively add 150 GWh of capacity in China by 2028, driven by falling cell costs—now estimated at $78 kWh—and supportive policy incentives for long‑duration storage. The 707 MW/29 GWh installation demonstrates that LFP, once considered a low‑energy‑density option, can achieve utility‑scale performance when paired with modular design and advanced thermal management.
