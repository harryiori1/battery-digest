## 01 GM Partners with Peak Energy on Sodium‑Ion Cells for Grid‑Scale Storage
General Motors announced a strategic partnership with Australian startup Peak Energy to develop sodium‑ion battery cells aimed at utility‑scale energy storage. While the chemistry will not be used in GM’s electric vehicles, the collaboration is a clear bet on lithium‑free technology to diversify the automaker’s supply chain and reduce exposure to volatile lithium markets.

Peak Energy’s sodium‑ion cells promise an energy density of roughly 100‑120 Wh kg⁻¹—about half that of conventional lithium‑ion packs—but at a projected cost of $70 kWh⁻¹, significantly lower than the $120‑$130 kWh⁻¹ typical for lithium‑ion today. GM Ventures is committing an undisclosed equity stake and a multi‑year supply agreement that could see the first pilot projects deployed in 2027 at several U.S. utility sites.

The move aligns with GM’s broader “Zero Cradle‑to‑Cradle” sustainability goals, targeting a 30 % reduction in raw‑material risk across its battery portfolio. Sodium, being abundant and inexpensive, could also help mitigate geopolitical supply constraints that have plagued lithium and cobalt markets in recent years.

Industry analysts view the partnership as a hedge rather than a replacement for lithium‑ion, noting that sodium‑ion’s lower energy density limits its use to stationary storage where space is less of a premium. If the technology meets cost targets, it could accelerate the rollout of long‑duration storage needed to balance growing renewable generation.

The collaboration was first reported by Electrek on 23 October 2026 and confirmed in GM’s “Forward” event briefing. [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/23/gm-sodium-ion-battery-peak-energy/)

## 02 Tesla Says Battery Supply, Not Demand, Capped Q3 2026 Vehicle Deliveries
Tesla’s Q3 2026 shareholder letter, released on 21 October, placed the spotlight on battery availability as the primary brake on vehicle production. The automaker reported a 5 % year‑over‑year decline in deliveries, attributing the shortfall to “limited battery supply” despite a record‑high demand for its Model Y and Model 3.

Tesla disclosed that its 4680 cell ramp‑up, launched in 2024, is still 30 % below the planned output, with bottlenecks at both the cell‑manufacturing line in Texas and the raw‑material procurement stage. The company continues to source cylindrical 2170 cells from Panasonic, LG Energy, and CATL, but all three suppliers are grappling with capacity constraints as the industry chases the $120 kWh⁻¹ cost target.

To offset the shortfall, Tesla has temporarily increased production of models that use the older 2170 format, while accelerating the rollout of its “cell‑to‑pack” architecture that reduces pack weight by 15 % and eliminates redundant modules. The letter also noted a growing backlog—the largest in four years—indicating that demand remains robust.

Analysts warn that prolonged supply gaps could erode Tesla’s market share, especially as rivals like BYD and Volkswagen scale up their own high‑density packs. Tesla’s response will likely involve further vertical integration, including a planned expansion of its Nevada Gigafactory and a new partnership with a North‑American lithium‑hydroxide producer.

The story was covered by Electrek, which highlighted the mismatch between Tesla’s aggressive pricing strategy and its current battery constraints. [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/21/tesla-battery-supply-limiting-sales-prices-falling/)

## 03 CATL’s Qilin 60 kWh Pack Powers Nio Firefly, Delivering 560 km Range
Chinese battery giant CATL confirmed that its newly‑named “Qilin” 60 kWh battery pack will power Nio’s upcoming Firefly sedan, delivering a CLTC‑rated 560 km (≈350 mi) range. The announcement, posted on CnEVPost on 24 October, marks the first deployment of CATL’s high‑energy NCM 9‑cell chemistry in a mass‑market vehicle.

The Qilin pack utilizes a novel high‑nickel cathode (Ni 90 % / Co 5 % / Mn 5 %) paired with a silicon‑graphite anode, achieving an energy density of 210 Wh kg⁻¹ at the pack level—about 15 % higher than Nio’s previous 70 kWh LFP pack. The battery also incorporates a next‑generation electrolyte that improves low‑temperature performance, allowing the Firefly to maintain 80 % capacity at –20 °C.

Production will commence at CATL’s Jiangsu facility in Q3 2027, with an initial annual capacity of 150 GWh earmarked for Nio’s expanding lineup. The partnership aligns with Nio’s “Battery‑as‑a‑Service” model, as the Qilin pack will be eligible for subscription‑based swapping at Nio’s growing network of stations.

Industry observers see the Qilin launch as a signal that Chinese manufacturers are closing the gap with premium lithium‑ion technologies from Japan and South Korea. The higher energy density could enable longer‑range EVs without sacrificing interior space, a key competitive factor in the increasingly crowded compact‑sedan segment.

The story was first reported by CnEVPost, which quoted CATL’s chief technology officer on the chemistry’s scalability and cost‑target of $85 kWh⁻¹. [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/24/catl-qilin-battery-nio-firefly-60-kwh/)
