## 01 GM Partners with Peak Energy to Deploy Sodium‑Ion Cells for Grid‑Scale Storage
General Motors has taken a decisive step toward diversifying its energy‑storage portfolio by partnering with Peak Energy, a U.S. specialist in sodium‑ion technology. The collaboration, announced on 23 October, combines GM Ventures’ strategic investment with Peak’s proprietary Na‑ion cell platform to produce multi‑gigawatt‑hour (GWh) battery systems for grid‑scale applications.  

Peak’s cells use a sodium‑based cathode and a hard‑carbon anode, delivering an energy density of roughly 150 Wh kg⁻¹—lower than lithium‑ion but sufficient for stationary use where cost and raw‑material security are paramount. The partnership targets an initial production capacity of 1 GWh by 2028, with a roadmap to scale to 5 GWh by 2032. GM plans to integrate the modules into its upcoming “Electrify America” storage projects, beginning with a 200 MWh pilot at a solar‑plus‑storage hub in Arizona.  

Why it matters: Sodium is the world’s most abundant alkali metal, and moving away from lithium reduces exposure to supply‑chain bottlenecks and geopolitical risk. The GM‑Peak deal also signals that legacy automakers are willing to fund non‑automotive battery chemistries when the economics align with large‑scale storage needs. If the pilot proves successful, the venture could accelerate sodium‑ion adoption across the U.S. grid, potentially shaving $200‑$300 per kWh off the levelized cost of storage compared with current lithium‑ion solutions.  

The agreement also includes a joint R&D lab in Michigan focused on extending cycle life beyond 2,000 full‑depth cycles and improving low‑temperature performance—two historic challenges for sodium‑ion technology. GM’s involvement brings automotive‑grade manufacturing expertise, while Peak contributes its patented electrode formulations.  

Overall, the partnership underscores a broader industry trend: automakers are leveraging their capital and engineering capabilities to back alternative chemistries that can meet the exploding demand for stationary storage as renewable penetration climbs.

## 02 Xiaomi Announces Investment in Sunwoda EVB to Deepen Battery Ties
Chinese consumer‑electronics titan Xiaomi has moved further into the electric‑vehicle ecosystem by committing to a strategic investment in Sunwoda EVB, a leading lithium‑ion battery manufacturer. Reported by CnEVPost on 30 October, Xiaomi will inject roughly ¥1.4 billion (≈ US$200 million) into Sunwoda, acquiring a minority stake and securing a long‑term supply agreement for EV battery packs.  

The deal is designed to give Xiaomi a foothold in the fast‑growing EV battery market while providing Sunwoda with capital to expand its NCM (nickel‑cobalt‑manganese) production lines. Sunwoda plans to use the funds to build a new 10 GWh cell fab in Suzhou, slated to start mass production in early 2027. The partnership also includes joint development of high‑energy‑density 400 Wh kg⁻¹ cells aimed at Xiaomi’s upcoming “Mi EV” concept, which is expected to debut in 2028.  

From a supply‑chain perspective, the investment helps Xiaomi mitigate the risk of battery shortages that have plagued many Chinese EV startups. By securing a dedicated production slot, Xiaomi can align its vehicle rollout schedule with a reliable source of cells, reducing lead times from 12‑18 months to under six months.  

Industry analysts note that Xiaomi’s move reflects a broader diversification strategy among Chinese tech firms, many of which are leveraging their cash reserves to capture value in the battery value chain. Sunwoda, already a top‑10 global pack supplier, will benefit from Xiaomi’s brand power and distribution network, potentially opening new export channels in Southeast Asia and Europe.  

If the collaboration delivers the promised 400 Wh kg⁻¹ cells, Xiaomi’s future EVs could achieve ranges exceeding 600 km on a single charge, positioning the brand competitively against established players such as BYD and Nio. The partnership also underscores the accelerating convergence of consumer electronics and automotive battery technologies, where expertise in thermal management, fast charging, and miniaturization can be cross‑applied.

## 03 GAC’s Inpow Begins Battery Pack Production at First Overseas Plant in Thailand
GAC Group’s battery subsidiary, Inpow, has officially started production at its inaugural overseas pack‑assembly facility in Rayong Province, Thailand. The plant, unveiled on 30 October by CnEVPost, marks Inpow’s first foray outside mainland China and is expected to deliver up to 5 GWh of lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) modules per year.  

The 30,000 m² factory is equipped with a fully automated line supplied by a joint venture with a German equipment maker, enabling high‑throughput welding, cell‑balancing, and thermal‑management integration. Inpow’s initial output will focus on 30‑kWh and 50‑kWh packs tailored for compact city EVs and light‑commercial vehicles destined for the Southeast Asian market.  

Strategically, the Thailand location gives GAC a logistical advantage for serving ASEAN manufacturers, reducing shipping costs and tariffs that have historically hampered Chinese‑made packs in the region. The plant also benefits from Thailand’s “Eastern Economic Corridor” incentives, which include tax breaks for high‑tech manufacturing and a skilled labor pool experienced in automotive assembly.  

From a market standpoint, the move reflects the growing demand for locally produced battery packs as regional EV adoption accelerates. Thailand’s EV sales are projected to exceed 400,000 units in 2027, and local OEMs such as EV Power and FOMM have already placed preliminary orders for Inpow’s LFP packs. The plant’s capacity is designed to be scalable; Inpow has filed plans to add a second line by 2029, potentially pushing annual output to 10 GWh.  

The launch also underscores a shift in the global battery supply chain toward regionalization. By establishing production closer to end‑users, Inpow can better manage inventory, respond to local regulatory standards (e.g., ASEAN’s upcoming safety certification), and reduce the carbon footprint associated with long‑haul freight.  

Overall, Inpow’s Thailand facility strengthens GAC’s vertical integration, giving the group greater control over pack quality, cost, and delivery timelines—key competitive levers as the ASEAN EV market matures.
