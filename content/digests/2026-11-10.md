## 01 CATL Expands Battery‑Swap Network to Accelerate Sodium‑Ion Adoption
China’s dominant lithium‑ion cell producer CATL announced on November 10 that it will broaden its battery‑swap infrastructure to include sodium‑ion (Na‑ion) packs, a move aimed at fast‑tracking the commercial rollout of the technology. The company plans to retrofit 1,200 existing swap stations across major Chinese megacities with Na‑ion compatible hardware by the end of 2027, adding roughly 300 kW of charging capacity per site. CATL expects the Na‑ion packs to deliver 150‑200 Wh/kg energy density, a modest drop from its flagship lithium‑ion cells but with a cost advantage of up to 30 % thanks to abundant sodium resources and a simpler electrolyte formulation.  

The strategic push follows a series of pilot projects launched in 2025 with ride‑hailing fleets in Shanghai and Shenzhen, where Na‑ion packs demonstrated a 20 % lower degradation rate over 1,000 full cycles compared with conventional LFP cells. By integrating swapping with Na‑ion chemistry, CATL hopes to address two lingering concerns: range anxiety and the high upfront cost of large‑capacity batteries for commercial vehicles.  

Industry analysts view the expansion as a litmus test for Na‑ion viability beyond niche applications. If the swap network can deliver a seamless user experience comparable to lithium‑ion swaps, it could unlock a new market segment for low‑cost, long‑life energy storage, especially in logistics and public transport where vehicle downtime directly impacts revenue. However, challenges remain, including the need for standardized pack dimensions and the development of robust thermal‑management systems for the higher operating temperatures typical of Na‑ion chemistries.  

CATL’s announcement also signals a broader diversification strategy as the company seeks to hedge against potential lithium supply constraints and price volatility. By leveraging its existing swap ecosystem, CATL can accelerate Na‑ion adoption without building a parallel network from scratch, potentially shortening the technology’s path to mass market. The move is expected to influence other OEMs and battery‑swap operators worldwide, prompting them to evaluate mixed‑chemistry solutions for future fleets.

## 02 Mustang Mach‑E Real‑World Battery Data Reveals 5‑Year Durability Trends
A five‑year longitudinal study of Ford’s 2021 Mustang Mach‑E fleet, released by CleanTechnica on November 9, provides the most comprehensive real‑world insight yet into the durability of first‑generation EV battery packs. The analysis covers 12,400 vehicles across North America, tracking capacity loss, range degradation, and charging‑behavior patterns under everyday use. On average, the packs retained 87 % of their initial 75.7 kWh usable capacity after 60 000 miles (≈96 000 km), translating to an annual degradation rate of roughly 2.6 %.  

Key findings include a pronounced “early‑life” drop of 5 % within the first 12 months, after which the degradation curve flattened. Vehicles that predominantly used DC fast charging (≥150 kW) exhibited a slightly higher cumulative loss—about 1.2 % more than those relying on Level 2 AC charging. Temperature also played a role: packs operating in climates with average ambient temperatures above 30 °C degraded 0.8 % faster, underscoring the importance of thermal‑management software updates.  

Ford’s over‑the‑air (OTA) battery‑management updates, rolled out in 2023 and 2024, appear to have mitigated some of the fast‑charging stress, reducing the rate of capacity fade by an estimated 0.4 % per year. The study also highlighted that the majority of owners (68 %) still achieved more than 250 km of usable range per charge after five years, well above the 200 km threshold often cited for end‑of‑life decisions.  

For the broader industry, the data validates the durability expectations set by early lithium‑ion chemistry models and offers a benchmark for upcoming second‑generation packs that promise higher energy densities. It also reinforces the value of OTA software as a tool for extending battery life, a practice that could become standard across OEMs seeking to protect warranty costs and improve resale values.

## 03 Guoxuan High‑Tech Unveils Full‑Chain Plan to Meet Projected 5 TWh Battery Demand by 2030
Guoxuan High‑Tech, a leading Chinese cathode manufacturer, published a detailed roadmap on November 10 outlining how it intends to satisfy an anticipated global demand of 5 TWh of battery capacity by 2030. The plan, reported by 第一电动, integrates upstream raw‑material securing, mid‑stream cell production scaling, and downstream recycling initiatives into a single “full‑chain” ecosystem.  

Upstream, Guoxuan has signed long‑term supply contracts for lithium carbonate and nickel sulfate with miners in Australia, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Chile, locking in roughly 1.2 Mt of lithium and 0.9 Mt of nickel annually. The company also announced a joint venture with a Chinese rare‑earth producer to develop a low‑cobalt cathode chemistry (NMC 811‑low‑Co) that aims to cut cobalt usage by 40 % while maintaining a target energy density of 250 Wh/kg.  

On the production side, Guoxuan will commission two new cathode lines in its Yixing facility, each capable of 30 GWh per year, bringing total annual cathode output to 120 GWh by 2028. The lines will employ a patented aqueous‑coating process that reduces solvent emissions by 70 % and cuts production costs by an estimated 15 %.  

Downstream, the firm is launching a “closed‑loop” recycling program in partnership with three major Chinese EV manufacturers. The program will collect end‑of‑life packs, recover up to 95 % of cathode material, and feed it back into the new production lines, thereby reducing reliance on virgin raw materials. Guoxuan projects that by 2030, recycled content will account for 30 % of its cathode feedstock.  

Analysts view the integrated approach as a strategic hedge against raw‑material price volatility and tightening environmental regulations. By controlling the entire value chain, Guoxuan aims to offer OEMs a more predictable cost structure and faster time‑to‑market for next‑generation high‑energy‑density cells. The roadmap also signals China’s broader ambition to dominate the global battery supply chain, especially as Western manufacturers grapple with supply‑chain disruptions and ESG pressures.
