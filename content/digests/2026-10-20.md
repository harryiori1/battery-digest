## 01 GM Announces Prismatic LMR Cells to Boost Energy Density by 33% While Cutting Costs

General Motors disclosed that its upcoming “LMR” (Lithium‑Metal‑Rich) prismatic cells will be the first to reach mass‑production scale in the United States. The company claims the new chemistry delivers a 33 % increase in gravimetric energy density over its current LFP (lithium‑iron‑phosphate) lineup, while keeping unit costs comparable to LFP and well below the $120/kWh benchmark that has become the industry’s cost‑per‑kilowatt‑hour target.

The LMR cells use a high‑nickel cathode (approximately 85 % Ni) paired with a silicon‑enhanced graphite anode, a combination that pushes the cell voltage to 3.9 V and enables a specific energy of roughly 260 Wh/kg. GM plans to manufacture the cells at its new Ohio “Battery Hub” facility, slated to start volume production in Q2 2027. The plant will employ a dry‑coating process that reduces solvent use and shortens drying time, further lowering CAPEX.

Why it matters: If GM’s cost and performance targets hold, the LMR cells could close the gap between affordable LFP and high‑energy NMC chemistries, giving automakers a cheaper path to 400‑plus‑kilometer ranges without the cobalt supply risks that have plagued the industry. The announcement also signals a strategic shift away from the industry‑wide focus on cylindrical cells, which dominate in China, toward prismatic formats that better suit large‑format packs used in SUVs and trucks.

The move aligns with GM’s broader “Ultium Next” roadmap, which envisions a modular battery architecture that can be scaled from 30 kWh city‑car packs to 150 kWh long‑range packs using the same cell family. Analysts estimate the LMR platform could shave $5,000–$7,000 off the price of a 75 kWh pack, accelerating the company’s goal of a sub‑$30 kWh battery cost by 2030.

The announcement was made at GM’s “Battery Tech Day” in Detroit and was covered by Electrek, which quoted internal GM documents and statements from the company’s battery‑technology chief, Dr. Anjali Patel. The rollout will be closely watched by rivals such as Tesla, BYD, and CATL, all of whom are racing to commercialize next‑generation chemistries.

## 02 Mercedes‑Benz Secures Exclusive Rights to ProLogium’s Gen4 Solid‑State Cells

Mercedes‑Benz announced a strategic partnership with Taiwanese solid‑state pioneer ProLogium, granting the German automaker exclusive first‑right access to the company’s fourth‑generation (Gen4) solid‑state battery cells. The Gen4 cells are based on a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte and a lithium‑metal anode, promising an energy density of up to 500 Wh/kg—roughly double that of today’s best lithium‑ion packs.

ProLogium’s Gen4 architecture integrates a thin‑film solid electrolyte that eliminates the need for a liquid separator, improving safety by removing flammable electrolytes. The cells also feature a “self‑healing” interlayer that mitigates dendrite formation, a major hurdle for lithium‑metal designs. According to ProLogium, the cells can be charged to 80 % in under 10 minutes while maintaining a cycle life of more than 1,000 full‑depth cycles.

Mercedes plans to debut the technology in its flagship EQS‑S line, targeting a 2028 launch for a limited‑run “EQS‑S Solid‑State” model. The automaker expects the solid‑state pack to enable a 600 km WLTP range with a 20 % weight reduction compared with its current NMC‑based packs. The partnership also includes joint R&D at ProLogium’s Hsinchu campus, where Mercedes will contribute its vehicle integration expertise.

The deal is significant because it marks the first time a major OEM has secured exclusive rights to a solid‑state cell that is already in pilot‑scale production. While many startups claim solid‑state breakthroughs, few have demonstrated a manufacturable cell that meets automotive safety standards. Mercedes’ move could accelerate the industry’s transition from lithium‑ion to solid‑state, especially for premium vehicles where cost is less of a barrier.

Industry observers note that the partnership may pressure rivals—BMW, Audi, and Hyundai—to accelerate their own solid‑state roadmaps. ProLogium expects to ramp up to 10 GWh of annual capacity by 2030, enough to supply multiple OEMs if additional agreements are signed.

## 03 Chicago Metra Unveils First Battery‑Electric Locomotive for Commuter Service

The Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning (Metra) announced the arrival of its first battery‑electric locomotive, a Stadler FLIRT‑BEV (Battery‑Electric Variant) slated to enter service on the Milwaukee District/West line in early 2027. The locomotive is powered by a 2 MWh lithium‑ion battery pack built by a joint venture between Northvolt and a local U.S. supplier, delivering a continuous output of 4 MW and a peak of 6 MW.

The FLIRT‑BEV can travel up to 120 km on a single charge, covering the 30‑km commuter segment between Union Station and the western suburbs without any diesel assistance. Charging is performed via a fast‑charge depot at Union Station, where the battery can be replenished from 20 % to 80 % in under 30 minutes using a 1 MW DC charger. The locomotive also features regenerative braking that recovers up to 30 % of kinetic energy, extending range and reducing overall energy consumption.

Metra’s decision follows a pilot study that demonstrated a 40 % reduction in local emissions and a 25 % drop in operating costs compared with its aging diesel fleet. The agency expects to replace up to 15 diesel units with battery‑electric equivalents over the next decade, aligning with Chicago’s broader climate‑action plan to cut transportation emissions by 50 % by 2035.

The rollout is notable for being the first U.S. commuter rail operator to adopt a fully battery‑powered locomotive for regular passenger service, rather than a hybrid or diesel‑electric model. It showcases the scalability of large‑format battery packs for rail applications, a sector traditionally dominated by overhead catenary or diesel power. Analysts predict that successful deployment could spur similar projects in other dense‑urban corridors across the United States, especially where electrification infrastructure is cost‑prohibitive.

The announcement was covered by Electrek, which highlighted the partnership between Metra, Stadler, and Northvolt, and quoted Metra’s chief operating officer, Lisa Hernandez, emphasizing the project’s role in “modernizing our fleet while delivering cleaner, quieter rides for Chicago commuters.”
