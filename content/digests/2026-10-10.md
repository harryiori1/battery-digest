## 01 BYD Rolls Out 800 hp Q3 Electric Semi‑Truck, 150,000th Commercial NEV Milestone

BYD announced that its Q3 electric semi‑truck, rated at 800 hp (≈ 600 kW) and equipped with a 600 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack, became the 150,000th commercial new‑energy vehicle (NEV) to roll off the company’s Huai’an truck plant. The Q3 is the latest iteration of BYD’s heavy‑duty line, featuring a modular battery architecture that can be swapped in under 15 minutes at BYD’s expanding network of “Battery‑as‑a‑Service” stations.

The 600 kWh pack delivers a claimed range of up to 800 km (500 mi) under typical freight loads, while supporting a peak charging rate of 1.2 MW using BYD’s proprietary 800 V fast‑charge system. This enables a 0‑80 % charge in roughly 30 minutes, a figure that rivals diesel refuelling times and could reshape long‑haul logistics. The truck also incorporates BYD’s Blade battery safety design, which has passed over‑charge, puncture and nail‑penetration tests without thermal runaway.

Why it matters: The milestone underscores BYD’s rapid scaling in the commercial‑vehicle segment, a market that has traditionally lagged behind passenger EV adoption. By delivering a high‑power, high‑energy pack that can be quickly swapped, BYD addresses two of the biggest pain points for fleet operators—downtime and battery degradation. The 150,000‑unit figure also signals that Chinese manufacturers are now the dominant source of electric trucks globally, a shift that could pressure Western OEMs to accelerate their own heavy‑duty electrification programs.

The Q3’s battery chemistry (LFP) offers lower cost and better thermal stability than nickel‑cobalt‑manganese (NCM) chemistries, albeit at a modest energy‑density penalty (≈ 150 Wh/kg). BYD’s economies of scale have driven the pack cost down to an estimated $120 kWh⁻¹, making the total battery cost roughly $72 million per truck—a figure that could be offset by lower operating expenses and government subsidies in key markets.

Overall, the Q3 semi‑truck demonstrates that large‑format LFP packs can meet the demanding power and range requirements of freight transport, potentially setting a new industry benchmark for cost‑effective, safe, and fast‑charging heavy‑duty EVs. ## 02 Geely Galaxy Unveils Cruiser 700 Off‑Road EV SUV with 90 kWh Battery

Chinese automaker Geely’s premium sub‑brand Galaxy launched the Cruiser 700, an off‑road‑oriented electric SUV aimed at consumers who need rugged capability without sacrificing zero‑emission performance. The vehicle is built on Geely’s Sustainable Architecture (GSA) platform and houses a 90 kWh LFP battery pack that delivers a WLTP‑rated range of 400 km (≈ 250 mi) on mixed terrain.

The Cruiser 700’s battery pack is integrated into a reinforced under‑floor chassis, providing a low centre of gravity that enhances stability on uneven surfaces. Fast‑charging capability is a key selling point: the pack can accept up to 800 kW, allowing an 80 % charge in just 30 minutes at compatible DC stations. Geely also offers an optional battery‑swap module for fleet customers operating in remote regions where charging infrastructure is sparse.

Power output is 300 kW (≈ 400 hp) with a peak torque of 650 Nm, enabling the SUV to tow up to 2.5 t and climb gradients of 30 %. The vehicle’s thermal‑management system uses a two‑stage liquid‑cooling loop that keeps cell temperatures within the optimal 20‑30 °C window, extending cycle life to an estimated 2,000 full‑depth cycles (≈ 8 years under typical use).

Why it matters: The Cruiser 700 is one of the first mass‑market EVs explicitly engineered for off‑road applications, a segment traditionally dominated by internal‑combustion‑engine (ICE) models. By pairing a high‑capacity LFP pack with robust fast‑charging and optional swapping, Geely addresses the range‑anxiety and infrastructure challenges that have limited EV penetration in rural and adventure‑oriented markets.

The 90 kWh pack also showcases the continued maturation of LFP technology for higher‑energy applications. While NCM chemistries still dominate long‑range passenger EVs, LFP’s safety, cost, and recyclability advantages are becoming compelling for SUVs and trucks where weight penalties are less critical. Geely’s move may encourage other OEMs to explore LFP for larger vehicles, potentially reshaping supply‑chain dynamics for cathode materials.

Overall, the Cruiser 700 positions Geely as a pioneer in the emerging “off‑road EV” niche, and its battery architecture could set a template for future rugged electric vehicles. [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/10/geely-galaxy-launches-cruiser-700-suv/)
