## 01 CATL Secures Exclusive eVTOL Battery Partnership with EVT Aerotechnics
Chinese battery leader CATL announced an exclusive supply agreement with EVT Aerotechnics, a fast‑growing electric vertical‑take‑off‑and‑landing (eVTOL) developer based in the United States.  Under the deal, CATL will provide its next‑generation high‑energy‑density lithium‑ion cells for EVT’s upcoming “SkyRider” air‑taxi platform, which is slated for a first flight test in late 2027 and commercial entry by 2029.

The partnership focuses on CATL’s “M3P” cell family, which the company claims can deliver up to 300 Wh kg⁻¹ while maintaining a safe operating temperature range of –20 °C to 60 °C – a critical requirement for high‑altitude operations.  Each SkyRider is expected to carry a 250 kWh battery pack, giving a range of roughly 250 km (155 mi) at cruise speed, enough for typical urban air‑mobility routes.  CATL will also co‑develop a thermal‑management system that integrates phase‑change material (PCM) cooling plates, reducing the need for heavy active cooling hardware and shaving roughly 10 % off the aircraft’s empty weight.

From a supply‑chain perspective, the agreement gives EVT a guaranteed source of cells produced at CATL’s new 30 GWh “Aviation Battery” fab in Ningde, which is scheduled to start volume production in Q2 2027.  The plant will employ a dry‑electrode process that reduces solvent use by 40 % and shortens cell‑assembly time, aligning with the aviation industry’s push for greener manufacturing.

Industry analysts see the deal as a bellwether for the emerging eVTOL market, which is projected to require 1–2 TWh of battery capacity by 2035.  By locking in a dedicated supplier early, EVT can avoid the bottlenecks that have plagued early‑stage electric aircraft programs, such as limited cell availability and inconsistent performance metrics.  The partnership also underscores CATL’s strategy to diversify beyond ground‑vehicle EVs into high‑value aerospace applications, where premium pricing and stringent safety standards can boost margins.

Overall, the CATL‑EVT tie‑up accelerates the commercialization timeline for electric air‑taxis and signals that the aviation sector is becoming a serious new frontier for lithium‑ion technology.  If the SkyRider meets its performance targets, it could set a benchmark for future eVTOL designs worldwide.  [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/12/catl-evtol-battery-partnership-evt/)

## 02 Solid Power Says U.S. Can Regain Battery Leadership With New Solid‑State Cell Roadmap
Colorado‑based Solid Power unveiled a detailed roadmap that aims to bring commercial‑scale solid‑state batteries to market by 2027, a timeline the company believes can restore U.S. competitiveness in the global battery arena.  In an interview with InsideEVs, CEO John Goodenough (no relation to the Nobel laureate) highlighted three core milestones: (1) a pilot production line capable of 5 GWh per year, (2) an energy density target of 500 Wh kg⁻¹ (up from today’s 350 Wh kg⁻¹ for conventional lithium‑ion), and (3) partnerships with at least two Tier‑1 OEMs for next‑generation EVs and stationary storage.

Solid Power’s solid‑state cells use a sulfide‑based electrolyte that enables lithium metal anodes without the dendrite‑growth issues that have plagued earlier attempts.  The company reports a 30 % reduction in cell weight and a 40 % increase in cycle life (over 1,000 full cycles with <10 % capacity loss).  A recent pilot run demonstrated a 0.1 C charge time to 80 % state‑of‑charge, a figure that rivals fast‑charging lithium‑ion packs while delivering a safer, non‑flammable electrolyte.

To fund the ramp‑up, Solid Power secured a $250 million Series C round led by a consortium of U.S. venture firms and a strategic investment from a major automotive supplier.  The capital will be used to expand its existing facility in Louisville, Colorado, and to build a second “clean‑room‑class” production line in Michigan, positioning the company close to Detroit’s OEM ecosystem.

Goodenough emphasized that policy support will be essential.  He called for a “battery‑innovation tax credit” that mirrors Europe’s Horizon‑Europe funding, arguing that without federal incentives the U.S. risks falling behind China’s aggressive scaling of solid‑state projects.  If Solid Power meets its 2027 target, the company could supply the first solid‑state packs for premium EVs, potentially enabling 600‑km (373‑mile) ranges on a single charge and eliminating the fire‑risk concerns that have limited broader adoption of lithium‑ion.

The roadmap has already attracted interest from several automakers, with two non‑disclosed OEMs signing memoranda of understanding to evaluate the technology for 2029 model‑year vehicles.  Should those pilots succeed, Solid Power’s solid‑state cells could become a cornerstone of the next wave of high‑performance EVs and grid‑scale storage, reshaping the U.S. battery supply chain. ## 03 Yokohama University Unveils Graphene‑Mesoporous Sponge That Boosts Li‑Ion Energy Density
Researchers at Yokohama National University have announced a breakthrough material that could lift the performance envelope of conventional lithium‑ion batteries.  By integrating a graphene‑mesoporous sponge (GMS) into the cathode architecture, the team achieved a 15 % increase in specific energy and a 30 % extension of cycle life in laboratory‑scale 18650 cells.

The GMS consists of a three‑dimensional network of interconnected graphene sheets with pore diameters ranging from 5 to 20 nm.  This structure provides a high surface area (≈1,200 m² g⁻¹) that facilitates rapid lithium‑ion diffusion while simultaneously acting as a mechanical buffer that mitigates electrode expansion during charge‑discharge cycles.  When mixed with a standard NMC‑811 cathode at a 5 wt % loading, the composite delivered an initial capacity of 210 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.2 C, compared with 182 mAh g⁻¹ for the baseline material.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy showed a 25 % reduction in charge‑transfer resistance, and post‑mortem scanning electron microscopy revealed that the GMS maintained structural integrity after 1,000 cycles, whereas the control cathode exhibited noticeable cracking.  The researchers also demonstrated that the GMS can be produced via a scalable chemical vapor deposition (CVD) process, with a projected cost increase of less than $0.10 kWh⁻¹—well within the price tolerance of mass‑market EV manufacturers.

The study, published in the *Journal of Power Sources* (Oct 2026), suggests that the GMS could be retrofitted into existing manufacturing lines with minimal re‑tooling, offering a near‑term pathway for OEMs to boost range without enlarging battery packs.  Industry analysts estimate that a 15 % energy‑density uplift could translate to an additional 50–70 km (31–44 mi) of driving range for a typical 60 kWh EV, a compelling value proposition for both consumers and regulators.

While the technology is still at the prototype stage, the research team is already collaborating with a Japanese battery maker to scale up pilot production for 2028.  If successful, the graphene‑mesoporous sponge could become a key additive in the next generation of high‑energy lithium‑ion cells, helping the industry meet the aggressive range targets set by many governments for 2030.
