## 01 CATL Advances to 20 Ah Solid‑State Cells Targeting 500 Wh/kg
Chinese battery leader CATL announced a major milestone in its solid‑state program: a 20 Ah pouch‑type solid‑state cell that delivers a gravimetric energy density of 500 Wh kg⁻¹ in laboratory testing. The prototype uses a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte and a high‑nickel NMC cathode, while the anode is a lithium‑metal foil protected by a thin interfacial layer to suppress dendrite growth. CATL says the cell can be charged to 80 % state‑of‑charge in under ten minutes at 1 C, and it retains 95 % capacity after 1,000 cycles at 25 °C.

The company plans to start limited pilot production in late 2027, targeting premium EV models and high‑performance electric aircraft. CATL has already signed non‑binding memoranda of understanding with several OEMs, including a German luxury brand that intends to launch a limited‑run sports sedan in 2029 equipped with the new solid‑state pack.

Why it matters: Reaching 500 Wh kg⁻¹ would close the gap between current lithium‑ion packs (≈250 Wh kg⁻¹) and the theoretical limits of solid‑state chemistry, potentially halving vehicle weight and extending range beyond 800 km for midsize cars. The 20 Ah capacity also aligns with the industry’s shift toward larger single‑cell formats that simplify pack architecture and reduce thermal‑management complexity. If CATL can scale the technology, it could reshape the competitive landscape, pressuring rivals such as Samsung SDI and QuantumScape to accelerate their own roadmaps.

The announcement follows a series of solid‑state breakthroughs in 2025‑2026, including Toyota’s 10 Ah prototype and Samsung’s 15 Ah cell, but CATL’s energy‑density claim is the highest publicly disclosed to date. Analysts at BloombergNEF estimate that solid‑state batteries could capture up to 30 % of the global EV battery market by 2035 if cost targets of $80 kWh⁻¹ are met. CATL’s aggressive timeline suggests it aims to be a first‑mover in that future market.

## 02 CATL Unveils 9‑MWh ‘Tener+’ Modular Storage System in Australia
CATL revealed its newest grid‑scale offering, the Tener+ modular storage system, at a launch event in Adelaide. The 9‑MWh solution consists of 12‑module containers, each housing 750 kWh of lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) cells with a 4 hour discharge rating. The system is designed for rapid deployment: a single crew can install a full 9‑MWh plant in under 48 hours, and the containers are stackable for up to 27 MWh in a single site.

The first deployment will be at the Hornsdale Power Reserve in South Australia, where the Tener+ will complement the existing Tesla‑Mitsubishi battery farm. CATL claims the new system can deliver a round‑trip efficiency of 94 % and a calendar life of 20 years, thanks to an advanced cell‑balancing algorithm and a proprietary thermal‑management coating that reduces temperature gradients across the pack.

Strategically, the launch deepens CATL’s presence in the Australian market, where renewable penetration has risen to 68 % of total generation. By offering a modular, high‑efficiency storage solution, CATL aims to capture a larger share of the projected AU$12 billion Australian energy‑storage market through 2035. The company also announced a partnership with local integrator Powerlink to provide operation‑and‑maintenance services, ensuring the system meets the stringent reliability standards required for frequency regulation and peak‑shaving applications.

Industry observers note that the Tener+ architecture mirrors CATL’s recent push to standardize LFP modules for both automotive and stationary use, leveraging economies of scale to drive down the $120 kWh⁻¹ price point that has become a benchmark for utility‑scale storage. If the Hornsdale pilot meets performance expectations, CATL could secure additional contracts with the Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) and expand into neighboring New Zealand, where similar renewable‑integration challenges exist.

## 03 CATL Signs 3 GWh Sodium‑Ion Storage Deal with Germany’s Intilion
In a move that underscores the growing commercial interest in sodium‑ion technology, CATL signed a five‑year agreement with German energy‑storage specialist Intilion to supply up to 3 GWh of sodium‑ion battery modules. The deal, announced on 28 October, will see CATL deliver standardized 1 MWh “Power‑Blocks” that can be stacked to create megawatt‑scale installations for grid‑balancing, renewable‑energy firming, and off‑grid micro‑grids across Europe.

Intilion’s sodium‑ion chemistry, based on a Prussian‑blue cathode and a hard‑carbon anode, offers a nominal energy density of 150 Wh kg⁻¹—lower than LFP but with a cost advantage of roughly 30 % due to the absence of cobalt and nickel. The cells operate safely at temperatures up to 60 °C and are tolerant of deep‑discharge cycles, making them well‑suited for long‑duration storage (LDS) applications that require 6‑12 hour discharge windows.

The partnership will initially focus on two pilot projects: a 20 MWh installation at a wind farm in Brandenburg and a 15 MWh system supporting a district‑heating network in Munich. Both sites are slated for commissioning in 2028. CATL will handle cell production at its Jiangxi facility, where it has recently re‑tooled a line to accommodate the larger, rectangular sodium‑ion cells, while Intilion will integrate the modules into its proprietary Battery‑Management System (BMS) and provide after‑sales service.

Analysts at Wood Mackenzie view the agreement as a validation of sodium‑ion’s commercial viability, especially in markets where raw‑material supply constraints and ESG concerns limit the use of cobalt‑rich chemistries. The 3 GWh volume represents roughly 0.5 % of Europe’s projected 600 GWh stationary‑storage demand by 2030, but it could serve as a catalyst for broader adoption if performance targets—particularly cycle life of 2,500 cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge—are met.
