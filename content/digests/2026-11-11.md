## 01 QuantumScape Unveils Osprey All‑Solid‑State Cell with 1,000‑Cycle Life at 1 atm

QuantumScape announced on 11 November that its new Osprey platform has achieved a milestone for sulfide‑based solid‑state batteries: 1,000 full charge‑discharge cycles while retaining 80 % of its initial capacity, all under a modest one‑atmosphere pressure. The Osprey cell replaces the liquid electrolyte in the cathode with a sulfide solid electrolyte, a chemistry that has traditionally required heavy clamping or high‑pressure housings to maintain ionic contact. By engineering a composite cathode architecture that self‑stabilises the interface, QuantumScape claims the cell can be assembled in a conventional pouch format, dramatically reducing packaging complexity and cost.

Key specifications disclosed include a nominal energy density of 350 Wh kg⁻¹ and a power rating of 1.5 kW kg⁻¹, positioning the Osprey between current lithium‑ion and next‑generation solid‑state offerings. The company also reported a fast‑charge capability of 80 % state‑of‑charge in under 15 minutes at 1 C, thanks to the high ionic conductivity (≈10⁻³ S cm⁻¹) of the sulfide electrolyte. QuantumScape plans to scale the technology to 50 Ah pouch cells by 2028, targeting electric‑vehicle (EV) applications that demand both high energy and long cycle life, such as premium sedans and light‑commercial vehicles.

Industry analysts view the 1 atm breakthrough as a potential game‑changer because it eliminates the need for expensive pressure‑vessel modules that have hampered earlier solid‑state prototypes. If the performance translates to larger formats, OEMs could adopt Osprey cells for models that require 400‑plus km range with a 10‑year warranty, reducing reliance on conventional lithium‑ion chemistries. However, skeptics point out that the disclosed cycle test was performed at a relatively low C‑rate and that long‑term calendar ageing, thermal stability, and manufacturability at scale remain unproven.

QuantumScape’s announcement follows a wave of solid‑state activity from rivals such as Toyota and Samsung SDI, but its emphasis on low‑pressure operation differentiates it from the high‑pressure sulfide cells that dominate current research. The company has secured a $300 million strategic investment from a consortium of automotive partners, indicating strong commercial interest. The next milestone will be a pilot production line in Arizona slated for early 2027, where the Osprey cells will be integrated into a prototype EV platform for real‑world validation.

## 02 Four Chinese Cities Partner with CATL to Replace Aging Bus Batteries

On 11 November, a joint statement from the municipal governments of Chengdu, Wuhan, Xi’an and Shenyang announced a coordinated partnership with CATL to replace the aging lithium‑ion battery packs in their public‑transit bus fleets. The initiative targets roughly 12 000 electric buses that have entered the “mid‑life” replacement window, defined as 4–6 years of service or 600 000 km of cumulative mileage. CATL will supply new lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) modules with a nominal capacity of 300 kWh per bus, delivering an estimated 1.2 GWh of fresh storage across the four cities.

The replacement packs incorporate CATL’s latest “M3P” LFP chemistry, which promises a 20 % increase in energy density (up to 160 Wh kg⁻¹) and a 30 % reduction in weight compared with the legacy packs installed in 2018‑2020. In addition, the modules feature an upgraded battery‑management system (BMS) that enables real‑time health monitoring, predictive maintenance alerts, and a “fast‑swap” capability that can replace a depleted pack in under five minutes at designated depots.

Financially, the project is backed by a combination of municipal budgets and a 15 % subsidy from the Chinese Ministry of Transport, which aims to accelerate the turnover of sub‑optimal battery assets and improve overall fleet efficiency. CATL estimates that the new packs will extend the average bus range by 25 % (from 250 km to roughly 315 km) and cut operating costs by 12 % through lower degradation rates and reduced cooling power consumption.

Industry observers see the coordinated rollout as a template for other megacities grappling with the “second‑life” challenge of early‑stage battery degradation. By synchronising procurement, the cities achieve economies of scale that lower per‑kilowatt‑hour costs to roughly ¥1 200 (≈ US$170), a figure competitive with new bus purchases. Moreover, the fast‑swap infrastructure aligns with China’s broader “battery‑as‑a‑service” (BaaS) strategy, allowing operators to decouple vehicle ownership from battery ownership and improve asset utilisation.

The partnership also includes a recycling clause: once the new packs reach end‑of‑life (estimated 2035‑2040), CATL will reclaim up to 95 % of the LFP material for reuse in future packs, supporting China’s circular‑economy goals for battery materials. The first batch of replacement packs is scheduled for delivery in Q1 2027, with full fleet conversion expected by the end of 2028.
