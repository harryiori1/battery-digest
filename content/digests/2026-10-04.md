## 01 BYD Expands Seagull EV Battery to 45 kWh, Adds Premium Features
China’s BYD is giving its ultra‑affordable Seagull electric car a substantive power‑train upgrade ahead of the 2026 model year. The updated version, unveiled in late September, swaps the original 30 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) pack for a 45 kWh unit, a 50 % increase that lifts the claimed WLTP range from roughly 300 km to about 460 km. The larger pack also supports a new 30 kW DC fast‑charging capability, cutting the 0‑80 % charge time to under 30 minutes—significantly quicker than the previous 50 kW‑limited system.

Pricing, however, will climb. While the base Seagull launched at an eye‑catching ¥70,000 (≈ $10,000), the upgraded model is expected to start near ¥84,000 ($12,000), reflecting the higher‑capacity battery and added interior trim such as a digital instrument cluster and upgraded infotainment. BYD says the new pack uses its latest “Blade” LFP cell architecture, which delivers a higher volumetric energy density (≈ 210 Wh/L) while retaining the safety and cost advantages of LFP chemistry.

The move signals BYD’s strategy to push low‑cost EVs up the value chain without abandoning the price‑sensitive market segment. By leveraging economies of scale in its massive cell factories, BYD can afford a larger pack while keeping the vehicle’s price competitive against rivals like the Wuling Hongguang Mini EV. Industry analysts note that the upgrade could also help BYD meet stricter Chinese fuel‑economy standards slated for 2027, which effectively mandate higher average vehicle ranges.

Overall, the Seagull’s battery boost illustrates how even entry‑level EVs are benefitting from rapid advances in cell energy density and cost reductions, narrowing the gap between budget and premium models. The upgrade may also pressure other low‑cost manufacturers to reconsider their battery sizing, potentially accelerating the overall market shift toward larger, more capable packs in the sub‑$15,000 segment. ## 02 BYD Announces 1,000 km Range Han EV with 120 kWh Pack
BYD has set a launch date for the next‑generation Han sedan, positioning it as the first production EV to regularly exceed 1,000 km (621 mi) on a single charge. The flagship will debut in early November and will be equipped with a 120 kWh ternary‑NCM (nickel‑cobalt‑manganese) battery pack, built in BYD’s “Cell‑to‑Pack” (CTP) format that eliminates traditional module housing to boost volumetric efficiency to roughly 250 Wh/L.

The Han’s advertised WLTP range of 1,050 km translates to an energy consumption of about 115 Wh/km, comparable to premium European models that rely on high‑energy‑density chemistries. BYD claims the pack can charge from 10 % to 80 % in 25 minutes using a 350 kW DC fast‑charger, thanks to a new high‑current electrolyte formulation that mitigates lithium plating at high charge rates. The vehicle will also feature an integrated thermal‑management system that keeps cell temperature within a 15‑°C window, preserving cycle life and limiting degradation to less than 5 % after 1,000 cycles.

Pricing is expected to start around ¥350,000 ($48,000), placing the Han in direct competition with Tesla’s Model S and the upcoming Mercedes‑EQE. BYD’s decision to use a high‑nickel NCM chemistry, rather than its traditional LFP platform, underscores a strategic shift toward premium‑segment batteries where energy density outweighs cost considerations.

The announcement is significant for the global battery supply chain. BYD has pledged to source 70 % of the cathode material from its newly announced joint venture with a South Korean mining consortium, aiming to secure nickel and cobalt supplies ahead of anticipated shortages. If the Han meets its range and cost targets, it could accelerate the adoption of high‑energy‑density packs in the mass market, pressuring competitors to upscale their own cell technologies. ## 03 CleanTechnica Analyzes Europe’s IAA‑Driven Battery Value‑Chain Expansion
A new analysis published by CleanTechnica on October 1 examines how the European Union’s Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA) is expected to catalyze a massive build‑out of the continent’s battery ecosystem. The paper models the impact of the IAA’s €30 billion incentive package, which targets cell, cathode, anode, and electrolyte production, as well as recycling facilities.

According to the study, the incentives could unlock more than 100 GWh of new cell capacity by 2030, effectively doubling Europe’s current installed capacity of roughly 55 GWh. The model assumes a 30 % subsidy for “greenfield” cell fabs and a 20 % credit for retrofits that adopt low‑carbon manufacturing processes, such as renewable‑powered sintering and water‑based electrolyte coating. The analysis also projects that the IAA will stimulate the construction of at least 12 cathode plants and 9 anode plants, closing the current material‑supply gap that forces many OEMs to import >70 % of their active materials.

Beyond capacity, the policy is expected to generate roughly 15 000 new jobs across the value chain and reduce the EU’s battery‑related CO₂ emissions by an estimated 12 MtCO₂e per year, thanks to higher domestic production and lower transport distances. The paper highlights that the IAA’s “circular‑economy” provisions—mandating a minimum 70 % recycling rate for end‑of‑life packs—could spur the development of advanced hydrometallurgical recycling hubs capable of recovering >95 % of lithium, nickel, and cobalt.

Critics argue that the funding may be insufficient to compete with China’s aggressive subsidies, but the analysis notes that the IAA’s focus on high‑value‑added processes (e.g., solid‑state cell R&D) could give Europe a technological edge. If the projected capacity materializes, Europe would be positioned to meet the IAA’s own target of 300 GWh of domestic cell production by 2035, reducing reliance on imports and strengthening supply‑chain resilience for the EV market. [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/10/01/leveraging-the-iaa-to-build-europes-battery-value-chain/)
