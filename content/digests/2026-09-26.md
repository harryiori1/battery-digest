## 01 Tesla Deploys 144 Megapacks for Australia’s First 8‑Hour Grid Battery
RWE’s Limondale Energy Storage project in New South Wales has become Australia’s longest‑duration grid‑scale battery, powered by 144 Tesla Megapacks. Each Megapack delivers 10 MWh of usable energy, giving the installation a total capacity of 1.44 GWh and the ability to discharge continuously for eight hours at full power – a first for the Australian market, where most storage assets are limited to two‑hour durations.

The project, commissioned in August 2026, is linked to the 1.2‑GW Limondale solar farm and is designed to smooth the farm’s output, provide frequency regulation, and act as a firm‑capacity back‑up for the regional grid. RWE estimates the battery can shave up to 2 GW of peak demand for short periods, reducing reliance on gas peakers and cutting annual CO₂ emissions by roughly 300 kt.

Tesla’s Megapack architecture, which integrates inverter, thermal management, and battery management system (BMS) in a single container, simplifies deployment and reduces balance‑of‑system costs. The eight‑hour capability is achieved through a combination of higher energy density NCA cells and a sophisticated software stack that optimises charge‑discharge cycles to preserve cell health.

Industry analysts see the Limondale installation as a benchmark for future Australian projects, especially as the nation pushes toward 100 GW of renewable generation by 2035. The scale also demonstrates that utility‑scale lithium‑ion storage can now meet both short‑term ancillary services and longer‑duration firming roles, potentially accelerating policy support for similar deployments.

## 02 CATL’s 14‑Year‑Old LFP Prismatic Cells Pass Decade‑Long Age Test
A recent study published by InsideEVs examined a batch of CATL‑manufactured LFP prismatic cells that were first installed in 2009 for a large‑scale energy‑storage project in China. After 14 years of continuous operation, the cells were removed, bench‑tested, and compared against brand‑new equivalents.

Key findings show that the aged cells retain 92 % of their original nominal capacity (approximately 3.3 Ah per cell) and exhibit only a modest increase in internal resistance—about 15 % higher than fresh cells. Safety tests, including nail penetration and over‑charge simulations, revealed no thermal runaway events, confirming the intrinsic stability of LFP chemistry even after prolonged cycling.

The project’s cumulative throughput exceeded 1.2 million full‑cycle equivalents, translating to roughly 5 GWh of energy throughput per cell. CATL’s robust cell‑to‑cell uniformity and its proprietary electrolyte formulation are credited for the minimal degradation. The results bolster confidence in the long‑term economics of LFP‑based storage, where a 10‑year service life is often a prerequisite for utility financing.

From a recycling perspective, the study suggests that cells with such low degradation can be repurposed for second‑life applications (e.g., micro‑grids, backup power) before entering material recovery streams, further improving the overall sustainability profile of lithium‑ion storage.

The findings are likely to influence procurement specifications for utilities and developers, who may now consider LFP as a viable alternative to NMC/NCA for long‑duration projects, especially where safety and cost are paramount.

## 03 BYD Announces Flagship Han EV with Over 1,000 km Range
Chinese automaker BYD has set a launch date for its upcoming flagship sedan, the Han EV, slated for release in October 2026. The vehicle will be equipped with the latest generation of BYD’s Blade Battery—a large‑format LFP pack that delivers a claimed 150 kWh of usable energy, enabling a WLTP‑tested range of more than 1,000 km (621 mi) on a single charge.

Pricing starts at roughly $37,000 (USD) for the base model, positioning the Han as a direct competitor to the Tesla Model S and the Mercedes‑EQE. BYD highlights a 30 % charge in 15 minutes using its proprietary 350 kW “Flash Charging 2.0” system, which can be accessed at the company’s expanding network of 2,000 highway fast‑charging stations across China.

The Blade Battery’s cell‑to‑pack integration reduces weight by 15 % compared with conventional LFP packs, while maintaining the chemistry’s renowned thermal stability. BYD also announced that the Han will feature an upgraded BMS capable of predictive health monitoring, extending the pack’s warranty to 10 years or 1.5 million km.

Analysts view the Han’s range claim as a watershed moment for LFP technology, which has traditionally lagged behind NMC/NCA in energy density. If the real‑world performance matches the WLTP figures, BYD could shift market perception, encouraging other manufacturers to adopt LFP for premium models, especially in markets where safety regulations and cost pressures favour lithium‑iron‑phosphate.

The launch underscores BYD’s strategy of leveraging its vertical integration—from cell chemistry to pack engineering—to undercut rivals on price while delivering performance that rivals higher‑cost chemistries.
