## 01 Svolt Starts Mass Production of 100 kWh Semi‑Solid Fast‑Charging Packs  
Chinese battery maker Svolt announced on 26 October that it has begun full‑scale production of its 100 kWh semi‑solid lithium‑ion packs at the newly‑completed Jiangsu plant. The semi‑solid design replaces the traditional slurry‑coating step with a pre‑mixed, partially‑solid electrode that can be cast in a single pass, cutting manufacturing time by roughly 30 % and reducing solvent emissions.  

According to the company, the 100 kWh modules deliver an energy density of about 300 Wh kg⁻¹ and can be charged to 80 % state‑of‑charge in under 10 minutes using a 350 kW charger. The fast‑charge capability is enabled by a high‑conductivity solid‑state electrolyte that remains liquid‑like at room temperature, mitigating the dendrite‑growth concerns that have hampered earlier solid‑state concepts.  

Svolt plans an annual output of 5 GWh from the Jiangsu line, enough to equip roughly 50,000 medium‑size EVs or to provide grid‑scale storage for 2 GW‑h of renewable‑energy buffering. The company says its first customers are a fleet operator in Shanghai that will use the packs for electric buses, and a utility in Guangdong that will deploy them in a 200 MWh battery‑as‑a‑service project.  

The move marks the first time a semi‑solid chemistry has reached commercial‑scale volumes, a milestone that could accelerate the industry’s shift toward higher energy density and faster charging without the safety penalties of pure solid‑state cells. Analysts at BloombergNEF note that if Svolt can maintain yields above 95 %, the cost per kWh could fall to $80, making the technology competitive with conventional LFP packs for both automotive and stationary markets.  

The announcement follows a wave of Chinese firms scaling next‑generation chemistries after the Chinese government’s 2025 “Battery Innovation” subsidy program, which earmarked ¥12 billion for semi‑solid and solid‑state pilots. Svolt’s rapid transition from pilot to mass production suggests the subsidy criteria—demonstrated cycle life of >2 000 cycles and a safety rating equivalent to IEC 62660‑2—have been met.  

Overall, Svolt’s launch could reshape supply‑chain dynamics, offering OEMs a new source of high‑energy, fast‑charging packs that sidestep the lithium‑cobalt constraints that have dominated the market for the past decade.

## 02 BYD Opens Reservations for Second‑Gen Seal 07 with Blade Battery and 15‑Minute Flash Charge  
On 26 October BYD announced that reservations are now open for the second‑generation Seal 07, the latest iteration of its flagship sedan built around the company’s proprietary Blade Battery. The new model retains the LFP chemistry that underpins the Blade design but incorporates a revised cell architecture that pushes gravimetric energy density to roughly 210 Wh kg⁻¹—about 15 % higher than the first‑gen version.  

The most headline‑grabbing feature is the “15‑minute flash charge”: using a 250 kW DC fast‑charger, the 70 kWh pack can be topped up from 0 % to 80 % in just 15 minutes, a claim BYD backs with internal testing that shows a temperature rise of less than 12 °C, well within its thermal‑management limits. The flash‑charge protocol is compatible with the China‑wide GB/T 27930 standard, meaning most public fast‑charging stations can support the function without firmware upgrades.  

Range figures have also been nudged upward. BYD lists a CLTC‑based 600 km (373 mi) range for the base trim, while the top‑end “Performance” variant, equipped with dual‑motor all‑wheel drive, promises 650 km. Pricing starts at ¥199,900 (≈ US$27,800) for the rear‑wheel‑drive version, with the all‑wheel‑drive model adding ¥30,000. Deliveries are slated for the first quarter of 2027, initially targeting the domestic Chinese market before a planned rollout to Europe in late 2027.  

Industry observers note that BYD’s decision to keep the Blade Battery LFP‑based, rather than moving to NMC or nickel‑rich chemistries, reflects a strategic bet on safety, cost, and raw‑material security. The Blade design’s thin, staggered cell layout also reduces the need for heavy metal casings, cutting pack weight by roughly 8 % compared with conventional LFP packs.  

The reservation campaign has already attracted more than 30,000 pre‑orders within the first 48 hours, according to BYD’s sales chief, indicating strong consumer appetite for a high‑range, fast‑charging sedan that avoids the cobalt‑price volatility that has plagued many competitors. Analysts at IHS Markit predict the Seal 07 could capture up to 5 % of the Chinese midsize‑sedan market by 2028, bolstering BYD’s position as the world’s largest EV maker by volume.

## 03 Audi Recalls 9,048 Q4 E‑Tron SUVs Over Battery‑Fire Risk  
Audi issued a Europe‑wide safety recall on 26 October for 9,048 Q4 E‑Tron SUVs built between 2022 and 2024 after discovering a defect that could cause the high‑voltage battery pack to overheat and, in rare cases, ignite. The problem stems from a manufacturing inconsistency in the cooling‑plate brazing process supplied by LG Energy Solution, which can lead to a localized hot spot under high‑load conditions such as rapid acceleration or fast charging.  

The affected vehicles cover model years 2022‑2024 and are equipped with the 77 kWh (gross) lithium‑ion pack that delivers an EPA‑rated 265 mi (426 km) range. Audi’s technical service bulletin instructs dealers to replace the entire battery module—approximately 120 kg of cells, cooling hardware, and associated wiring—at no cost to owners. The replacement modules incorporate an updated cooling‑plate design with a reinforced brazed joint and an additional temperature sensor that triggers a protective shutdown if the pack temperature exceeds 55 °C.  

Audi estimates the recall will affect roughly 0.3 % of all Q4 E‑Trons sold globally, but the company is taking a proactive stance to protect its brand reputation after a similar battery‑fire issue forced Volkswagen to recall over 12,000 ID.4s earlier this year. The recall is being coordinated with the European Union’s Rapid Alert System for Non‑Food Products (RAPEX), and owners will be notified via email, postal mail, and the Audi Connect app.  

The incident has reignited debate over the safety of high‑energy‑density packs in premium EVs. While the Q4 E‑Tron’s pack uses a nickel‑cobalt‑manganese (NCM) chemistry with an energy density of about 250 Wh kg⁻¹, the failure mode is unrelated to chemistry and instead tied to thermal‑management hardware. LG Energy Solution has pledged to audit its production lines and has already begun a joint root‑cause analysis with Audi’s engineering team.  

From a market perspective, the recall could shave a few points off Audi’s EV sales forecast for 2026‑27, but analysts at Morgan Stanley expect the impact to be limited given the relatively small volume and the brand’s strong after‑sales support network. The episode also underscores the importance of rigorous quality‑control in battery pack assembly, especially as OEMs push for higher energy densities and faster charging rates.
