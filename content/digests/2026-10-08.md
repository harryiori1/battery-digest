## 01 Suzuki e SKY Launches with BYD 48 kWh LFP Pack, Pricing at $13,500
Suzuki announced the Japanese market debut of the **e SKY**, its first electric kei‑car, priced at an eye‑catching **US $13,500**. The low price is made possible by a partnership with BYD, which supplies a **48 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack** built on BYD’s latest cell chemistry. The pack delivers a WLTP range of roughly **210 km** (130 mi), sufficient for typical urban commuting and the short‑distance trips that define the kei‑car segment.

The e SKY’s battery is integrated into a skateboard chassis, allowing a flat floor and maximizing interior space within the 3.4‑meter wheelbase. BYD’s LFP cells are touted for their **thermal stability, low cost, and 2,000‑cycle lifespan**, attributes that align with Suzuki’s goal of an affordable, low‑maintenance EV. Production will start at Suzuki’s **Sakurai plant** in early 2027, with an initial annual volume of **30,000 units** earmarked for the Japanese market.

Why it matters: The collaboration demonstrates how established OEMs can leverage BYD’s mature LFP supply chain to undercut competitors on price without sacrificing safety or durability. At a time when many automakers are shifting to higher‑energy‑density NMC or nickel‑cobalt‑aluminum chemistries, Suzuki’s choice underscores a growing niche for cost‑focused LFP solutions, especially in markets where vehicle price sensitivity outweighs range anxiety. If the e SKY meets its sales targets, it could spur other Japanese manufacturers to adopt similar BYD‑sourced packs, reinforcing BYD’s position as a global battery supplier beyond China.

## 02 LG Energy Solution Posts Record Q3 2026 Battery Sales, Driven by EV and Energy‑Storage Demand
South Korean battery giant **LG Energy Solution (LG ES)** released its third‑quarter 2026 results on **October 3**, reporting a **27 % year‑over‑year increase** in combined electric‑vehicle (EV) and energy‑storage‑system (ESS) shipments. The company shipped **23.8 GWh** of EV cells and **5.2 GWh** of ESS modules, pushing total output to **29.0 GWh**, the highest quarterly volume in its history.

The EV surge was anchored by **high‑nickel NMC 811 cells** supplied to **Tesla, Hyundai, and several Chinese OEMs**, with an average energy density of **240 Wh/kg**. Meanwhile, the ESS growth stemmed from a **45 % jump in utility‑scale storage contracts** in the United States and Europe, where LG ES’s **prismatic LFP modules** (up to **350 Wh/L**) are being deployed to back renewable‑energy farms and data‑center micro‑grids.

Profitability rose to **₩1.9 trillion** (≈ US $1.4 bn), driven by higher average selling prices (ASPs) for NMC cells and improved yields at its new **Gigafactory 5** in South Korea, which began mass production in early 2026. The company also announced a **$2 billion investment** to expand its LFP line in the United States, aiming for an additional **10 GWh** of annual capacity by 2029.

Implications: LG ES’s record underscores the **dual‑track growth** of the battery market—high‑energy‑density cells for premium EVs and cost‑effective LFP modules for stationary storage. The U.S. investment signals a strategic shift to localize supply chains amid geopolitical pressures, potentially easing the chronic “cell shortage” that has plagued automakers since 2023. Competitors such as CATL and BYD will need to match LG ES’s capacity expansion to retain market share in both segments.

## 03 BYD Introduces Ti 7 Micro‑EV in Uzbekistan, Featuring 30 kWh LFP Pack and Local Assembly
Chinese EV leader **BYD** launched the **Ti 7** (marketed locally as **Fang Cheng Bao Tai 7**) in **Tashkent, Uzbekistan**, on **October 8**. The vehicle is a **micro‑electric crossover** aimed at urban commuters and rides‑hailing fleets. It is equipped with a **30 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack** that delivers a WLTP range of **280 km** (174 mi) and supports **DC fast charging at 80 kW**, reaching 80 % state‑of‑charge in roughly **30 minutes**.

Production will be carried out at BYD’s newly‑established **Uzbekistan assembly plant**, a joint venture with the state‑owned **Uzbekistan Automotive Holding**. The plant is designed to localize **≈ 70 %** of component content, including the battery pack, which will be assembled from BYD‑manufactured cells shipped from its **Ningde** facility. BYD expects an initial annual output of **15,000 units**, with plans to scale to **50,000** by 2029 as demand for affordable EVs rises across Central Asia.

Strategic relevance: The Ti 7 marks BYD’s first **full‑scale vehicle production** outside China in the Central Asian region, signaling a push to capture emerging‑market share before Western OEMs establish a foothold. By integrating its own LFP technology, BYD avoids reliance on nickel‑based chemistries, reducing exposure to volatile commodity prices and aligning with regional preferences for **low‑cost, long‑life batteries**. The move also dovetails with Uzbekistan’s “**Green Mobility**” policy, which offers tax incentives for locally assembled EVs and aims to have **30 %** of new vehicle registrations be electric by 2035.
