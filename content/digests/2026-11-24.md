## 01 Jackery and Every Electric Launch Plug‑In Virtual Power Plant in New York City
Jackery, the maker of portable power stations, has teamed with Brooklyn‑based aggregator Every Electric to roll out the United States’ first plug‑in virtual power plant (VPP) aimed at dense urban housing. The pilot, announced on 21 November, will initially enrol up to 5,000 apartment units across Manhattan and Brooklyn, aggregating roughly 10 MW of dispatchable capacity. Each participating household receives a Jackery Explorer 2000‑plus unit – a 2 kWh lithium‑ion pack equipped with a built‑in inverter and IoT‑enabled controller. When the grid signals a shortage, the VPP software automatically draws power from the distributed packs, feeding it back to the local distribution network or directly offsetting the building’s load.

The model differs from traditional utility‑scale batteries because the assets remain on‑site, providing owners with backup power during outages while earning revenue through grid services. Every Electric’s platform handles real‑time forecasting, bid submission into the New York Independent System Operator (NYISO) market, and settlement, allowing participants to earn an estimated $30‑$45 per kWh per year in ancillary service payments.

Industry analysts view the project as a proof‑point for “distributed‑resource‑as‑a‑service” models that could unlock gigawatt‑scale capacity without the need for new land‑intensive farms. If successful, the partnership plans to expand to other dense metros such as Chicago and Los Angeles, leveraging Jackery’s growing portfolio of 1‑5 kWh units. The initiative also aligns with New York’s “Reforming the Energy Vision” (REV) agenda, which encourages flexible, customer‑owned resources to bolster grid resilience.

## 02 U.S. Cumulative Grid‑Scale Battery Storage Tops 200 GWh
The United States crossed the 200 GWh threshold for installed grid‑scale battery storage on 21 November, according to data compiled by the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) and corroborated by industry tracker Wood Mackenzie. The milestone reflects a 38 % year‑over‑year increase, driven largely by a surge in megawatt‑hour‑scale projects announced in the first half of 2026. In the past twelve months, developers added roughly 30 GW of new capacity, with the bulk coming from lithium‑ion systems supplied by Tesla (Megapack), Fluence (Gridstack), and CATL’s LFP modules.

Key projects include a 1.2 GW/4.8 GWh battery farm in Texas backed by a joint venture between Vistra and CATL, a 800 MW/3.2 GWh installation in California’s Mojave Desert commissioned by NextEra Energy, and a series of 200 MW/800 MWh “behind‑the‑meter” assets for data‑center clusters in the Pacific Northwest. The average round‑trip efficiency of the new fleet sits at 92 %, while the weighted average cost per kilowatt‑hour has fallen to $115, a 22 % drop from early‑2025 levels.

The expansion is being propelled by several policy levers: the Inflation Reduction Act’s tax credit for energy storage, state‑level mandates for renewable‑integration, and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (FERC) Order 2222, which enables aggregated storage to compete in wholesale markets. Analysts predict the U.S. storage market could reach 500 GWh by 2029, positioning battery farms as a cornerstone of the nation’s 2030 carbon‑neutral target.

## 03 China’s October Power‑Battery Installations Hit Record 99.4 GWh, LFP Share Near 87%
China reported a record‑high 99.4 GWh of power‑battery installations in October 2026, according to the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM). The figure represents a 12 % month‑on‑month rise and pushes the cumulative 2026 installation total past the 500 GWh mark for the first time. Notably, lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) chemistry accounted for 86.9 % of the October volume, underscoring the continued dominance of LFP in both passenger‑vehicle and utility‑scale applications.

The surge was driven by several large‑scale projects: a 2.5 GWh LFP battery park in Shandong built by CATL for a regional grid, a 1.8 GWh LFP pack supplied by BYD for a new solar‑plus‑storage hub in Inner Mongolia, and a series of 300 MWh “behind‑the‑meter” installations for industrial parks in Guangdong. The average energy density of the newly installed packs hovered around 160 Wh/kg, reflecting incremental gains from high‑nickel cathode tweaks and silicon‑enhanced anodes, while maintaining the safety and cost advantages of LFP.

Policy incentives remain a key catalyst. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) extended its “New Energy Vehicle” subsidy through 2027, with a bonus tier for vehicles equipped with domestically produced LFP packs. Simultaneously, the State Grid Corporation announced a 5 % tariff rebate for projects that integrate LFP storage with renewable generation, aiming to curb cobalt dependence and improve supply‑chain resilience.

Analysts view the October record as a bellwether for the rest of 2026, projecting that total annual installations could exceed 1.2 TWh, cementing China’s role as the world’s largest battery‑manufacturing and deployment hub.
