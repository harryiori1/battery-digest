## 01 Redwood Materials and Rivian Expand EV Battery Recycling Partnership  
Redwood Materials and Rivian announced an expanded recycling agreement that will double the capacity of material recovery at Rivian’s Normal, Illinois factory. The original 2024 partnership covered a single “closed‑loop” line capable of processing up to 5 GWh of used lithium‑ion packs per year. Under the new deal, a second line will be installed by mid‑2027, bringing total throughput to roughly 10 GWh annually—enough to handle the end‑of‑life batteries from Rivian’s R1T pickup, R1S SUV and the upcoming R2 platform.  

Redwood will take the spent modules, strip them down to cathode, anode and electrolyte components, and apply its proprietary hydrometallurgical process to recover nickel, cobalt, lithium and manganese at >95 % purity. The recovered materials will be sold back to battery manufacturers, including CATL and LG Energy Solution, creating a “circular” supply chain that reduces reliance on virgin mining.  

The partnership also includes a joint R&D fund of $50 million aimed at improving the recyclability of high‑energy‑density chemistries such as NMC 811 and solid‑state cells, which are expected to dominate new EV models from 2028 onward. Rivian’s Chief Sustainability Officer, Jeff Kram, said the move “accelerates our goal of achieving a 100 % recycled‑content battery pack by 2035.”  

Industry analysts view the expansion as a bellwether for the broader EV recycling market, which BloombergNEF estimates will need to process 1,200 GWh of used packs by 2030 to meet circular‑economy targets. Redwood’s scaling effort could set a template for other OEM‑recycler collaborations, especially as regulatory pressure mounts in the U.S. and EU to enforce end‑of‑life stewardship.

## 02 Battery‑Electric Solar‑Powered Reefer Units Begin Commercial Service in Australia  
Wickham Freight Lines, a family‑owned logistics company operating across New South Wales, has taken delivery of the first production‑grade battery‑electric refrigerated trailer (reefer) units from Australian start‑up GreenTech Logistics. The units combine a 150 kWh lithium‑ion pack with a roof‑mounted 2 kW solar array, delivering up to 12 hours of continuous cooling without diesel fuel.  

Each unit is equipped with a high‑efficiency vapor‑compression chiller that draws 3 kW at peak load, while the battery management system (BMS) optimises charge‑discharge cycles to maintain the cargo temperature between 2 °C and 8 °C. The solar array supplies roughly 30 % of the daily energy demand under typical Australian summer insolation, reducing grid draw and extending range.  

GreenTech claims the total cost of ownership (TCO) is 25 % lower than conventional diesel‑powered reefers over a five‑year horizon, thanks to fuel savings, lower maintenance (no engine oil changes), and eligibility for government emissions rebates. The company also highlights a 40 % reduction in CO₂ emissions per tonne‑kilometre transported.  

The rollout follows a pilot program in 2025 that demonstrated a 98 % reliability rate across 200 000 km of operation. Industry observers note that the technology could be a game‑changer for cold‑chain logistics, especially in remote regions where diesel supply is costly and emissions regulations are tightening. If the adoption curve mirrors that of electric trucks, the market for battery‑electric reefers could reach 10 % of the global refrigerated transport fleet by 2032.

## 03 CATL Plant Tour Highlights Battery‑Fire Anxiety and Power‑Loss Concerns  
Chinese EV maker Zhiji (智己) recently published a video tour of CATL’s Ningde Times “Qilin” plant, focusing on the company’s response to growing OEM anxiety over battery safety and energy‑density loss. The footage, posted on Zhiji’s official WeChat channel and reported by the industry portal 第一电动, shows engineers dissecting a 60 kWh NCM 811 pouch cell that exhibited a 12 % capacity fade after just 300 cycles.  

CATL’s senior technical director, Liu Wei, explained that the degradation stemmed from uneven lithium plating during fast‑charge events exceeding 2 C. To mitigate this, CATL is rolling out a new “gradient‑doping” cathode architecture that reduces localised current density, aiming to cut capacity loss to under 5 % after 500 cycles at 1.5 C charge rates.  

The tour also addressed fire‑risk concerns. CATL demonstrated a new flame‑retardant separator made from ceramic‑coated polyethylene, which survived a 1 kW short‑circuit test for 30 seconds without thermal runaway. The company claims the separator can lower the probability of cell‑level ignition by 70 % compared with conventional polyolefin separators.  

Zhiji’s CEO, He Xiaopeng, praised the transparency, noting that “OEMs need to understand the trade‑offs between energy density, fast‑charging capability and safety.” He added that Zhiji will incorporate the gradient‑doped cathodes in its next‑generation Lixiang model slated for 2027, targeting a 650 km WLTC range with 15‑minute 80 % charging.  

Analysts see the public dissection as a sign that Chinese battery makers are moving from proprietary secrecy toward collaborative safety standards, a shift that could ease regulatory scrutiny in Europe and North America where battery‑fire incidents have prompted stricter testing regimes.
