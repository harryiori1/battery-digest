## 01 CATL Advances 20 Ah Solid‑State Cells, Targets 500 Wh/kg
Chinese battery leader CATL announced a major milestone in its solid‑state research program: a 20 Ah pouch‑type cell that delivers an energy density of 500 Wh kg⁻¹ on a laboratory basis. The prototype, unveiled at a closed‑door technical symposium in Shanghai, uses a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte and a high‑nickel NMC cathode paired with a silicon‑graphite anode. According to CATL’s chief technology officer, the cell’s gravimetric energy density surpasses the best commercial lithium‑ion offerings by roughly 30 % while maintaining a safety profile that eliminates the risk of thermal runaway.

The company plans to begin pilot‑line production of 5 Ah solid‑state cells in 2026, scaling to a 20 Ah line in 2027. CATL expects the first automotive applications to appear in premium EVs from Chinese OEMs, with a target of 2029 for volume production. The move is significant because solid‑state technology has long been touted as the next leap for EV range and charging speed, yet most players remain in the proof‑of‑concept stage. CATL’s announcement positions it as the first major manufacturer to approach commercial‑scale volumes.

Industry analysts note that the 500 Wh/kg figure, if reproducible at scale, could enable sub‑400 km range‑increase per kilowatt‑hour, dramatically reducing the battery weight penalty for long‑range models. However, challenges remain in electrolyte stability, manufacturing yield, and cost—solid‑state cells are currently projected to be 1.5‑2 × more expensive than conventional lithium‑ion packs. CATL’s aggressive timeline suggests it is betting on economies of scale and a vertically integrated supply chain to offset these costs.

The development also has geopolitical implications. By advancing solid‑state technology domestically, China reduces its reliance on foreign lithium‑ion patents and could capture a larger share of the premium EV market. Competitors such as Samsung SDI, Toyota, and QuantumScape have disclosed similar targets, but none have yet demonstrated a 20 Ah cell at this energy density.

Overall, CATL’s breakthrough could accelerate the industry’s shift toward safer, higher‑energy batteries, reshaping vehicle architecture, charging infrastructure, and the economics of EV ownership. ## 02 EVE Energy Opens German VMI Warehouse in Leipzig for BMW and Other OEMs
EVE Energy, a leading Chinese lithium‑ion cell maker, has inaugurated a Vendor‑Managed Inventory (VMI) facility in Leipzig, Germany, to serve European automakers such as BMW, Audi, and several emerging EV startups. The 8,000 m² warehouse, operational from early October, holds up to 1.5 GWh of ready‑to‑ship LFP and NCM cells, enabling OEMs to pull inventory on a just‑in‑time basis without the need for long‑haul shipping from China.

The Leipzig hub is part of EVE’s broader “Europe‑First” strategy, which includes a new cell‑manufacturing line slated for 2028 in nearby Poland. By positioning inventory closer to the end‑user, EVE aims to cut lead times from 45‑60 days to under 10 days, a critical advantage as European manufacturers grapple with supply‑chain disruptions caused by geopolitical tensions and pandemic‑related port backlogs.

EVE’s VMI model also incorporates real‑time demand forecasting using AI algorithms that sync with OEM production schedules. The system automatically replenishes stock when thresholds are crossed, reducing the risk of stock‑outs and excess inventory. For BMW, the arrangement translates into a projected 15 % reduction in battery‑related logistics costs and a smoother rollout of its next‑generation iX3 and i4 models.

Industry observers see the Leipzig warehouse as a signal that Chinese cell suppliers are moving beyond pure export models toward integrated supply‑chain partnerships in key markets. This mirrors similar moves by CATL and BYD, which have opened assembly and recycling facilities in Europe. The VMI approach could also help EVE meet the EU’s upcoming Battery Passport requirements by providing traceability data directly from the warehouse management system.

While the facility currently focuses on standard cylindrical and pouch cells, EVE plans to introduce its upcoming high‑energy LFP cells (up to 260 Wh kg⁻¹) later this year, further aligning with European automakers’ push for cobalt‑free battery chemistries. ## 03 Project 707 Reaches 29 GWh Cumulative LFP Production Capacity
China’s massive “Project 707” LFP battery line, operated by a joint venture of several domestic manufacturers, has officially entered full‑scale production, achieving a cumulative capacity of 29 GWh. The milestone was announced by the OFweek 锂电 portal on 5 November and marks the first time the plant’s output has matched its design target of 30 GWh per year.

Project 707, located in the Jiangsu province, combines a continuous casting line, high‑throughput electrode coating, and an automated cell‑assembly line capable of producing 1.2 million 18650‑type cells per month. The plant primarily manufactures lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) cells for stationary storage, grid‑balancing, and low‑cost EV applications. With an average energy density of 160 Wh kg⁻¹, the cells are optimized for safety, longevity, and raw‑material cost reduction, aligning with China’s policy to phase out cobalt‑based chemistries.

The 29 GWh figure represents the total output since the line’s commercial debut in March 2025, and the plant is now operating at a 95 % capacity utilization rate. Analysts estimate that the additional supply will shave roughly 5 % off the global LFP price curve, potentially bringing the cost below $80 kWh for utility‑scale storage projects. This price pressure could accelerate the adoption of LFP in markets traditionally dominated by NCM chemistries, especially in Europe where regulatory incentives favor cobalt‑free solutions.

Beyond cost, the scale of Project 707 has strategic implications for the global battery supply chain. The plant’s vertical integration—from raw‑material preprocessing to cell assembly—reduces dependence on imported cathode materials and mitigates the impact of recent export restrictions on nickel and cobalt. Moreover, the joint‑venture structure includes a 20 % stake held by a European investment fund, facilitating technology transfer and potential downstream partnerships for battery pack manufacturers.

The achievement also dovetails with China’s broader “dual‑circulation” policy, reinforcing domestic capacity while positioning the country as a key exporter of affordable LFP modules for overseas grid‑storage projects. With the global stationary storage market projected to exceed 500 GWh by 2030, Project 707’s output could account for up to 6 % of total installed capacity, underscoring its significance in the race for energy‑storage dominance.
