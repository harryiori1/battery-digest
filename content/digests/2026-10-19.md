## 01 Huawei’s Newcool Joins CATL’s 2,520‑Station Battery‑Swap Network
Huawei’s automotive arm, branded as Newcool, signed a strategic cooperation framework with CATL and its swap subsidiary CAES on 19 October 2026. The agreement gives Huawei access to CATL’s “Choco‑Swap” network, which already operates 2,520 battery‑swap stations across China’s major cities and highways. Under the deal, Newcool‑branded electric vehicles will be compatible with the existing swap infrastructure, allowing drivers to replace a depleted pack in under three minutes.

The partnership is significant for several reasons. First, it validates CATL’s long‑standing bet that battery swapping can coexist with fast‑charging, especially for high‑utilisation fleets such as ride‑hailing and logistics. Second, Huawei brings its expertise in IoT, cloud connectivity, and AI‑driven fleet management, promising a more data‑rich swap experience—real‑time battery health monitoring, predictive maintenance, and dynamic pricing. Third, the collaboration helps Huawei re‑enter the EV market after a period of regulatory headwinds, positioning the company as a service‑oriented player rather than a traditional OEM.

Both parties will co‑develop a “smart‑swap” protocol that integrates Newcool’s vehicle telematics with CATL’s battery‑management system, enabling seamless authentication and billing via a unified app. The rollout will begin in the Pearl River Delta in Q4 2026, with plans to expand to the Yangtze River Delta and northern tier cities by mid‑2027. Analysts estimate that the combined network could support an additional 1.2 million swaps per year, potentially shaving 15 % off the total electricity demand of urban EV fleets.

The move also intensifies competition with Nio’s Swappable Battery Service, which currently operates around 1,800 stations. By leveraging CATL’s scale and Huawei’s digital ecosystem, the new alliance could set a de‑facto standard for battery‑swap operations in China and, if successful, provide a template for other markets where charging time remains a barrier.

## 02 Tesla Roadster to Feature a 200 kWh ‘Hyper‑Pack’ with 5‑Minute Ultra‑Fast Charging
Tesla’s upcoming 2026 Roadster is set to debut the company’s most advanced battery pack to date, dubbed the “Hyper‑Pack.” According to InsideEVs, the pack will deliver a usable capacity of 200 kWh, roughly double the 100 kWh pack used in the Model S Plaid. The Hyper‑Pack employs a new high‑nickel NMC chemistry combined with a proprietary silicon‑graphite anode, delivering an energy density of 350 Wh/kg at the cell level.

One of the headline features is a 5‑minute ultra‑fast charge capability: the Roadster can accept up to 1 MW of DC power, allowing an 80 % charge (160 kWh) in just five minutes. Tesla achieves this by integrating a 12‑MW on‑board charger that operates at 1 200 V and 800 A, supported by a liquid‑cooled thermal management system that keeps cell temperatures within the optimal 20‑30 °C window during high‑rate charging.

The pack architecture also introduces a modular “cell‑in‑module” design, where each module contains 96 cells arranged in a 12 × 8 matrix. This configuration simplifies assembly, improves redundancy (a single module failure reduces range by only 2 %), and enables rapid field replacement. The Hyper‑Pack is expected to cost roughly $150 kWh, a modest increase over the current $130 kWh price point for Tesla’s premium cells, thanks to economies of scale and the new manufacturing line slated for Fremont in early 2027.

From a market perspective, the Roadster’s battery specs aim to erase range anxiety for high‑performance sports cars and to showcase Tesla’s charging technology ahead of the broader rollout of its 800‑V Supercharger network. If the 5‑minute charge claim holds up in real‑world testing, it could redefine consumer expectations for ultra‑fast charging across the EV industry.

## 03 Volkswagen ID. Cross Now Offers a 37 kWh Battery for Urban Customers
Volkswagen has expanded the power‑train options for its compact ID. Cross crossover by adding a 37 kWh net‑capacity battery pack, announced on 19 October 2026. The new pack, built on VW’s MEB platform, uses a lithium‑ion NMC chemistry optimized for cost and durability, delivering a WLTP‑rated range of up to 324 km (201 mi) and a combined fuel‑economy figure of 5.8 L eq/100 km.

The 37 kWh variant targets city‑dwelling buyers who prioritize affordability over long‑distance capability. Priced at €24,990 before incentives, it undercuts the 45 kWh version by roughly €3,500, making the ID. Cross one of the most competitively priced EVs in the European market. Power output is offered in two tunes: an 85 kW (115 hp) motor for the base model and a 99 kW (135 hp) version for the higher‑spec trim, both paired with a single‑speed gearbox.

Charging performance is also tailored for urban use. The pack supports DC fast charging at up to 90 kW, allowing an 80 % charge in about 30 minutes, while AC charging on a standard 11 kW home wallbox replenishes the battery from 0 % to 100 % in roughly 4 hours. VW highlights a new thermal‑management algorithm that reduces degradation by 15 % compared with the previous 45 kWh pack, extending the warranty to 8 years or 160,000 km.

Analysts see the move as VW’s response to growing demand for lower‑cost EVs in densely populated European cities, where range‑anxiety is less of a concern and total‑ownership cost drives purchase decisions. By offering a smaller, cheaper battery, VW can improve its margin on the ID. Cross while still leveraging the same production line, preserving economies of scale.
