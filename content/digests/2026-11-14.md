## 01 Huawei Unveils 20 kW Home Battery to Challenge Tesla Powerwall
Huawei has entered the residential energy‑storage market with a 20 kW home battery that dwarfs Tesla’s Powerwall offerings. The system combines lithium‑ion cells with a silicon‑carbide (SiC) inverter, delivering conversion efficiencies above 98 % and a compact footprint that can be wall‑mounted or installed in a garage. A key differentiator is the AI‑powered energy‑management platform, which continuously forecasts household load, solar generation, and grid tariffs to optimise charge‑discharge cycles, provide peak‑shaving, and even participate in ancillary services for the local grid.

Modular design lets users stack multiple units, scaling capacity from the base 20 kW/40 kWh up to 100 kW/200 kWh for larger homes, small commercial sites, or micro‑grids in remote communities. Huawei claims the system can sustain 10 kW continuous discharge for up to 10 hours, enough to keep a typical 4‑person household running during a prolonged outage. The battery is equipped with a built‑in battery‑health monitoring system that predicts degradation and schedules preventive maintenance.

Pricing has not been disclosed, but Huawei’s strategy is to undercut the Powerwall 3, which tops out at 30 kW/45 kWh, by offering higher power at a comparable cost per kilowatt‑hour. The move signals Chinese consumer‑electronics firms leveraging their expertise in power electronics and AI to capture a fast‑growing home‑storage market, especially in regions where grid reliability is still a concern. If the product gains traction, it could pressure incumbent Western manufacturers to accelerate SiC adoption and AI integration across their storage portfolios.

## 02 Electrovaya Supplies Battery‑Swap Cells for Janus Electric’s Class 8 Semi‑Truck
Australian conversion specialist Janus Electric announced a supply agreement with Canadian battery maker Electrovaya to provide lithium‑ion cells for its upcoming Class 8 battery‑swap semi‑truck platform. The trucks are built on a chassis‑agnostic design that replaces a conventional diesel power‑train with a 300 kWh battery pack split into two 150 kWh modules. The modules are engineered for rapid removal and replacement in under five minutes at dedicated swap stations, enabling operators to maintain near‑continuous uptime without lengthy charging pauses.

Electrovaya will deliver its high‑energy NMC‑811 cells, which feature a 250 Wh/kg gravimetric energy density and a 2 C charge capability. The cells are paired with a proprietary thermal‑management system that keeps module temperatures below 35 °C during fast swaps, mitigating safety risks. Janus expects an initial production run of 150 trucks for logistics firms in Australia and New Zealand, with a pipeline of 1,200 units over the next three years.

The partnership highlights the growing importance of a dedicated supply chain for swap‑ready batteries, a niche that differs from standard long‑range EV packs. By securing a reliable source of high‑energy cells, Janus aims to address one of the major barriers to heavy‑duty electrification: the lack of fast, high‑capacity charging infrastructure. If the swap model proves economically viable, it could accelerate adoption of zero‑emission trucks in regions where charging time and depot space are limiting factors.

## 03 Korea Institute of Science and Technology Announces Next‑Gen Battery Separator
Researchers at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) have unveiled a next‑generation battery separator that could significantly improve charging speed and energy density for lithium‑ion cells. The new separator combines a thin ceramic coating with a highly porous polymer matrix, creating a uniform pore size of 0.2 µm that facilitates rapid lithium‑ion transport while maintaining mechanical strength and thermal stability up to 200 °C.

In laboratory tests, 18650‑format cells equipped with the KIST separator achieved an 80 % state‑of‑charge in 12 minutes at a 0.5 C rate—about a 30 % reduction in charge time compared with conventional polyethylene separators. Energy density also rose by roughly 10 % due to the thinner separator (12 µm vs. 20 µm) and reduced electrolyte volume. Importantly, the separator demonstrated superior dendrite suppression, lowering the risk of short‑circuit during high‑rate charging.

KIST plans to collaborate with major Chinese and Korean cell manufacturers to scale the technology, targeting mass production by 2028. If adopted widely, the separator could enable EVs to add 200 km of range in a ten‑minute charge, easing range‑anxiety concerns and reducing the need for extensive fast‑charging networks. The development also aligns with industry goals to improve safety and longevity, as the ceramic layer adds an extra barrier against thermal runaway.
