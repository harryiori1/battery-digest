## 01 Ford Begins In‑House Production of LFP Batteries at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan
Ford announced that its BlueOval Battery Park Michigan (BBPM) is now producing lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) cells for its upcoming electric vehicles, marking the automaker’s transition from a pure licensing model with CATL to full‑scale in‑house manufacturing. The first production line, a 30 GWh annual capacity “fast‑track” cell line, began ramp‑up in July 2026 and is expected to reach 150 GWh by 2032, enough to supply roughly 1 million EVs per year.

The BBPM facility, located in Flat Rock, Michigan, uses a proprietary dry‑coating process that reduces solvent usage by 40 % and cuts cell‑to‑pack cost to $95 kWh, a $10 kWh improvement over the previous CATL‑licensed supply. Ford’s engineering team has integrated a new high‑energy‑density LFP chemistry that delivers 210 Wh/kg and a 2,000‑cycle life, enabling a 300‑mile (480 km) range on a single pack for its F‑150 Lightning and upcoming Mustang Mach‑E refresh.

Strategically, the move gives Ford greater control over supply chain resilience, especially amid ongoing geopolitical tensions affecting cobalt and nickel markets. By internalising cell production, Ford can also accelerate its “BlueOval Battery” roadmap, which includes a solid‑state pilot slated for 2028. The partnership with CATL remains for cathode material supply, but Ford will own the cell‑making equipment and IP.

Industry analysts see the development as a watershed for U.S. battery sovereignty. The U.S. Department of Energy has earmarked $2 billion in grants for domestic cell capacity, and Ford’s project qualifies for $300 million in tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act. If the plant meets its 2028 target of 80 GWh, it would become the largest single LFP line in North America, narrowing the gap with Asian rivals.

Ford’s in‑house production also promises faster iteration cycles. The company claims it can introduce new cell formats within six months, compared with the 12‑month cadence typical of external suppliers. This agility is crucial as automakers chase higher energy density and lower cost to meet the 2030 target of sub‑$100 kWh battery packs.

Overall, Ford’s shift signals a broader trend of OEMs moving up the value chain to secure supply, reduce costs, and tailor chemistries to vehicle platforms. The success of BBPM will likely influence other U.S. manufacturers considering similar vertical integration.

## 02 Huawei Unveils 20 kW AI‑Managed Home Battery to Rival Tesla Powerwall
Huawei’s Consumer Business Group introduced a 20 kW residential battery system on 14 November 2026, positioning it as a direct competitor to Tesla’s Powerwall 2.0. The new “FusionHome” unit combines a silicon‑carbide (SiC) inverter, a modular lithium‑ion pack, and an AI‑driven energy management platform that optimises charge‑discharge cycles based on real‑time grid tariffs, weather forecasts, and household consumption patterns.

The battery pack consists of four 5 kWh modules that can be stacked for up to 40 kWh capacity, offering flexibility for larger homes or small commercial sites. Each module uses a high‑voltage (800 V) LFP cell architecture, delivering a round‑trip efficiency of 96 % and a 5‑year warranty covering 80 % of original capacity. The SiC inverter, a first for residential storage, enables a peak power output of 20 kW with a 99 % efficiency, reducing thermal losses and extending component life.

Huawei’s AI engine, built on its Ascend chipset, continuously learns the household’s load profile and predicts optimal charging windows, especially leveraging time‑of‑use tariffs. Early field trials in Shenzhen showed a 12 % reduction in electricity bills compared with conventional inverter‑only systems, and a 30 % improvement in self‑consumption of rooftop solar generation.

From a market perspective, the FusionHome targets the premium segment, with a list price of ¥149,000 (≈ $21,500) for the 20 kWh configuration—roughly 15 % higher than the Powerwall 2.0, but justified by higher power output and AI features. Huawei plans to ship the system globally through its partner network, starting with Europe and North America in Q1 2027, followed by Asia‑Pacific later in the year.

The launch underscores Huawei’s broader push into the energy storage arena, complementing its earlier 10 kW commercial battery and its “Smart Energy” cloud platform. Analysts note that Huawei’s strong presence in telecom infrastructure could give it an edge in integrating storage with 5G‑enabled micro‑grids, potentially opening new revenue streams in grid‑services markets.

If the FusionHome gains traction, it could pressure Tesla to accelerate its own next‑generation Powerwall roadmap, while also prompting other Chinese OEMs to upscale their residential storage offerings.

## 03 East Midlands Mayor Secures £47.55 M for 189 Battery‑Electric Buses
The East Midlands Combined Authority (EMCA) announced on 25 November 2026 a £47.55 million investment to procure 189 battery‑electric buses (BEBs) for the region’s public‑transport network. The funding, sourced from a mix of local authority budgets, the UK Department for Transport’s Zero‑Emission Bus Grant, and private‑sector contributions, aims to replace aging diesel fleets and meet the UK’s target of a fully zero‑emission bus fleet by 2035.

The contract has been awarded to a consortium led by BYD, which will supply the BEBs equipped with its latest Blade LFP battery packs. Each bus will carry a 350 kWh battery, delivering a range of up to 250 miles (400 km) on a single charge—sufficient for a full day of urban operation with a single overnight top‑up. The batteries are designed for rapid 30‑minute DC fast charging at depot stations, enabling a “charge‑while‑park” model that minimises downtime.

In addition to the vehicles, the project includes the installation of 12 depot‑level 250 kW chargers and 6 on‑route rapid‑charge stations strategically placed along high‑frequency routes. The charging infrastructure is managed by a cloud‑based energy‑management system that balances grid load, incorporates renewable energy from nearby solar farms, and provides ancillary services such as frequency regulation.

The investment is expected to cut local CO₂ emissions by an estimated 120,000 tonnes annually and reduce noise pollution, improving air quality in densely populated towns such as Nottingham, Derby, and Leicester. The project also creates approximately 350 jobs in vehicle maintenance, charging‑station operation, and battery‑recycling services, with a dedicated end‑of‑life plan that will send spent modules to a UK‑based recycling facility targeting a 95 % material recovery rate.

Financially, the BEB procurement averages £250,000 per bus, a 30 % cost reduction compared with similar orders in 2022, thanks to economies of scale and the declining price of LFP cells (now under $80 kWh). The EMCA expects a total cost of ownership advantage of £15,000 per vehicle over a 12‑year lifecycle, driven by lower fuel, maintenance, and emissions‑tax expenses.

The initiative aligns with the UK’s “Green Bus Challenge” and serves as a template for other regional authorities seeking to decarbonise public transport. Success will depend on reliable battery performance, effective charging logistics, and continued policy support for clean‑energy mobility.
