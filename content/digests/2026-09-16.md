## 01 CATL Announces Joint‑Tech Deal with Egypt’s BME to Build 10 GWh Battery‑Pack Plant
Chinese battery giant CATL has signed a technology‑transfer agreement with Egypt’s Battery Manufacturing Enterprise (BME) to establish a local lithium‑ion pack production line. The partnership, announced on 16 September, will see CATL provide its proprietary cell‑to‑pack architecture, battery management system (BMS) software, and quality‑control protocols, while BME will handle cell assembly, module integration, and final pack testing.

The plant is slated to start construction in early 2027 with an initial annual capacity of 10 GWh, enough to equip roughly 150,000 medium‑range electric vans or 30,000 heavy‑duty trucks. CATL will also set up a training centre to up‑skill Egyptian engineers, aiming to create a self‑sustaining supply chain for the rapidly growing North‑African EV market. The deal includes a five‑year supply guarantee of CATL‑produced cells, currently manufactured at its Jiangsu facility, ensuring consistent chemistry (NMC 811) and performance metrics such as 250 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy and 0.1 C fast‑charge capability.

Strategically, the agreement helps CATL diversify its production footprint beyond China and Europe, reducing exposure to geopolitical trade restrictions and shipping bottlenecks. For Egypt, the project aligns with the government’s “Green Mobility 2030” plan, which targets 1 million EVs on the road by 2030 and calls for at least 30 % of batteries to be sourced domestically. Analysts estimate the venture could create up to 2,000 jobs and attract ancillary industries such as recycling and raw‑material processing.

The collaboration also signals a broader trend of Chinese battery firms partnering with emerging‑market OEMs to accelerate EV adoption in regions that have lagged behind Europe and North America. If successful, the BME plant could become a hub for exporting packs to neighboring countries like Sudan, Libya, and Saudi Arabia, further cementing CATL’s position as the world’s largest battery supplier.

## 02 CATL Unveils New Megawatt‑Class Truck Battery Rated for 620 mi Range and 900,000 mi Lifetime
InsideEVs reported on 16 September that CATL has completed development of a next‑generation megawatt‑class battery designed specifically for heavy‑duty electric trucks. The new pack delivers a nominal 800 kWh of usable energy, enabling a real‑world range of up to 620 miles (≈ 1 000 km) on a single charge under typical haul‑cycle conditions. What sets the system apart is its “cell‑to‑pack” integration, which eliminates traditional module hardware and raises pack energy density to 260 Wh kg⁻¹, a 15 % improvement over CATL’s current flagship truck battery.

Durability is another headline feature: the battery is engineered to survive more than 900 000 miles (≈ 1.5 million km) of cumulative driving, equivalent to roughly 30 years of operation for a 200,000‑mile‑per‑year fleet. CATL achieved this by using a high‑nickel NMC 811 cathode paired with a silicon‑graphite composite anode, and by implementing an advanced thermal‑management system that keeps cell temperature within a 5 °C window during fast‑charge bursts of up to 1 MW. The pack can be recharged from 10 % to 80 % state‑of‑charge in under 15 minutes using compatible megawatt‑scale chargers.

The company has already secured pilot orders from two European logistics firms, which plan to integrate the battery into their upcoming Class 8 electric tractor units slated for 2028 delivery. CATL expects to begin low‑volume production in late 2027 at its Ningde facility, with a target annual output of 5 GWh by 2030. The move reinforces CATL’s ambition to dominate the long‑haul segment, where battery cost per kWh and cycle life are the primary cost drivers.

Industry observers note that the announced lifetime of 900 000 miles could dramatically lower total‑cost‑of‑ownership (TCO) for fleet operators, potentially making electric trucks price‑competitive with diesel even before accounting for fuel savings. If the performance claims hold up in field trials, the technology could also accelerate the rollout of electric freight corridors across Europe and North America.

## 03 Scania Rolls Out 720‑km Battery‑Electric Heavy‑Duty Truck with Modular Pack Architecture
CleanTechnica detailed on 15 September that Swedish truckmaker Scania has unveiled a new long‑haul battery‑electric model capable of traveling 720 km (≈ 474 miles) on a single charge. The vehicle, dubbed the Scania R‑Series BEV, incorporates a modular battery system that stacks three 250 kWh high‑energy‑density packs in the chassis, delivering a total usable capacity of 750 kWh. The packs use Scania’s in‑house developed NMC 622 cells, offering 240 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy and a 0.2 C fast‑charge rate that can add 300 km of range in 30 minutes when paired with 800 kW DC chargers.

To achieve the extended range, Scania extended the vehicle’s front overhang and re‑engineered the suspension to accommodate the additional battery modules without compromising payload. The truck’s regenerative braking system recovers up to 30 % of kinetic energy under typical European highway conditions, further extending range. The powertrain delivers 600 kW (≈ 800 hp) continuous output and 2 000 Nm of torque, matching the performance of Scania’s diesel counterparts.

Scania plans to launch the BEV in pilot fleets across Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden in early 2028, with a target of 5 % of its European sales volume being electric by 2030. The company also announced a partnership with Swedish charger operator Vattenfall to deploy a dedicated high‑power charging network along major freight corridors, ensuring that the 720‑km range can be topped up during short rest periods.

The announcement underscores a shift in the heavy‑truck market: manufacturers are moving from single‑pack prototypes to scalable, modular solutions that can be tailored to specific route requirements. By offering a 720‑km range, Scania aims to address the “range anxiety” that has limited electric truck adoption on longer haul routes, while the modular design simplifies maintenance and future upgrades.

Analysts predict that Scania’s approach could set a new benchmark for European OEMs, prompting rivals such as Volvo and Daimler to accelerate their own modular‑battery strategies. If the truck meets its projected efficiency—estimated at 1.2 kWh km⁻¹—fleet operators could see up to a 40 % reduction in operating costs compared with diesel, especially when combined with lower carbon‑pricing regimes expected in the EU.
