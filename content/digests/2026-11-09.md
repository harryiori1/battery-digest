## 01 BMW Confirms Use of Cheaper LFP Batteries in New EV Lineup
BMW has officially announced that its next‑generation electric models will be equipped with lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) cells instead of the higher‑energy‑density nickel‑cobalt‑aluminum (NCA) chemistry it has used for most of its premium EVs. The decision, revealed in a press briefing and confirmed by InsideEVs, applies first to the 2027 model year i4 sedan and the iX3 crossover, with production slated to begin at the Leipzig plant in early 2028.

LFP cells offer several advantages for a mass‑market premium brand. Their chemistry is inherently safer—less prone to thermal runaway—and they do not require cobalt, a material that has faced supply‑chain volatility and ethical scrutiny. Cost is the primary driver: industry analysts estimate a 15‑20 % reduction in battery pack price per kilowatt‑hour compared with NCA, translating into a potential €2,000‑€3,000 price advantage for the consumer.

The trade‑off is lower specific energy. BMW’s LFP packs are expected to deliver 70–80 kWh of usable capacity, with an energy density of roughly 250 Wh/kg, versus the 300 Wh/kg typical of its current NCA packs. To mitigate range loss, BMW will increase pack size where feasible and optimise vehicle aerodynamics. The i4 will retain a WLTP range of about 460 km (285 mi), while the iX3 will target 470 km (292 mi), figures that BMW says are “competitive within the segment.”

The move mirrors a broader industry shift. Tesla has been using LFP in its standard‑range Model 3 and Model Y for several years, and several Chinese OEMs have already adopted the chemistry for domestic models. By embracing LFP, BMW not only reduces exposure to cobalt price spikes but also aligns with EU policy goals that encourage the use of more abundant, lower‑impact materials.

The announcement also signals a strategic partnership with CATL, which will supply the LFP cells. CATL’s recent capacity expansion in Ningde is expected to add 30 GWh of LFP output by 2027, ensuring a stable supply for European automakers. BMW’s shift may accelerate LFP adoption across the premium segment, prompting rivals to reassess their own battery roadmaps.

## 02 Volkswagen China Acquires 5.3% Stake in Guoxuan High‑Tech, Boosting Domestic Cathode Supply
Volkswagen Group’s China subsidiary has completed the purchase of a 5.30 % equity stake in Guoxuan High‑Tech (Gotion High‑Tech Co., Ltd.), a leading Chinese cathode‑material manufacturer. The transaction, reported by D1EV, was executed through a cash injection of approximately €210 million and is part of VW’s broader “China‑First” strategy to secure critical battery‑material supply chains for its rapidly expanding EV portfolio in the country.

Guoxuan High‑Tech is best known for its lithium‑nickel‑cobalt‑aluminum (NCA) cathode production, a chemistry that underpins the high‑energy‑density packs used in many premium EVs, including VW’s own ID. series. By taking a minority stake, VW gains preferential access to Guoxuan’s planned 30 GWh of NCA cathode capacity slated for commissioning by 2029, as the company expands its Ningbo and Chengdu facilities.

The investment also dovetails with VW’s recent announcement to localise 70 % of the battery materials for its Chinese‑built models by 2030. Analysts view the move as a hedge against the tightening of raw‑material export controls and the volatility of cobalt and nickel markets. Securing a domestic source of NCA cathodes reduces logistics costs, shortens lead times, and aligns with Chinese government incentives that favour locally sourced battery components.

From a financial perspective, VW’s stake is expected to generate a modest equity return while providing strategic insight into Guoxuan’s R&D pipeline. Guoxuan has been piloting a next‑generation “high‑nickel, low‑cobalt” cathode that promises an energy density of 210 Wh/kg at the cell level, a figure that could push VW’s ID.7 and upcoming ID.8 models into the 600‑km (373‑mile) WLTP range bracket.

The deal also reflects the intensifying competition among global OEMs for Chinese battery‑material assets. While Tesla has secured long‑term supply contracts with several Chinese cathode makers, and BYD continues to integrate vertically, VW’s equity position gives it a seat at the table in technology development and capacity planning.

## 03 Geely’s Li Shufu Endorses Battery‑Swapping Model Amid Lifespan Mismatch Debate
Geely Chairman Li Shufu has publicly reiterated his support for the battery‑swapping model as a pragmatic solution to the “vehicle‑battery lifespan mismatch” that has begun to surface in China’s fast‑growing EV market. In an interview published by D1EV on November 9, Li argued that while a typical electric vehicle is designed for a 10‑year, 150,000‑km lifespan, the battery pack often degrades significantly earlier, creating a cost and convenience barrier for owners.

Geely’s position is backed by concrete rollout plans. The automaker, together with its subsidiary Zeekr, aims to install 200 battery‑swap stations across major Chinese cities by the end of 2027, up from the current 78 stations. Each station will be capable of swapping a 70 kWh pack in under three minutes, using a standardized interface that Geely has been promoting across its brand portfolio, including the Emgrand EV and the Lynk & Co models.

The strategic push aligns with recent policy guidance from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, which encourages “modular battery solutions” to improve EV affordability and accelerate adoption. Li highlighted that swapping can decouple the vehicle’s depreciation curve from that of the battery, allowing consumers to purchase a vehicle at a lower upfront price while paying a subscription fee for the battery’s energy and degradation management.

Geely is also leveraging its partnership with CATL, which supplies the high‑energy‑density ternary‑cell packs used in the swap‑compatible models. CATL has been developing a “plug‑and‑play” battery module that can be hot‑swapped without the need for complex cooling‑system disconnections, reducing station downtime and maintenance costs.

Industry observers note that Geely’s endorsement could intensify competition with Nio, which currently operates the world’s largest swap network (over 1,200 stations). However, Geely’s broader dealer network and lower‑cost vehicle positioning may attract a different customer segment, especially in second‑ and third‑tier cities where Nio’s premium brand has less penetration.

If successful, the model could reshape the Chinese EV market by shifting consumer expectations from “owning” a battery to “accessing” energy on demand, potentially influencing global OEM strategies as battery‑swap economics become clearer.
