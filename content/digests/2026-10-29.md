## 01 Volkswagen Expands EV Battery Recall to 85,000 Vehicles, Adding Audi Models
Volkswagen announced on 28 October that its ongoing electric‑vehicle battery recall now covers **85,000** units worldwide, up from the 71,000 cars recalled a month earlier. The expansion brings **13,000 Audi Q4 E‑Tron and Q4 Sportback** models into the program, making the recall the largest battery‑related safety action in the group’s history.

The root cause is a **software defect in the Battery Management System (BMS)** that can cause the high‑voltage pack to overheat under fast‑charging conditions. While no fires have been reported, the defect can accelerate cell degradation, potentially shortening the pack’s useful life. As a precaution, Volkswagen is instructing owners to **limit charging to 80 % of the pack’s capacity** until a software update is applied. The update, which can be installed via the OTA system, recalibrates the BMS to keep cell temperatures within safe limits during high‑current charging.

The recall affects **all model‑year 2022‑2024 Q4 E‑Tron variants** built at the Zwickau plant in Germany and the U.S. plant in Chattanooga. Volkswagen will cover the cost of the OTA update and, where necessary, a **hardware inspection of the BMS module**. Dealers are expected to start the service rollout in early November, with a target to complete all fixes by the end of Q1 2027.

Industry analysts see the recall as a **wake‑up call for the broader EV market**, where rapid‑charging adoption is outpacing the validation of BMS software across multiple OEMs. The incident underscores the importance of **robust OTA capabilities** and may accelerate the shift toward **cell‑to‑pack architectures** that reduce the number of control points. It also adds pressure on battery suppliers—primarily **LG Energy Solution, CATL, and Samsung SDI**—to tighten quality‑control protocols for high‑energy‑density cells used in European‑spec EVs.

The recall’s financial impact on Volkswagen is modest relative to its €250 billion 2026 revenue, but the reputational risk is significant. The company has pledged to **enhance its BMS testing regime** and to **share lessons learned** with industry groups such as the European Battery Alliance. The episode may also influence upcoming EU regulations on **mandatory BMS safety standards**, which are slated for adoption in 2028.

## 02 Xpeng Deploys Small‑Batch Solid‑State Cells for Its Iron Humanoid Robot
Chinese EV and robotics maker **Xpeng Motors** announced on 28 October that its **Iron humanoid robot** has entered small‑batch production powered by **solid‑state lithium‑metal batteries**. The move marks the first commercial deployment of solid‑state cells outside the automotive sector and highlights the technology’s suitability for **high‑energy‑density, low‑temperature‑drift applications**.

The solid‑state pack, developed in partnership with **SolidPower China**, delivers **450 Wh kg⁻¹** (approximately 30 % higher than Xpeng’s best LFP packs) and operates safely at temperatures ranging from **‑20 °C to 60 °C**. Each robot is equipped with a **12 kWh** solid‑state module that powers a **10‑hour continuous operation window** while supporting a **peak power output of 3 kW** for dynamic movements. The cells use a **sulfide‑based solid electrolyte**, eliminating the flammable liquid electrolyte that has traditionally limited battery safety in robotics.

Production is limited to **5,000 units** for the first year, primarily targeting **industrial logistics and warehouse automation** customers in China’s Tier‑1 cities. Xpeng expects to scale to **50,000 units by 2028** as the supply chain for solid‑state electrolytes matures. The company has secured a **¥1.2 billion** (≈ US$170 million) investment from the **China Battery Innovation Fund** to expand its solid‑state cell line.

Analysts view the rollout as a **proof‑of‑concept** that could accelerate solid‑state adoption in EVs, where the technology promises **500 Wh kg⁻¹** and **800 Wh L⁻¹** targets by the early 2030s. Xpeng’s decision to start with robotics sidesteps the high‑volume cost pressures of automotive production while allowing the firm to **collect real‑world performance data** on cycle life, thermal management, and safety.

The announcement also underscores a **strategic shift** for Chinese battery manufacturers, who are increasingly diversifying beyond traditional LFP and NCM chemistries. If Xpeng’s robots meet reliability targets, the solid‑state supply chain could see a **10‑fold increase in demand** within the next five years, prompting **cell‑to‑pack redesigns** across the industry.

## 03 EU Pushes Battery Recycling Capacity with the Circular Economy Act
On 29 October, a coalition of European industry groups—including **T&E, Recharge, Clean Tech for Europe, and the European Institute for Energy Solutions (EIES)**—issued a joint letter urging the European Commission to adopt **concrete measures** under the forthcoming **Circular Economy Act** to scale battery‑recycling capacity. The letter calls for **minimum recycling targets of 70 % material recovery by 2035** and for **public‑private financing mechanisms** to bring new recycling facilities online.

Europe currently recycles **≈ 30 %** of end‑of‑life lithium‑ion batteries, far below the **80 % target** set by the EU’s **Battery Directive revision**. The coalition highlights that **four new recycling plants** are under construction in Germany, France, and the Netherlands, but capacity will still fall short of the projected **1.5 TWh** of retired batteries expected by 2030. To bridge the gap, the letter proposes:

1. **Fast‑track permitting** for recycling projects that meet stringent environmental standards.  
2. **EU‑wide “Battery Recycling Credits”** similar to carbon credits, allowing manufacturers to offset material‑sourcing costs.  
3. **Mandatory design‑for‑recycling guidelines**, encouraging cell‑to‑pack architectures that simplify disassembly.  

Key industry players such as **Umicore, Northvolt, and Li-Cycle** have pledged to **invest €12 billion** in new capacity, but they argue that **regulatory certainty** is essential to secure financing. The coalition also warns that without a level playing field, **non‑EU manufacturers** could dominate the recycling market, undermining the EU’s strategic autonomy over critical minerals like **cobalt, nickel, and lithium**.

The Circular Economy Act, slated for adoption in early 2027, could become the **legal backbone** for these initiatives. If implemented, it would align with the EU’s **Fit‑for‑55** climate package by ensuring that **up to 80 % of battery raw materials** are sourced from recycled streams, reducing reliance on imports from the Democratic Republic of Congo and Indonesia.

Stakeholders view the letter as a **critical step** toward closing the loop on battery materials, a prerequisite for meeting the EU’s **30 million EV target** by 2030. The push for recycling also dovetails with the **European Battery Alliance’s** roadmap, which envisions a **fully circular battery value chain** by 2040.
