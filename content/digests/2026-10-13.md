## 01 BYD Unveils Luxury Han EV with 1,000 km Range and 150 kWh Blade LFP Pack  

BYD announced the launch of the “Great Han,” a luxury sedan that promises a CLTC‑rated range of more than 1,000 km (621 mi) while starting at roughly $35,000 in the Chinese market. The centerpiece of the vehicle is an upgraded version of BYD’s Blade lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) battery pack, now sized at 150 kWh and arranged in a 96‑cell series‑parallel architecture that retains the Blade’s thin‑profile safety advantages.  

The larger pack delivers a nominal energy density of 210 Wh/kg at the pack level, a modest increase over the previous 180 Wh/kg, thanks to a new cell chemistry that raises the nickel content while keeping the cobalt at under 2 %. Fast‑charging capability is also improved: a 15‑minute DC charge can push the pack from 10 % to 80 % state‑of‑charge, equivalent to roughly 800 km of range. BYD claims a 0‑100 km/h sprint in 3.8 seconds for the top‑spec “Performance” trim, which pairs the large pack with a dual‑motor 500 kW drivetrain.  

Why it matters: The Han’s price‑to‑range ratio undercuts most premium EVs in Europe and the U.S., where comparable range typically requires a 75‑kWh nickel‑cobalt‑manganese (NCM) pack priced well above $45,000. BYD’s move signals that LFP technology, once dismissed for lower energy density, can now compete on range when cell design and pack integration are optimized. The launch also reinforces BYD’s strategy of scaling Blade production to 200 GWh per year by 2028, a capacity that could reshape global supply chains for LFP cathode material and reduce reliance on nickel‑heavy chemistries.  

The Han will be sold through BYD’s existing dealer network in China beginning next month, with export plans to Southeast Asia and Europe slated for early 2027. Analysts at BloombergNEF project that the vehicle could capture up to 5 % of the premium sedan market in China by 2028, accelerating the shift toward safer, cheaper LFP packs in high‑end EVs.

## 02 U.S. Department of War Awards $75 M to Amprius for Drone‑Grade Silicon‑Anode Battery Production  

The U.S. Department of War announced a $75 million grant to Amprius Technologies to convert an existing electric‑vehicle (EV) cell line into a dedicated production line for high‑energy‑density batteries intended for military unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). The funding, part of the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency’s (DARPA) “High‑Performance Energy for Autonomous Systems” initiative, will be used to retrofit Amprius’s silicon‑nanowire anode technology from its current 300 Wh/kg lab benchmark to a production‑ready 350 Wh/kg target.  

Amprius plans to produce cylindrical 18650‑style cells with capacities of 5–10 Ah, optimized for the weight‑critical demands of long‑endurance drones. The grant also covers the installation of a pilot‑scale dry‑room and a high‑precision electrode coating line capable of handling the delicate silicon anode without compromising cycle life. The company projects a first‑article‑inspection (FAI) run by Q2 2027, with an initial annual output of 10 GWh—enough to power a fleet of 1,200 medium‑size UAVs at 2 kWh per aircraft per mission.  

Strategically, the award underscores a shift in U.S. defense procurement toward battery‑only power solutions, reducing reliance on legacy fuel‑based logistics. Silicon‑anode cells promise a 30 % weight reduction over conventional graphite anodes, directly translating into longer flight times or higher payload capacity for drones used in reconnaissance, electronic warfare, and supply delivery.  

The partnership also has commercial implications. Amprius has hinted that the same production line could later be leveraged for civilian high‑energy‑density applications such as electric aviation and long‑range EVs, potentially lowering the cost barrier for silicon‑anode batteries that have historically been expensive to scale. If successful, the program could accelerate the broader industry’s transition from graphite‑based to silicon‑enhanced chemistries, a move projected by IDTechEx to add 150 GWh of silicon‑anode capacity globally by 2030.

## 03 Home‑Battery Market Diversifies: Six Viable Alternatives to the Tesla Powerwall  

While Tesla’s Powerwall still dominates headlines, a new wave of residential storage solutions is gaining traction in 2026, offering higher capacities, lower cost per kilowatt‑hour, and tighter integration with smart‑home ecosystems. A recent Electrek roundup highlighted six competitors that together account for roughly 35 % of the U.S. residential battery market, which is now valued at $12 billion after three consecutive years of double‑digit growth.  

1. **LG Chem RESU 13** – 13 kWh capacity, 98 % round‑trip efficiency, priced at $7,800 (≈ $600/kWh). The latest version adds a built‑in inverter, eliminating the need for a separate power‑wall inverter.  
2. **Sonnen eco 10** – Modular 10 kWh system with integrated AI that optimizes charge‑discharge based on weather forecasts; cost $9,200.  
3. **Enphase IQ Battery 3** – 3.4 kWh micro‑inverter‑compatible units that can be stacked up to 10.2 kWh; $4,500 for a three‑unit stack.  
4. **Blue Planet Energy Blue Ion 2.0** – 12 kWh lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) pack with a 20‑year warranty; $8,500.  
5. **EVE Energy PowerCube 8** – 8 kWh LFP system with a 95 % depth‑of‑discharge (DoD) and a price tag of $5,600.  
6. **Pika Energy Pika‑Home 15** – 15 kWh LFP pack, the highest capacity in the list, priced at $10,800, and includes a built‑in grid‑interactive controller for demand‑response programs.  

Key market trends driving this diversification include:  

* **Cost compression** – Average residential battery prices fell 30 % YoY, from $1,200/kWh in 2023 to $840/kWh in 2026, largely due to economies of scale in LFP production and the maturation of second‑life EV pack repurposing.  
* **Policy incentives** – Federal tax credits of up to 30 % for systems installed before 2027, combined with state‑level time‑of‑use rebates, have spurred adoption in regions with high electricity rates, especially California and the Northeast.  
* **Grid services** – Utilities are increasingly enrolling home batteries in frequency‑regulation and peak‑shaving programs, providing owners with an additional revenue stream of $0.02–$0.04 /kWh.  

The shift away from a single‑vendor market is expected to intensify as manufacturers introduce hybrid‑storage solutions that combine LFP cells with flow‑battery modules for longer discharge durations. For homeowners, the expanding choice means better alignment of storage size, price, and smart‑home functionality with individual energy goals.
