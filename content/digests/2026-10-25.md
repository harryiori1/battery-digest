## 01 Finnish Startup Donut Lab Demonstrates EV Battery Survives -30°C Test
Donut Lab, a Finnish battery‑technology startup, announced that its newly developed lithium‑ion cell passed an extreme‑cold performance test at –30 °C, a temperature that typically cripples conventional EV batteries. The test, conducted by an independent laboratory, showed that the cell retained 92 % of its nominal capacity after a 30‑minute discharge cycle, and could be charged to 80 % state‑of‑charge in under 15 minutes using a standard 350 kW charger—figures that rival or exceed current mainstream chemistries.

The breakthrough stems from a proprietary electrolyte formulation that remains fluid at sub‑zero temperatures, combined with a high‑nickel NMC cathode engineered for reduced lithium plating. Donut Lab claims the chemistry also offers a 15 % improvement in energy density (up to 250 Wh/kg) and a 20 % reduction in degradation over 1,000 cycles compared with baseline NMC cells.

Why it matters: Cold‑climate markets such as Scandinavia, Canada, and parts of the U.S. Midwest have long struggled with reduced EV range and longer charging times in winter. A battery that can maintain performance without costly thermal‑management systems could lower vehicle cost, improve consumer confidence, and accelerate EV adoption in these regions. Moreover, the technology may be retrofitted into existing production lines, easing supply‑chain disruptions.

Donut Lab plans a pilot production run with a European OEM later this year, targeting a 2028 commercial launch. If the performance scales, the startup could also attract interest from heavy‑duty and aviation sectors that demand reliable operation in harsh environments.

## 02 Battery Show North America 2026 Highlights: Solid‑State, LFP Scale‑Up, and Supply‑Chain Shifts
The Battery Show North America, held October 12‑14 in Detroit, offered a panoramic view of where the global battery industry is heading in 2027 and beyond. Unlike previous editions that spotlighted speculative breakthroughs, this year’s exhibitors focused on scaling proven technologies and addressing supply‑chain bottlenecks.

Key takeaways:

* **Solid‑state cells move toward volume production.** ProLogium (Taiwan) and QuantumScape (U.S.) displayed pilot lines capable of delivering 5 GWh of solid‑state packs by 2028, targeting premium EVs with 400 km range and 10‑minute charge times. Both firms emphasized the use of sulfide electrolytes that promise higher safety margins and energy density above 350 Wh/kg.

* **Lithium‑iron‑phosphate (LFP) capacity expands.** CATL and BYD unveiled new 800 Ah LFP pouch cells, leveraging a high‑voltage (3.8 V) chemistry that pushes specific energy to 210 Wh/kg while retaining the low‑cost, cobalt‑free advantage of LFP. Production lines in China and Europe are slated to add a combined 120 GWh of LFP capacity by 2029.

* **Supply‑chain resilience.** Several raw‑material processors, including Glencore’s nickel‑sulfate plant in Indonesia and Umicore’s cobalt‑recycling hub in Belgium, announced capacity increases of 30 % and 25 % respectively. The announcements reflect a concerted effort to decouple battery production from geopolitical volatility.

* **Manufacturing automation.** Companies such as Tesla’s Gigafactory and Samsung SDI demonstrated AI‑driven cell‑stacking robots that can reduce labor costs by up to 15 % and improve yield consistency to 99.8 %.

The overarching narrative was clear: the industry is transitioning from “lab‑first” to “factory‑first,” with a focus on cost‑effective, high‑volume production of both next‑gen solid‑state and mature LFP chemistries. This shift is expected to drive down pack prices below $100/kWh by 2030, unlocking broader EV adoption and enabling large‑scale stationary storage.

## 03 GM Unveils Fourth‑Gen EV Platform Cutting Battery Pack Size by 20%
At its “GM Forward” event on October 23, General Motors revealed details of its fourth‑generation electric‑vehicle platform, dubbed “Ultium Next‑Gen.” The architecture integrates a new low‑mass chassis, 30 % fewer wiring harnesses, and a redesigned battery module that reduces overall pack volume by roughly 20 % without sacrificing range.

The core of the platform is a prismatic LMR (Lithium‑Metal‑Redox) cell that packs 250 Wh/kg—about 15 % higher than the current LFP‑based Ultium cells. By stacking cells vertically and eliminating redundant cooling channels, GM claims a 10 % weight reduction in the pack itself. Combined with the wiring and structural efficiencies, the total vehicle weight drops by an estimated 150 kg for a midsize SUV, translating to a 5‑7 % improvement in EPA‑rated range.

GM also announced a partnership with SK On to secure a steady supply of high‑nickel cathode material, mitigating the cobalt‑dependency that has plagued earlier generations. The new platform will debut in the 2027 Chevrolet Silverado EV and Cadillac Lyriq refresh, with an expected launch price 8 % lower than the 2025 models.

Industry analysts view the move as a strategic response to intensifying competition from Chinese manufacturers who are already delivering sub‑$30/kWh packs. By shrinking the battery footprint, GM can offer more interior space, lower vehicle cost, and faster assembly times—critical factors for scaling EV production in its Tennessee and Ohio plants.

If the projected cost and weight savings materialize, GM could improve its EV margin by up to 3 percentage points, a significant boost given the thin profitability of current electric models.
