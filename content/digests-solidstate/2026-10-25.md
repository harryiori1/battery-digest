## 01 Blue Solutions Announces GEN4 Solid‑State Battery Development Update

Blue Solutions, the French subsidiary of Saft that specializes in solid‑state lithium‑polymer (LMP) technology, released a detailed progress report on its fourth‑generation (GEN4) battery platform. The update, posted on the company’s website, outlines a series of technical milestones that bring the technology closer to commercial viability for automotive and stationary‑storage markets.

The GEN4 cells now achieve an energy density of **480 Wh kg⁻¹** at the cell level, up from 420 Wh kg⁻¹ in the previous GEN3 version. This gain stems from a new high‑voltage cathode formulation (LiNi₀.₈Mn₀.₁Co₀.₁O₂) paired with a **thinner, ceramic‑based solid electrolyte** that maintains ionic conductivity above 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹ at room temperature. In addition, the company reports a **charge‑time reduction to 15 minutes** for an 80 % state‑of‑charge, thanks to an optimized interface between the solid electrolyte and the lithium‑metal anode that mitigates dendrite formation.

Safety improvements are also highlighted. The solid‑state architecture eliminates the flammable liquid electrolyte, and the new ceramic separator can withstand temperatures up to **350 °C** before mechanical failure, a significant increase over the 200 °C limit of conventional lithium‑ion cells. Blue Solutions claims that the GEN4 design passes **UN 38.3** safety tests with a 30 % safety margin.

Why does this matter for solid‑state commercialization? First, the energy‑density figure approaches the 500 Wh kg⁻¹ target that many automakers have set for next‑generation EVs, suggesting that solid‑state cells could soon compete on range without sacrificing safety. Second, the fast‑charge capability addresses one of the primary consumer concerns that have slowed adoption of earlier solid‑state prototypes. Finally, the demonstrated thermal robustness opens doors for high‑temperature applications such as **grid‑scale storage in hot climates**.

Blue Solutions plans a pilot production line in **Toulouse** by mid‑2027, targeting an annual output of **5 GWh**. The company is already in talks with several European OEMs for a joint‑development program that could see the first GEN4‑based EVs on the road by 2029. If the roadmap holds, the GEN4 platform could become a benchmark for the industry, pressuring rivals such as QuantumScape and Solid Power to accelerate their own development cycles.

## 02 ProLogium and Elysian Aircraft Sign MoU to Test All‑Solid‑State Cells for Aviation

Taiwan‑based ProLogium, a leading developer of all‑solid‑state lithium‑metal batteries, announced a **memorandum of understanding (MoU)** with Dutch aerospace startup **Elysian Aircraft BV**. The agreement, published on ProLogium’s corporate site, sets out a collaborative program to qualify ProLogium’s solid‑state cells for use in **electric aircraft propulsion and auxiliary power units (APUs)**.

The MoU focuses on three technical objectives: (1) **demonstrating cycle life of at least 1,000 full‑charge cycles** at a 70 % depth‑of‑discharge under aviation‑grade temperature swings (‑20 °C to +50 °C); (2) achieving a **specific power of 1,200 W kg⁻¹** to meet the thrust‑to‑weight ratios required for short‑range commuter aircraft; and (3) validating **safety compliance with DO‑160 and CS‑23 standards** for aircraft battery systems. ProLogium will supply its latest **sulfide‑based solid electrolyte** (Li₁₀GeP₂S₁₂, or LGPS) cells, which have demonstrated **ionic conductivities of 12 mS cm⁻¹** and a **C‑rate capability up to 5 C** in laboratory tests.

Elysian Aircraft plans to integrate the cells into a **prototype 150‑kW electric motor** that will power a **six‑seat, short‑haul aircraft** slated for a maiden flight in 2028. The partnership also includes a joint‑funded **test‑bed facility** at Elysian’s Rotterdam campus, where both parties will conduct accelerated aging, vibration, and thermal runaway simulations.

The significance of this collaboration lies in its potential to **bridge the gap between solid‑state battery performance and aviation certification requirements**. While solid‑state cells have long been touted for their safety and energy density, the aviation sector has been cautious due to stringent reliability standards. By targeting a **specific energy of 350 Wh kg⁻¹** at the pack level—well above the 250 Wh kg⁻¹ typical of current lithium‑ion aircraft batteries—ProLogium aims to reduce aircraft weight and increase payload capacity, directly translating into lower operating costs and reduced emissions.

If successful, the ProLogium‑Elysian program could serve as a **template for future aerospace‑battery collaborations**, encouraging other manufacturers to consider solid‑state chemistries for both **regional air mobility (RAM)** and **urban air taxi** concepts. Moreover, the data generated from the certification‑focused testing will likely feed back into ProLogium’s broader commercial roadmap, accelerating the rollout of its solid‑state cells for **ground‑based EVs and grid storage**.
