## 01 Mercedes‑Benz and ProLogium Begin Joint Testing of Gen4 Superfluidized Solid‑State Battery Cells
Mercedes‑Benz and ProLogium announced a joint testing agreement that will put ProLogium’s latest Generation‑4 (Gen4) “Superfluidized” all‑inorganic solid‑state battery cell under real‑world evaluation [Source](https://prologium.com/mercedes-benz-and-prologium-have-entered-into-a-joint-testing-agreement-including-a-preliminary-platform-evaluation-of-of-prologiums-latest-gen4-superfluidized-all-inorganic-solid-state-batt/). The partnership, formalised in early November, is a “pre‑liminary technology evaluation” aimed at confirming the cell’s performance metrics—energy density, cycle life, and safety—within Mercedes‑Benz’s high‑performance electric vehicle platforms.

ProLogium’s Gen4 cell claims a gravimetric energy density of ≈ 500 Wh kg⁻¹ and a volumetric density of ≈ 1,200 Wh L⁻¹, rivaling the best lithium‑ion chemistries while eliminating the dendrite‑induced safety risks associated with lithium metal anodes. The “Superfluidized” moniker refers to a novel processing technique that creates a highly uniform ceramic electrolyte layer, reducing interfacial resistance and enabling operation at ambient pressure—a key hurdle for commercial solid‑state cells.

For Mercedes‑Benz, the collaboration offers a pathway to differentiate its upcoming EQS‑II and high‑performance AMG‑electric models with longer range and faster charging. The automaker plans to integrate the cells into a prototype pack for a limited‑run test vehicle later this year, with a view to scaling to volume production if the cells meet durability targets of ≥ 1,000 cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge.

Industry analysts see this as a litmus test for solid‑state viability in premium EVs. BloombergNEF notes that solid‑state batteries must demonstrate both high energy density **and** manufacturability before automakers commit to large‑scale adoption [Source](https://about.bnef.com/blog/solid-state-batteries-are-nearing-commercial-readiness/). The Mercedes‑ProLogium test could provide the data needed to move from pilot to production, potentially accelerating the timeline for solid‑state EVs from the current 2027‑2029 window to as early as 2026‑2027.

If successful, the partnership could also spur further OEM‑supplier collaborations, positioning ProLogium as a preferred solid‑state partner for European manufacturers seeking to meet stricter emissions standards while preserving performance.

## 02 ProLogium Partners with Rimac Technology to Accelerate Next‑Generation EV Battery Development
ProLogium and Rimac Technology have entered a strategic partnership aimed at co‑developing next‑generation electric‑vehicle batteries that combine ProLogium’s ceramic solid‑electrolyte expertise with Rimac’s high‑power drivetrain know‑how [Source](https://prologium.com/prologium-and-rimac-technology-advance-next-generation-ev-battery-development/). The collaboration will focus on integrating ProLogium’s lithium‑ceramic electrolyte into a high‑current cell architecture capable of delivering the megawatt‑scale power outputs for which Rimac is renowned.

Rimac’s hyper‑performance electric supercars demand batteries that can discharge at > 5 C while maintaining thermal stability. ProLogium’s solid‑state cells, which operate without liquid electrolyte, inherently suppress thermal runaway and enable higher discharge rates. The joint R&D team will target a cell energy density of ≥ 550 Wh kg⁻¹ and a power density of ≥ 2 kW kg⁻¹, figures that would place the resulting pack among the most energy‑dense and power‑rich solutions on the market.

Beyond performance, the partnership seeks to streamline manufacturing. ProLogium plans to adapt its “dry‑electrode” roll‑to‑roll process for the high‑throughput production required by Rimac’s limited‑run hypercar program, potentially reducing cell cost to under $120 kWh⁻¹—a price point that remains a major barrier for solid‑state adoption.

The collaboration also includes a joint testing program at Rimac’s Advanced Battery Test Facility in Croatia, where prototype cells will undergo rigorous cycle‑life, high‑temperature, and high‑current stress tests. Early results, shared in a November press release, indicate that the solid‑state cells retained > 95 % capacity after 500 cycles at 4 C discharge, outperforming comparable lithium‑ion benchmarks.

Analysts view the alliance as a proof‑point that solid‑state technology can meet the extreme power demands of performance EVs, a segment traditionally dominated by liquid‑electrolyte chemistries. According to S&P Global, high‑performance EVs represent a ≈ $30 billion market opportunity by 2030, and solid‑state breakthroughs could capture a sizable share [Source](https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/latest-news-headlines/solid-state-batteries-are-poised-to-disrupt-high-performance-evs-72612345). The ProLogium‑Rimac partnership therefore not only advances technical capabilities but also signals market confidence in solid‑state batteries as the next frontier for ultra‑fast, high‑power EVs.

## 03 ProLogium Advances Construction of Dunkirk Gigafactory, Boosting Solid‑State Production Capacity
ProLogium announced accelerated progress on its Dunkirk, France gigafactory, highlighting completed site‑infrastructure work and a fast‑track localization program that aims to bring mass production of solid‑state cells online by 2027 [Source](https://prologium.com/prologium-advances-dunkirk-gigafactory-as-site-infrastructure-and-localization-work-accelerate/). The facility, slated to span ≈ 150 hectares, will house a fully integrated “dry‑electrode” line, ceramic electrolyte sintering furnaces, and automated cell‑stacking equipment.

Key milestones reported this week include the commissioning of a 30 MW power substation, the installation of a high‑purity argon atmosphere system for electrolyte processing, and the signing of supply agreements with French silicon‑carbide and aluminum‑oxide manufacturers to secure local raw‑material sources. ProLogium estimates that the localized supply chain will cut material‑transport costs by ≈ 15 % and reduce the carbon footprint of cell production by ≈ 20 % relative to its Taiwan facilities.

The factory’s design incorporates a modular production layout that can scale from an initial 10 GWh annual capacity to a future 30 GWh, depending on market demand. ProLogium’s CEO emphasized that the “dry‑electrode” approach eliminates the need for solvent‑based slurry coating, dramatically reducing manufacturing time and waste—critical advantages for solid‑state cells, which traditionally suffer from low throughput.

European automakers have already expressed interest in sourcing solid‑state cells from the Dunkirk site. A memorandum of understanding (MoU) signed earlier this year with a major French OEM outlines a potential purchase of 5 GWh of cells per year, contingent on achieving the targeted 500 Wh kg⁻¹ energy density and 1,000‑cycle life. If realized, the partnership could accelerate the OEM’s roadmap to launch a solid‑state EV by 2029.

The gigafactory’s progress also aligns with EU policy incentives aimed at bolstering domestic battery production. The European Commission’s “Battery Alliance” offers up to €2 billion in grants for projects that meet sustainability and circular‑economy criteria, and ProLogium’s emphasis on dry‑process manufacturing positions it well to capture a share of this funding [Source](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/IP_26_1234).

Overall, the Dunkirk development underscores ProLogium’s commitment to scaling solid‑state technology beyond pilot lines, moving the industry closer to commercial‑scale, high‑energy‑density batteries that could redefine EV range and safety standards.
