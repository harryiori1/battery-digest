## 01 Dodge Charger EV Prototype Features Experimental Semi-Solid-State Battery
Dodge's electric Charger may be a flop, but its battery tech looks serious. According to a recent report, the Dodge Charger EV prototype is featuring an experimental semi-solid-state battery. This development is significant as it marks one of the first times a semi-solid-state battery is being tested in a real-world setting. The use of semi-solid-state batteries in electric vehicles could potentially lead to improved range, faster charging, and lower costs. As the automotive industry continues to shift towards electric vehicles, advancements in battery technology will play a crucial role in determining the success of these vehicles. 

The Dodge Charger EV prototype is a test mule for Stellantis, the parent company of Dodge, to explore the viability of semi-solid-state batteries. The prototype is being used to test the performance and durability of the battery technology in real-world conditions. While the Charger EV itself may not be a commercial success, the development of semi-solid-state battery technology could have far-reaching implications for the industry. 

As reported by [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/798496/dodge-charger-daytona-ev-solid-state-battery/), the Dodge Charger EV prototype is a significant step forward in the development of semi-solid-state batteries. The success of this technology could pave the way for wider adoption in the automotive industry, leading to improved performance, efficiency, and sustainability.
