## 01 ProLogium Begins Mass Production of All‑Solid‑State Cells with 381 Wh/kg Energy Density

ProLogium announced that it has entered full‑scale mass production of its latest all‑solid‑state lithium‑metal battery, achieving a gravimetric energy density of 381 Wh kg⁻¹ and a volumetric density of 903 Wh L⁻¹ 【18】. The cells use ProLogium’s proprietary “Superfluidized” inorganic solid electrolyte, a sulfide‑based glass‑ceramic that enables high ionic conductivity (>10 mS cm⁻¹) while maintaining mechanical stability against lithium dendrite penetration. Production is being carried out at the company’s newly commissioned line in Taiwan, with an initial annual capacity of 5 GWh.

Why it matters: Energy density has been the primary barrier preventing solid‑state batteries from displacing conventional lithium‑ion packs in high‑range electric vehicles. At 381 Wh kg⁻¹, ProLogium’s cells surpass the best commercial lithium‑ion chemistries (≈260 Wh kg⁻¹) and approach the theoretical limits of lithium‑metal anodes. The high volumetric density also makes the technology attractive for aerospace applications where space is at a premium.

The company highlighted several performance metrics that reinforce commercial viability: a cycle life of over 1,000 full‑charge cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge, a charge rate of 1 C (full charge in one hour) without significant temperature rise, and a safety test showing no thermal runaway even when subjected to a 1.5 kW short‑circuit for 10 seconds. ProLogium plans to ship the first batches to automotive partners in early 2027, with a roadmap to scale capacity to 30 GWh by 2030.

The announcement follows a wave of solid‑state milestones in 2026, including Blue Solutions’ GEN4 architecture and several European pilots. ProLogium’s move from pilot to volume production signals that the technology is crossing the “valley of death” and could accelerate the transition to lithium‑metal‑based EVs, potentially cutting vehicle weight by 15‑20 % and extending range by 200 km on a single charge. Industry analysts estimate that a successful mass‑production rollout could add $2‑3 billion in revenue for ProLogium within five years, while also attracting further OEM commitments.

*Sources: ProLogium press release on mass production*【18】.

## 02 ProLogium Accelerates Construction of the Dunkirk Gigafactory

ProLogium disclosed rapid progress on its planned gigafactory in Dunkirk, northern France, reporting that site infrastructure and localization efforts are now on schedule for a 2027 start‑up 【15】. The 200‑hectare campus will house cell‑assembly lines, electrolyte synthesis facilities, and a dedicated R&D hub focused on next‑generation solid electrolytes. The company has secured a €150 million subsidy from the French government and a €80 million loan from the European Investment Bank, earmarked for clean‑energy manufacturing and workforce training.

Key to the project’s speed is the “localization” strategy: ProLogium is partnering with French chemical firms to source raw materials for its sulfide‑based solid electrolyte, reducing reliance on Asian supply chains and cutting logistics costs by an estimated 12 %. In addition, a joint venture with a regional university will create a “solid‑state battery academy” to train 1,500 engineers and technicians over the next three years.

The factory is designed for a modular production line capable of scaling from 5 GWh to 30 GWh per annum without major retooling. ProLogium says the plant will employ up to 3,000 staff, with a focus on automation and AI‑driven quality control to maintain the tight tolerances required for solid‑electrolyte thin‑film deposition.

Strategic implications are significant. Europe’s automotive sector, led by manufacturers such as Renault and Stellantis, has pledged to electrify 70 % of its fleet by 2030, but faces a shortage of high‑energy‑density cells. A local solid‑state supply could alleviate that bottleneck and position Europe as a leader in next‑gen battery tech. Moreover, the Dunkirk site aligns with the EU’s “Fit for 55” climate goals by promising a carbon‑neutral manufacturing footprint, powered by on‑site wind and solar farms.

If the timeline holds, ProLogium could begin delivering its 381 Wh kg⁻¹ cells to European OEMs by late 2027, giving the company a first‑mover advantage in a market that is still dominated by conventional lithium‑ion suppliers.

*Sources: ProLogium announcement on Dunkirk gigafactory*【15】.

## 03 ProLogium to List on Nasdaq via Merger with Translational Development Acquisition Corp.

ProLogium disclosed that it will become a publicly traded company on the Nasdaq through a business combination with Translational Development Acquisition Corp. (TDAC), a special‑purpose acquisition company (SPAC) 【21】. The deal includes a $50 million primary capital infusion from TDAC, which will be earmarked for expanding ProLogium’s production capacity, accelerating electrolyte R&D, and funding the Dunkirk gigafactory build‑out.

The merger values ProLogium at roughly $1.2 billion on a cash‑free, debt‑free basis. Upon closing, the combined entity will trade under the ticker “PLGM”. ProLogium’s CEO highlighted that the public listing will provide “greater financial flexibility and visibility” to secure long‑term contracts with automotive OEMs and aerospace partners.

From a market perspective, the listing is notable because it marks one of the few pure solid‑state battery companies to achieve a Nasdaq debut, following QuantumScape’s 2023 listing and Solid Power’s 2024 SPAC merger. Analysts at BloombergNEF project that solid‑state batteries could capture 10‑15 % of the global EV battery market by 2035, and a publicly listed ProLogium could tap a broader investor base to fund the required scale‑up.

The capital raise will also support ProLogium’s “dry‑electrode” manufacturing pilot, a process that eliminates the slurry‑coating step and promises a 30 % reduction in production cost per kWh. If successful, this could address one of the lingering cost challenges that have kept solid‑state batteries from achieving price parity with conventional lithium‑ion packs.

Regulatory approval for the SPAC merger is expected by the end of Q4 2026, with trading slated for early 2027. The move underscores the growing confidence of capital markets in solid‑state technology and could trigger further financing for other players in the sector.

*Sources: ProLogium Nasdaq listing announcement*【21】.
