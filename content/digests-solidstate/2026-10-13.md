## 01 ProLogium partners with Rimac Technology to develop next‑generation solid‑state EV batteries
ProLogium Technology Co., a Taiwanese leader in all‑inorganic solid‑state batteries, announced a strategic partnership with Rimac Technology, the high‑performance electric‑drivetrain arm of Croatia’s hyper‑car maker Rimac Group. The joint effort focuses on co‑developing a next‑generation solid‑state cell that combines ProLogium’s lithium‑ceramic electrolyte platform with Rimac’s expertise in high‑power electrode engineering and vehicle integration.

The partnership targets a cell architecture that can deliver **energy densities above 400 Wh/kg**—a significant leap from the 300‑350 Wh/kg range typical of today’s commercial solid‑state offerings. ProLogium plans to leverage its patented “DCN” (Dual‑Crystal‑Network) sulfide‑based electrolyte, which promises high ionic conductivity (>10 mS cm⁻¹) and excellent mechanical stability, while Rimac will contribute advanced lithium‑metal anode designs and high‑current cathode formulations. Early prototype testing is slated for Q4 2026, with a pilot‑line production target for 2028.

Why this matters: achieving >400 Wh/kg would bring solid‑state batteries into a regime where they can comfortably replace conventional lithium‑ion packs in long‑range electric vehicles without sacrificing weight or volume. Higher energy density also translates to lower pack cost per kilowatt‑hour, a key barrier to mass adoption. Moreover, the collaboration blends ProLogium’s scale‑up experience—evidenced by its Dunkirk gigafactory construction—with Rimac’s performance‑focused engineering, potentially shortening the typical 5‑7‑year commercialization timeline for breakthrough chemistries.

The deal also includes a joint R&D fund of **US$30 million**, split evenly between the two firms, to accelerate electrolyte‑anode interface optimization and to qualify the cells under automotive standards (ISO 26262, IEC 62660). If successful, the first vehicle demonstrator could appear on a future Rimac hyper‑car platform as early as 2029, providing a high‑visibility showcase for the technology.

Industry observers see this as a “best‑of‑both‑worlds” move: ProLogium gains a high‑profile automotive partner that can validate performance at the system level, while Rimac secures a supply of solid‑state cells that could differentiate its future models from competitors still reliant on liquid‑electrolyte lithium‑ion packs. The partnership also signals growing confidence among traditional EV players that solid‑state chemistry is moving from laboratory to production line.

For now, the collaboration remains at the prototype stage, but the combined expertise and funding suggest a realistic path toward commercial volume by the early 2030s. If the energy‑density target is met, it could reshape battery economics, enable lighter EVs, and accelerate the broader transition to solid‑state powertrains across the automotive sector.
