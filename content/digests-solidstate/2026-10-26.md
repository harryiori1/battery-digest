## 01 Mercedes‑Benz and ProLogium Sign Joint Testing Agreement for Gen4 Superfluidized All‑Inorganic Solid‑State Cells
Mercedes‑Benz and ProLogium have entered a joint testing agreement that will evaluate ProLogium’s latest Gen4 Superfluidized All‑Inorganic Solid‑State Battery Cell Technology. The collaboration, announced on 26 October 2026, focuses on a preliminary technology evaluation that includes performance benchmarking, safety testing, and integration studies for future electric‑vehicle platforms [ProLogium](https://prologium.com/mercedes-benz-and-prologium-have-entered-into-a-joint-testing-agreement-including-a-preliminary-platform-evaluation-of-of-prologiums-latest-gen4-superfluidized-all-inorganic-solid-state-batt/).

The Gen4 cell architecture combines a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte with a lithium‑metal anode, promising energy densities above 400 Wh kg⁻¹ and a cycle life exceeding 1,000 full cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge. ProLogium claims the superfluidized process yields a uniform electrolyte‑interface that mitigates dendrite formation—a key barrier to commercial lithium‑metal solid‑state batteries. Mercedes‑Benz plans to run the cells in a prototype power‑train test rig later this year, with a view to a pilot‑scale production partnership by 2029.

Why it matters: The agreement represents one of the few high‑profile OEM‑supplier collaborations that place a solid‑state cell directly into an automotive validation pipeline. If the Gen4 cells meet Mercedes‑Benz’s durability and thermal‑management criteria, the partnership could shave 10‑15 % off vehicle weight and extend range by roughly 80 km compared with current lithium‑ion packs. Moreover, the joint testing framework sets a precedent for shared risk‑sharing and data transparency, which could accelerate industry‑wide adoption of solid‑state technology.

The broader implication is a signal to the market that major carmakers are willing to commit resources to solid‑state solutions despite remaining manufacturing challenges. Success would likely spur further investment from other OEMs and could tighten the competitive gap between traditional lithium‑ion suppliers and emerging solid‑state specialists.

## 02 Kyushu Electric Power Boosts Strategic Investment in ProLogium to Co‑Develop Next‑Generation Solid‑State Batteries
Kyushu Electric Power announced a strategic investment in ProLogium, deepening a collaboration that aims to accelerate the development of next‑generation solid‑state batteries for grid‑scale storage and electric‑vehicle applications [ProLogium](https://prologium.com/kyushu-electric-power-advances-strategic-investment-in-prologium-deepening-collaboration-in-next-generation-batteries-and-energy-sector/).

The partnership will focus on scaling ProLogium’s sulfide‑based solid electrolyte platform, which offers high ionic conductivity (>10 mS cm⁻¹) and compatibility with lithium‑metal anodes. Kyushu Electric Power will provide both capital and access to its research facilities in Fukuoka, enabling joint work on electrolyte synthesis, interface engineering, and pilot‑line production. The collaboration also includes a joint roadmap to achieve 400 Wh kg⁻¹ cell energy density and a target of 2,000‑cycle life at 25 °C.

Key numbers: The investment is reported to be ¥12 billion (≈ $78 million) over the next three years, with an initial tranche earmarked for a 10 m² pilot coating line capable of producing 5 g of electrolyte per hour. ProLogium expects to demonstrate a 5‑cell stack delivering 1 MWh of usable energy by 2028, a scale that would be sufficient for medium‑size micro‑grid deployments.

Why it matters: By aligning a utility‑scale power producer with a solid‑state pioneer, the deal bridges the gap between laboratory breakthroughs and real‑world energy‑storage needs. If successful, the technology could enable safer, higher‑energy storage for renewable integration, reducing reliance on liquid‑electrolyte lithium‑ion systems that pose fire risks at high energy densities.

The collaboration also underscores a growing trend of utilities investing directly in battery innovation to secure supply chains and tailor chemistries to their grid requirements. A successful outcome could position Kyushu Electric Power as a showcase customer for solid‑state storage, encouraging other utilities worldwide to follow suit.

## 03 ProLogium Secures $50 Million Primary Capital from TDAC to Finance SPAC Merger and Production Ramp‑Up
ProLogium and Translational Development Acquisition Corp. (TDAC) announced a $50 million primary capital commitment to support ProLogium’s proposed business combination and to fund the next phase of its solid‑state battery commercialization [ProLogium](https://prologium.com/prologium-and-translational-development-acquisition-corp-announce-50-million-committed-primary-capital-investment-to-support-proposed-business-combination/).

The capital infusion will be allocated to three core initiatives: (1) construction of a new pilot production line in Taiwan capable of delivering 200 MWh of solid‑state cells per year, (2) scaling up the proprietary “Superfluidized” sulfide electrolyte process to achieve a target throughput of 15 g min⁻¹, and (3) expanding the R&D team focused on lithium‑metal anode stabilization and high‑voltage cathode integration.

ProLogium projects that the pilot line will enable the launch of a 400 Wh kg⁻¹ cell series for premium electric‑vehicle applications by early 2029, with a projected cost of $120 kWh⁻¹—approximately a 30 % reduction from its current prototype price. The company also plans to begin limited‑volume supply to select OEM partners, including the Mercedes‑Benz test program described above.

Why it matters: Securing dedicated primary capital through a SPAC route provides ProLogium with the financial runway to move from small‑scale demonstration to commercial‑scale manufacturing, a transition that has stalled many solid‑state startups. The funding also signals confidence from the investment community in the commercial viability of ProLogium’s technology stack.

If the pilot line meets its performance targets, ProLogium could become the first solid‑state supplier to ship cells at a volume sufficient for niche premium EVs, potentially reshaping the competitive landscape against incumbent lithium‑ion manufacturers. The successful SPAC merger would also give the company a public market platform to raise further capital for full‑scale gigafactory construction, accelerating the timeline for solid‑state batteries to enter mainstream automotive production.
