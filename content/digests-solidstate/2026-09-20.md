## 01 TEDA Forum Highlights Solid-State Batteries as Emerging Competitive Arena
The 2026 TEDA Forum in Tianjin placed solid‑state batteries at the centre of its agenda, underscoring the technology’s transition from laboratory curiosity to a strategic competitive frontier.  Speakers—including senior executives from ProLogium, QuantumScape, and China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology—argued that the convergence of high‑energy‑density lithium‑metal anodes, sulfide‑based solid electrolytes, and dry‑electrode manufacturing is poised to overturn the conventional lithium‑ion market share hierarchy.

Why the buzz now?  Recent breakthroughs in garnet‑type oxides (e.g., LLZO) and argyrodite sulfides have pushed ionic conductivities above 10 mS cm⁻¹, while advances in interfacial engineering have reduced the notorious lithium‑dendrite formation risk that once plagued lithium‑metal anodes.  In parallel, dry‑electrode roll‑to‑roll processes—demonstrated at pilot scale by Factorial Energy and Ilika—promise to cut cell‑stacking costs by up to 30 % compared with slurry‑based wet‑coating, a key lever for commercial viability.

The forum highlighted three concrete metrics that signal commercial readiness:

1. **Energy density:** Several prototype cells now exceed 400 Wh kg⁻¹ gravimetric and 800 Wh L⁻¹ volumetric values, a 30‑40 % jump over the best current lithium‑ion packs.
2. **Cycle life:** New interfacial coatings (e.g., Li₃PO₄ and LiNbO₃) have enabled >1,000 full cycles with <10 % capacity fade at 0.5 C rates.
3. **Cost trajectory:** Dry‑electrode manufacturing combined with high‑throughput ceramic electrolyte sintering is projected to bring solid‑state pack costs below $120 kWh⁻¹ by 2028, according to a joint industry‑government study presented at the forum.

Policy implications were also on the table.  Chinese regulators announced a “Solid‑State Battery Innovation Fund” of ¥5 billion (~$700 million) to accelerate scale‑up, while the European Commission signaled intent to align its Battery Alliance funding with solid‑state milestones.  These moves suggest that solid‑state technology will soon be a focal point of both supply‑chain security and emissions‑reduction strategies.

The consensus among the panelists was clear: solid‑state batteries are no longer a niche research topic but a decisive factor in the next wave of electric‑vehicle and grid‑storage deployments.  The TEDA Forum’s emphasis on cross‑border collaboration, standard‑setting, and joint‑venture financing signals that the industry is coalescing around a shared roadmap—one that could see the first mass‑produced solid‑state EV battery roll off an assembly line as early as 2027.
