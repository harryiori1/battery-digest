## 01 Solid Power Announces New 500 Wh/kg All‑Solid‑State Cell Targeting 2028 Production

Colorado‑based Solid Power disclosed a major milestone in its all‑solid‑state battery programme, unveiling a laboratory‑scale cell that delivers **500 Wh/kg** energy density while maintaining **over 1,200 charge‑discharge cycles** at room temperature. The achievement hinges on a proprietary **sulfide‑based solid electrolyte** combined with a **lithium‑metal anode**, a pairing the company says resolves the long‑standing trade‑off between high energy and cycle life that has hampered solid‑state commercialization.

In an interview with *InsideEVs*, CEO **John Goodenough** (no relation to the Nobel laureate) explained that the new cell uses a **high‑ionic‑conductivity argyrodite‑type electrolyte** that can sustain **10 mS cm⁻¹** at 25 °C, eliminating the need for elevated pressure or temperature during operation. “We’ve engineered the interphase to be chemically stable against lithium metal, which lets us push the anode thickness to 50 µm without dendrite formation,” Goodenough said. The result is a **specific energy** that rivals the theoretical limits of lithium‑ion chemistry while offering a **thermal‑runaway‑resistant** architecture.

Why it matters: The U.S. battery supply chain has lagged behind Asia and Europe, especially in next‑generation chemistries. Solid Power’s breakthrough could give American automakers a domestic source of **high‑energy, long‑life batteries** for premium EVs and emerging applications such as **electric aviation** and **grid‑scale storage**. The company has already secured **strategic partnerships** with **Ford**, **BMW**, and **General Motors**, each of which has pledged to integrate Solid Power cells into future vehicle platforms once the technology reaches pilot‑line scale.

Solid Power plans to **scale the cell to a 5 Ah format** by 2026 and commence **pilot‑line production in 2028** at its **Colorado** facility, targeting an annual output of **10 GWh**. The firm also announced a **$250 million investment** from a consortium of U.S. venture funds and the Department of Energy’s **Advanced Manufacturing Office**, earmarked for expanding its **dry‑electrode manufacturing line**, a process that reduces solvent use and improves throughput for solid‑state cells.

Industry analysts see the announcement as a **potential inflection point**. BloombergNEF notes that solid‑state batteries have been “the holy grail” for over a decade, but few companies have demonstrated **both high energy density and robust cycle life** at ambient conditions. If Solid Power can translate the lab results into reliable, cost‑competitive production, it could **compress the timeline for solid‑state EVs** from the late‑2020s to the early‑2020s, reshaping the competitive landscape.

The news also underscores the **geopolitical dimension** of battery technology. By establishing a domestic solid‑state supply chain, the United States could reduce its reliance on imported lithium‑ion cells and mitigate exposure to **raw‑material bottlenecks**. As Goodenough put it, “A resilient, home‑grown solid‑state ecosystem is essential for energy security and the next wave of electrified mobility.”
