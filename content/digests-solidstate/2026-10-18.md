## 01 Mercedes‑Benz and ProLogium Sign Joint Testing Agreement for Gen4 Superfluidized All‑Inorganic Solid‑State Cells

Mercedes‑Benz and Taiwan‑based ProLogium have entered a joint testing agreement that will evaluate ProLogium’s fourth‑generation (Gen4) Superfluidized All‑Inorganic Solid‑State Battery Cell technology in a real‑world automotive environment. The collaboration, announced on 18 October 2026, includes a preliminary technology assessment, safety testing, and performance benchmarking on a prototype electric vehicle platform that Mercedes‑Benz plans to use for its next‑generation luxury models.

ProLogium’s Gen4 cells are built around a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte that is “super‑fluidized” during manufacturing, a process that eliminates voids and grain‑boundary resistance, delivering an unprecedented combination of high ionic conductivity (≈10 mS cm⁻¹) and mechanical robustness. According to the company, the cells achieve an energy density of 420 Wh kg⁻¹ and a volumetric density of 1 200 Wh L⁻¹—figures that rival or exceed the best lithium‑metal solid‑state prototypes shown in academic labs. The solid‑state design also promises intrinsic safety benefits: no flammable liquid electrolyte, higher thermal stability, and resistance to dendrite penetration even under high‑current charging.

For Mercedes‑Benz, the partnership is a strategic step toward meeting its 2030 target of offering at least one fully solid‑state‑powered model in the S‑Class lineup. The automaker’s engineering team will integrate the Gen4 cells into a test‑bed vehicle, subjecting them to accelerated cycle life tests (up to 1 500 full cycles), high‑rate charge/discharge (up to 5 C), and extreme temperature exposure (‑30 °C to +60 °C). Results will inform whether the technology can satisfy the brand’s performance, range, and safety standards.

The agreement also includes a joint intellectual‑property framework that allows both parties to co‑develop cell packaging, thermal‑management modules, and battery‑management‑system (BMS) algorithms tailored to solid‑state chemistry. If successful, the collaboration could shorten the typical 5‑year commercialization timeline for solid‑state batteries, moving them from pilot‑line to low‑volume production by the mid‑2020s.

Industry observers note that a major OEM‑level validation is a critical milestone for any solid‑state contender. “Automakers have been cautious because solid‑state cells have historically struggled with scale‑up and reliability,” says Dr. Elena Martínez, senior analyst at BloombergNEF. “A formal testing agreement with Mercedes‑Benz signals confidence in ProLogium’s manufacturing approach and could accelerate investor interest across the sector.” The partnership also puts ProLogium in direct competition with other solid‑state leaders such as QuantumScape and Solid Power, which are pursuing similar OEM collaborations.

The full announcement can be read on ProLogium’s website [ProLogium Press Release](https://prologium.com/mercedes-benz-and-prologium-have-entered-into-a-joint-testing-agreement-including-a-preliminary-platform-evaluation-of-of-prologiums-latest-gen4-superfluidized-all-inorganic-solid-state-batt/).

## 02 Kyushu Electric Power Boosts Strategic Investment in ProLogium to Accelerate Next‑Generation Solid‑State Battery Production

Kyushu Electric Power Co., Ltd., one of Japan’s leading regional utilities, announced a deepened strategic investment in ProLogium Technology Co., Ltd. on 18 October 2026. The utility will increase its equity stake by an additional ¥12 billion (≈ US$78 million) and commit to a multi‑year collaboration focused on scaling up ProLogium’s high‑energy solid‑state battery platform for grid‑storage and automotive applications.

The new capital injection is earmarked for three core initiatives: (1) expansion of ProLogium’s pilot production line in Taoyuan, Taiwan, to achieve a monthly output of 5 GWh of solid‑state cells; (2) joint R&D on next‑generation sulfide‑based electrolytes that target ionic conductivities above 12 mS cm⁻¹ and improved moisture tolerance, a known challenge for sulfide chemistries; and (3) development of a “solid‑state‑grid‑module” that pairs high‑power solid‑state cells with advanced BMS software to provide fast‑response ancillary services for the Japanese power grid.

Kyushu Electric Power’s CEO, Hiroshi Tanaka, highlighted the strategic fit: “Japan’s energy transition demands batteries that combine high energy density with intrinsic safety. ProLogium’s all‑inorganic solid‑state technology aligns with our goal to deploy large‑scale, long‑life storage that can operate safely in residential and industrial settings.” The utility also plans to pilot a 1 MWh solid‑state storage system at its Kagoshima substation by 2028, which would be the first commercial deployment of ProLogium’s cells in a utility environment.

From a commercialization perspective, the partnership addresses two persistent bottlenecks for solid‑state batteries: manufacturing throughput and cost. By leveraging Kyushu’s procurement power and engineering expertise, ProLogium aims to reduce the cell‑level cost from the current ¥1 200 Wh⁻¹ to below ¥800 Wh⁻¹, a price point that could make solid‑state cells competitive with high‑nickel NMC lithium‑ion packs for both EVs and stationary storage.

Analysts see the move as a vote of confidence in ProLogium’s “Superfluidized” process, which claims to eliminate the need for costly post‑mortem densification steps that have hampered other solid‑state manufacturers. “If Kyushu can help ProLogium achieve true volume production, it could reshape the supply chain for next‑generation batteries in Asia,” notes Takashi Yamada of Mitsubishi UFJ Research & Consulting.

The detailed press release is available on ProLogium’s corporate site [Kyushu Investment Announcement](https://prologium.com/kyushu-electric-power-advances-strategic-investment-in-prologium-deepening-collaboration-in-next-generation-batteries-and-energy-sector/).
