## 01 Kennesaw State Researcher Develops Safer, Faster Solid-State Battery Design
A researcher at Kennesaw State University has developed a new design for solid-state batteries that promises to improve safety and reduce charging times. According to the researcher, the new design uses a solid electrolyte that replaces the traditional liquid electrolyte found in most batteries, which can be prone to overheating and explosions. The solid electrolyte is made from a combination of materials that provide improved ionic conductivity and stability, allowing for faster charging and discharging of the battery. This development has significant implications for the commercialization of solid-state batteries, as it addresses one of the major challenges facing the technology: safety. With this new design, solid-state batteries could become a more viable option for a wide range of applications, from electric vehicles to consumer electronics. As [Kennesaw State University](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilwFBVV95cUxOZmllaGgwdTVKOGdBSEVmbjVhb0RtcHRJVjJFa2E0RWp4bFVBeTFHX3l0WDYxVXpESjlkNUh3aU1KRHRvU2ZhY0RkeUdsVmhlZDNsd25SQVRaMDFhZGRreWlHbVdyMTVXS3NCNU5ydFlXa2xpUHRuY1ZRQUJ2dThreEFVcE01VnpUWmJpeTZhZTlIRXBoLXpv) reports, this breakthrough could pave the way for the widespread adoption of solid-state batteries.
