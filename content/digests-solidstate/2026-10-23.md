## 01 ProLogium Accelerates Dunkirk Gigafactory Construction for Mass‑Production of Solid‑State Batteries
ProLogium Technology Co., Ltd. announced that the infrastructure phase of its new Dunkirk, France gigafactory is entering an accelerated schedule, with site‑level construction and supply‑chain localization now on track to begin cell‑production pilot lines by early 2027 [ProLogium](https://prologium.com/prologium-advances-dunkirk-gigafactory-as-site-infrastructure-and-localization-work-accelerate/). The facility, slated to occupy 120 ha, will be the first European‑based plant dedicated to the company’s patented lithium‑ceramic solid‑state electrolyte (SSE) technology, which replaces conventional liquid electrolytes with a dense, sulfide‑based ceramic that enables lithium‑metal anodes.

Key milestones disclosed include the completion of the plant’s power‑grid interconnection and the installation of a 30 MW on‑site renewable‑energy hub, intended to meet the high‑temperature sintering requirements of the ceramic electrolyte. ProLogium also confirmed that a local supply chain for high‑purity lithium carbonate and alumina‑based ceramic precursors is being established, reducing raw‑material lead times from six months to under two months. The company projects an initial annual output of 5 GWh of solid‑state cells, enough to power roughly 250,000 electric vehicles equipped with 200 Wh kg⁻¹ energy‑density packs.

Why this matters: solid‑state batteries promise a combination of higher energy density, faster charging, and intrinsic safety that could overcome the performance ceiling of current lithium‑ion chemistries. ProLogium’s ceramic SSE has demonstrated laboratory gravimetric energy densities of 350 Wh kg⁻¹ and cycle lives exceeding 1,000 full‑charge cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge [ProLogium]. Scaling these metrics to commercial volumes has been the industry’s biggest hurdle. By securing a European production base, ProLogium not only shortens the logistics chain for OEMs targeting the EU market but also signals confidence that its manufacturing processes—particularly the high‑temperature hot‑pressing of thin‑film electrolytes—are ready for volume scaling.

Implications for the market are immediate. Automakers that have pledged solid‑state roadmaps, such as Volkswagen and BMW, now have a nearer‑term source of cells that meet EU safety standards. Moreover, the localized supply chain could lower the projected cost of solid‑state packs from $250 kWh⁻¹ today to under $150 kWh⁻¹ by 2030, a price point that would make the technology competitive with high‑nickel NMC cathodes. If ProLogium meets its 2027 pilot‑line target, the Dunkirk gigafactory could become a benchmark for other solid‑state players—QuantumScape, Solid Power, and Ilika—who are still awaiting clear pathways to mass production. The move also underscores Europe’s growing role as a hub for next‑generation battery manufacturing, potentially reshaping global supply dynamics away from Asia‑centric models.
