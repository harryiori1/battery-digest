## 01 ProLogium Unveils “Third State” DCN Electrolyte at Paris Motor Show
ProLogium Technology Co., Ltd., a leading developer of all‑solid‑state batteries, announced the launch of its “Third State” DCN (Dual‑Conductivity Network) electrolyte at the 2026 Paris Motor Show. The new electrolyte is described as a hybrid solid‑state medium that simultaneously conducts lithium ions and electrons, a departure from traditional single‑ion‑conducting solid electrolytes such as sulfide, oxide, or polymer ceramics [ProLogium Press Release](https://prologium.com/prologium-unveils-dcn-opening-a-third-state-electrolyte-universe-at-paris-motor-show/).

The DCN architecture integrates a nanostructured ceramic framework with an embedded conductive polymer matrix. Laboratory tests reported an ionic conductivity of 1.2 mS cm⁻¹ at 25 °C—comparable to liquid electrolytes—while maintaining an electronic conductivity of 0.03 mS cm⁻¹, sufficient to enable self‑healing of micro‑cracks and to reduce interfacial resistance. In prototype pouch cells built with a lithium‑metal anode and a high‑voltage cathode (LiNi₀.₈Mn₀.₁Co₀.₁O₂), the DCN electrolyte delivered a specific energy of 420 Wh kg⁻¹ and retained 92 % capacity after 1,500 cycles at 0.5 C, surpassing the performance of ProLogium’s earlier sulfide‑based cells.

Why the “third state” matters is twofold. First, the simultaneous ionic and electronic pathways simplify cell architecture by eliminating the need for a separate current collector on the electrolyte side, potentially reducing stack thickness and cost. Second, the hybrid nature mitigates the mechanical brittleness that has plagued ceramic electrolytes, improving manufacturability on a roll‑to‑roll scale—a critical hurdle for commercial solid‑state batteries.

Industry analysts see the DCN electrolyte as a bridge between fully solid‑state designs and the more mature lithium‑ion ecosystem. By offering liquid‑like conductivity without sacrificing safety, the technology could accelerate the adoption of lithium‑metal anodes in high‑energy applications such as electric‑aircraft, long‑range EVs, and grid‑scale storage. ProLogium plans to begin pilot‑line production of DCN‑based cells in 2028, targeting a 2029 volume‑manufacturing start for automotive partners.

If the performance claims hold up in larger‑scale testing, the DCN electrolyte could reshape the solid‑state roadmap, shifting the focus from purely ionic ceramics to multifunctional hybrid media. That would not only improve energy density and cycle life but also lower the entry barrier for manufacturers transitioning from conventional lithium‑ion to solid‑state platforms. The coming years will reveal whether the “third state” can deliver on its promise of safer, higher‑energy batteries at a price point competitive with today’s lithium‑ion chemistries.
