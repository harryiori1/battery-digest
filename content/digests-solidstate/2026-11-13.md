## 01 Solid Power CEO Says Solid‑State Cells Could Restore U.S. Battery Competitiveness

Colorado‑based Solid Power announced that its next‑generation all‑solid‑state cells are on track to reach commercial production by 2029, a timeline the company believes could revive U.S. leadership in battery technology. Speaking at a press briefing, CEO John Goodenough highlighted three key milestones: achieving an energy density of 500 Wh kg⁻¹, demonstrating 1,000‑plus charge‑discharge cycles at 1 atm pressure, and scaling a pilot line capable of producing 10 GWh per year by 2028.  

The announcement follows a series of technical breakthroughs over the past two years, including the successful integration of a sulfide‑based electrolyte that delivers ionic conductivities above 10 mS cm⁻¹ while maintaining stability against lithium metal anodes. Solid Power’s proprietary “dual‑layer” architecture—combining a thin ceramic separator with a polymer interlayer—has reportedly reduced interfacial resistance to under 20 Ω cm², a figure that rivals the best laboratory results reported in recent literature.  

Why it matters: solid‑state batteries promise higher energy density, improved safety (no flammable liquid electrolyte), and longer cycle life, all of which are critical for next‑generation electric vehicles (EVs) and grid‑scale storage. If Solid Power can meet its 2029 target, U.S. automakers could source domestic solid‑state cells, reducing reliance on Asian supply chains that currently dominate lithium‑ion production. The company also claims its technology is compatible with existing lithium‑metal anode manufacturing processes, potentially easing the transition for current battery factories.  

The roadmap has attracted interest from several Tier‑1 OEMs and defense contractors, with preliminary supply agreements reportedly worth $200 million in total. Moreover, Solid Power’s partnership with a major U.S. research university aims to co‑develop a dry‑electrode manufacturing line, which could cut production costs by up to 30 % compared with conventional slurry‑coated processes.  

Industry analysts see the timeline as ambitious but plausible, noting that Solid Power’s recent pilot‑scale cell yields of 85 % and its ability to operate cells at room temperature address two of the most persistent hurdles for solid‑state commercialization. If successful, the move could reposition the United States as a competitive player in the global battery market, potentially spurring further investment in domestic solid‑state supply chains.
