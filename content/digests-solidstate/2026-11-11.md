## 01 QuantumScape Unveils Osprey All‑Solid‑State Cell with 1,000 Cycles at 1 atm
QuantumScape announced that its next‑generation “Osprey” platform has achieved a milestone that could accelerate the commercial rollout of solid‑state batteries for electric vehicles. The company says the Osprey cell, which replaces the liquid electrolyte in the cathode with a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte, has demonstrated 1,000 full charge‑discharge cycles while retaining 80 % of its initial capacity—all under a modest 1 atm of pressure [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/11/quantumscape-osprey-all-solid-state-sulfide-battery/).

The breakthrough addresses two long‑standing hurdles for sulfide solid electrolytes: the need for heavy mechanical clamping to keep the brittle solid electrolyte in intimate contact with the electrodes, and rapid capacity fade under repeated cycling. By engineering a composite cathode that integrates the sulfide electrolyte directly into the active material matrix, QuantumScape claims it can maintain a stable interfacial contact without external pressure. The result is a cell that can be assembled in a conventional roll‑to‑roll format, potentially simplifying manufacturing lines that are already geared for lithium‑ion production.

Performance figures released with the announcement are noteworthy. The Osprey cell delivers an energy density of roughly 350 Wh kg⁻¹ (based on active material mass) and a power capability of 1 kW kg⁻¹, positioning it competitively against the best commercial lithium‑ion chemistries while offering the safety advantages of a non‑flammable solid electrolyte. The 1,000‑cycle benchmark translates to an estimated 300,000 km of driving for a typical EV, assuming a 400 km range per charge and a 75 % depth‑of‑discharge operating window.

Industry analysts see the 1 atm operation as a game‑changer because it eliminates the need for expensive high‑pressure cell housings that have plagued earlier solid‑state prototypes. “If QuantumScape can scale this chemistry while keeping costs comparable to current lithium‑ion packs, it could close the cost‑performance gap that has kept solid‑state batteries in the research phase for years,” notes battery analyst Maya Patel of BloombergNEF.

QuantumScape plans to begin pilot‑line production of Osprey cells later this year, with an eye toward a limited‑volume EV launch in 2028. The company is also partnering with several OEMs to integrate the cells into next‑generation vehicle platforms that require higher energy density and improved thermal stability. While the path to mass production still involves scaling the dry‑room processing and securing a reliable supply chain for high‑purity sulfide powders, the Osprey results suggest that solid‑state technology is moving from the lab toward the showroom floor faster than many had expected.
