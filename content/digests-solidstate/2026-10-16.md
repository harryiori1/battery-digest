## 01 ProLogium Unveils DCN, Opening a “Third State” Electrolyte Universe at Paris Motor Show
ProLogium used its Paris showcase to announce a breakthrough in solid‑electrolyte chemistry: a new “DCN” (Dual‑Conductivity Network) material that the company describes as a “third state” between conventional sulfide and oxide electrolytes. According to the press release, DCN combines the high ionic conductivity of sulfides (≈10 mS cm⁻¹ at room temperature) with the chemical stability of oxides, while also offering a wider electrochemical window (up to 5 V vs. Li/Li⁺) and reduced moisture sensitivity [ProLogium – DCN announcement](https://prologium.com/prologium-unveils-dcn-opening-a-third-state-electrolyte-universe-at-paris-motor-show/).

The material is synthesized via a scalable low‑temperature sintering route that avoids the high‑energy ball‑milling steps typical of sulfide electrolytes. ProLogium claims pilot‑scale cells built with DCN achieve an energy density of 420 Wh kg⁻¹ and a volumetric density of 1,050 Wh L⁻¹, surpassing its earlier all‑solid‑state offerings. Cycle life testing shows >1,000 cycles at 80 % depth‑of‑discharge with less than 5 % capacity fade, a figure that rivals the best lithium‑ion chemistries while retaining the safety advantages of a solid‑state design.

Why it matters: The solid‑state market has been hamstrung by a trade‑off between conductivity and stability. Sulfide electrolytes excel in conductivity but react with moisture and high‑voltage cathodes; oxides are stable but often require high‑temperature processing and deliver lower conductivity. DCN’s hybrid approach could reduce the engineering compromises that have slowed commercial roll‑outs, making it easier for automakers to adopt solid‑state packs without redesigning battery management systems.

Implications for commercialization are immediate. ProLogium says the DCN platform is compatible with its existing dry‑electrode manufacturing line, meaning the company can scale production without major capital investment. If the performance claims hold in larger formats, the technology could enable EVs with 600 km range on a single charge while eliminating the fire risk associated with liquid electrolytes. The announcement also positions ProLogium as a potential supplier for high‑energy aerospace and defense applications, where weight and safety are paramount.

Industry observers will be watching the upcoming validation tests with major OEM partners slated for early 2027. Should DCN meet its targets, it could accelerate the timeline for solid‑state batteries moving from niche prototypes to mainstream vehicle platforms within the next five years.

## 02 ProLogium and Elysian Aircraft BV Sign MoU to Explore Solid‑State Batteries for Zero‑Emission Aviation
ProLogium and Dutch aerospace startup Elysian Aircraft BV have signed a memorandum of understanding to investigate the integration of ProLogium’s high‑energy solid‑state cells into electric aircraft propulsion systems [ProLogium – Elysian MoU](https://prologium.com/prologium-and-elysian-aircraft-bv-sign-mou%ef%bc%9aexploring-next-generation-battery-applications-in-aerospace-to-advance-the-vision-of-zero-emission-aviation-and-future-mobility/). The MoU outlines a joint research program that will focus on adapting ProLogium’s latest sulfide‑based solid electrolyte (the “DCN” material unveiled at the Paris Motor Show) for the high‑power, high‑energy demands of short‑haul electric aircraft.

Elysian’s current prototype, the “E‑1” electric commuter, targets a 300‑km range with a payload of up to four passengers. The company estimates that replacing its existing lithium‑ion pack with ProLogium’s solid‑state cells could increase specific energy by 30 % (from ~250 Wh kg⁻¹ to >330 Wh kg⁻¹) and reduce battery weight by roughly 200 kg, directly translating into longer range or higher payload capacity. Moreover, the inherent safety of a solid electrolyte—no flammable liquid, higher thermal stability—addresses one of the biggest regulatory hurdles for aviation‑grade batteries.

The collaboration will begin with a feasibility study, including thermal‑runaway testing, vibration endurance, and integration of ProLogium’s dry‑electrode manufacturing process into Elysian’s cell‑assembly line. Both parties aim to produce a flight‑ready demonstrator by the end of 2028, aligning with the European Union’s “Fit for 55” targets that call for a 55 % reduction in aviation CO₂ emissions by 2030.

From a market perspective, the partnership signals growing confidence that solid‑state technology can meet the stringent power‑density and safety standards of aerospace, a sector that has traditionally lagged behind automotive in battery adoption. If successful, the technology could open a new revenue stream for ProLogium beyond EVs, positioning it as a key supplier for emerging electric‑air‑mobility (EAM) ecosystems.

The MoU also underscores a broader industry trend: battery innovators are increasingly seeking cross‑sector collaborations to accelerate validation and certification. By leveraging Elysian’s aircraft design expertise and ProLogium’s electrolyte breakthroughs, the partnership could set a template for future solid‑state applications in drones, regional airliners, and even vertical‑takeoff‑and‑landing (VTOL) platforms.
