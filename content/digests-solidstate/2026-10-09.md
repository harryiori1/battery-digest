## 01 Kyushu Electric Power invests in ProLogium to accelerate solid‑state battery rollout

Kyushu Electric Power Co., a major Japanese utility, announced a strategic equity investment in ProLogium Technology Co., the Taiwanese leader in all‑inorganic solid‑state batteries.  The deal, disclosed on 8 October 2026, deepens a partnership that already includes joint R&D on high‑energy‑density sulfide‑free electrolytes and lithium‑metal anodes.  While the exact stake was not disclosed, the press release emphasizes that the funding will be earmarked for scaling ProLogium’s “Gen4” solid‑state cell platform, which the company claims can reach > 380 Wh kg⁻¹ and operate safely at temperatures up to + 150 °C [Kyushu Electric Power – ProLogium](https://prologium.com/kyushu-electric-power-advances-strategic-investment-in-prologium-deepening-collaboration-in-next-generation-batteries-and-energy-sector/).

The investment is significant for several reasons. First, it provides ProLogium with a reliable source of capital to expand its pilot line in Taiwan and to begin construction of a larger production facility in Europe, a step that has been cited as a bottleneck for solid‑state commercialization. Second, Kyushu Electric Power brings a deep understanding of grid‑scale energy storage, opening a pathway for solid‑state batteries to be deployed in stationary applications where safety and long cycle life are paramount. Finally, the partnership aligns with Japan’s national roadmap to achieve mass‑produced solid‑state batteries by 2030, a target that has been challenged by cost and manufacturing yield issues.

Industry observers note that utility‑backed financing could help bridge the “valley of death” that many solid‑state startups face after prototype validation.  If ProLogium can translate its laboratory performance into a reproducible manufacturing process, the combined expertise of a utility and a battery specialist may accelerate the transition from niche aerospace and premium EV pilots to broader automotive and grid markets.  The next milestones to watch are the announced 2027 pilot‑scale production run and the first joint demonstration of a 10 kWh solid‑state storage module for grid‑balancing services.

## 02 ProLogium appoints Rimac CEO Antony Sheriff to board, boosting commercialization expertise

ProLogium announced on 9 October 2026 that Antony Sheriff, the chief executive officer of Rimac Automobili, has been appointed to its board of directors.  Sheriff’s tenure at Rimac, a Croatian hyper‑car manufacturer renowned for its high‑performance electric powertrains and advanced battery packs, is expected to bring valuable commercialization and systems‑integration experience to ProLogium’s solid‑state program [ProLogium – Board Appointment](https://prologium.com/prologium-appoints-antony-sheriff-ceo-of-rimac-group-to-board-of-directors%ef%bc%9aappointment-intended-to-strengthen-commercialization-scaling-and-cross-border-governance-capabilities-as-prologium-prepares-for-its-next-stage-of-global-growth/).

The appointment comes at a critical juncture as ProLogium prepares to transition from pilot‑line production to volume manufacturing.  Sheriff’s background in scaling high‑energy‑density lithium‑ion packs for super‑car applications, as well as his involvement in the development of ultra‑lightweight battery enclosures, aligns with ProLogium’s goal of delivering solid‑state cells that can meet the weight and power‑density demands of premium EVs.  In a brief statement, Sheriff highlighted the “unmatched safety profile and energy density of ProLogium’s all‑inorganic solid‑state chemistry” and expressed confidence that the partnership could help bring the technology to market within the next three years.

Analysts view the board addition as a signal that ProLogium is positioning itself for strategic OEM collaborations, especially with manufacturers seeking a competitive edge in the high‑end EV segment.  Sheriff’s network in the European automotive ecosystem could facilitate joint‑development projects, similar to the earlier Mercedes‑Benz testing agreement, but with a stronger focus on series‑production readiness.  The move also underscores a broader industry trend: solid‑state battery firms are recruiting executives with proven track records in high‑performance EVs to accelerate technology transfer from the lab to the road.

Key upcoming events include a planned demonstration of a 500 Wh solid‑state pack in a Rimac Concept Two prototype slated for early 2027, and a series of joint engineering workshops aimed at integrating ProLogium’s garnet‑type ceramic electrolyte with Rimac’s high‑current power electronics.

## 03 ProLogium secures $50 million primary capital from TDAC for SPAC merger, funding solid‑state expansion

On 10 October 2026 ProLogium disclosed that Translational Development Acquisition Corp. (TDAC) has committed $50 million of primary capital to support a proposed business combination that would list ProLogium on the Nasdaq.  The capital raise is intended to fund the scaling of ProLogium’s solid‑state battery production, including the construction of a 200 MWh annual capacity line in Europe and the expansion of its dry‑electrode manufacturing capability [ProLogium – TDAC Investment](https://prologium.com/prologium-and-translational-development-acquisition-corp-announce-50-million-committed-primary-capital-investment-to-support-proposed-business-combination/).

The financing marks the largest single infusion of public‑market capital into a solid‑state battery company to date.  ProLogium plans to allocate the funds across three core areas: (1) capital equipment for high‑throughput ceramic electrolyte sintering, (2) development of a roll‑to‑roll dry‑electrode process that eliminates the need for solvent‑based slurry coating, and (3) a battery‑pack engineering team focused on integrating the cells into automotive platforms.  By leveraging the dry‑electrode approach, ProLogium aims to cut manufacturing costs by up to 30 % compared with conventional slurry‑coated lithium‑ion cells, a cost metric that has long been a barrier to solid‑state adoption.

The SPAC route also provides ProLogium with a public‑market valuation that can be used as currency for future strategic partnerships, potentially with OEMs or other battery innovators.  Market watchers note that the $50 million injection, combined with the earlier Kyushu Electric Power investment, positions ProLogium with roughly $150 million in near‑term funding—a level that could sustain the company through the critical “scale‑up” phase that many solid‑state startups fail to survive.

If the merger proceeds as planned, ProLogium expects to complete its Nasdaq listing by Q2 2027, coinciding with the first commercial shipment of its Gen4 solid‑state cells to a European premium‑EV manufacturer.  The successful execution of this capital plan could set a precedent for other solid‑state firms seeking public‑market financing, and may accelerate the overall timeline for solid‑state batteries to achieve mass‑market penetration.
