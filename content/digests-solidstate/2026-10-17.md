## 01 Blue Solutions Shows 70% More Autonomy with GEN4 Lithium‑Metal Solid‑State Cells
French battery specialist Blue Solutions announced that its fourth‑generation (GEN4) lithium‑metal solid‑state cells have delivered a **70 % increase in vehicle autonomy** compared with its earlier solid‑state offerings. The company demonstrated the performance gain on a prototype electric bus equipped with the new cells, reporting a range of **approximately 350 km** on a single charge—up from roughly 205 km with the previous generation.

The breakthrough stems from a combination of higher energy density cathodes, a **sulfide‑based solid electrolyte** with improved ionic conductivity (≈ 10 mS cm⁻¹ at room temperature), and a **dry‑electrode manufacturing process** that eliminates the need for solvent‑based slurry casting. By integrating a **lithium‑metal anode** directly onto the solid electrolyte, Blue Solutions has pushed the gravimetric energy density of its cells to **≈ 420 Wh kg⁻¹** and volumetric energy density to **≈ 1 200 Wh L⁻¹**, figures that rival or exceed many conventional lithium‑ion chemistries.

Why it matters: The 70 % autonomy boost directly addresses one of the chief criticisms of solid‑state batteries—limited practical range due to lower energy density and manufacturing challenges. Blue Solutions’ dry‑electrode approach reduces production steps, cuts solvent waste, and improves cell uniformity, which could lower costs and accelerate scale‑up. Moreover, the use of a sulfide electrolyte mitigates dendrite formation on the lithium‑metal anode, a persistent safety concern that has hampered earlier solid‑state prototypes.

Implications for the market are significant. If the performance can be replicated at volume, Blue Solutions could position its GEN4 cells as a viable alternative for **urban transit, delivery fleets, and eventually passenger EVs**, where safety, energy density, and fast charging are paramount. The company has indicated plans to commence pilot production at its **Bordeaux facility in early 2027**, targeting a **annual output of 200 MWh** for commercial partners. This timeline aligns with several European automakers’ roadmaps for solid‑state integration by the early 2030s.

Industry observers note that the demonstration adds credibility to the broader solid‑state ecosystem, which includes players such as QuantumScape and Solid Power that are also pursuing lithium‑metal anodes but rely on different electrolyte chemistries. Blue Solutions’ progress may spur **cross‑technology collaborations**, especially in the area of dry‑electrode processing, a bottleneck that many firms cite as a barrier to mass production.

Overall, the 70 % autonomy improvement showcases that solid‑state batteries are moving beyond laboratory prototypes toward real‑world performance gains. Continued validation, cost reductions, and supply‑chain scaling will determine whether GEN4 can become a mainstream power source for next‑generation electric mobility.
