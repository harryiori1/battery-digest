## 01 CATL’s Qilin Solid‑State Battery Powers Nio’s Firefly EV with 560 km Range
Chinese battery giant CATL has taken a concrete step toward commercial solid‑state technology by equipping the Nio Firefly electric sedan with its newly announced “Qilin” solid‑state battery pack. According to CATL, the Qilin cell delivers a usable energy density of roughly 350 Wh/kg and enables a real‑world driving range of 560 km (WLTP) in the Firefly, a compact EV aimed at the mass market. The pack uses a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte that promises higher ionic conductivity than traditional oxide ceramics while maintaining thermal stability, a combination that has been a persistent hurdle for solid‑state scaling.

The announcement is significant because it marks the first time a production‑ready solid‑state pack has been installed in a vehicle slated for immediate market launch, moving the technology out of the laboratory and into the supply chain. CATL says the Qilin cells are manufactured on a pilot line capable of producing 10 GWh per year, with plans to expand to a full‑scale gigafactory by 2029. If the Firefly meets its projected sales targets, the Qilin battery could see volumes exceeding 500,000 units in the next three years, providing a real‑world data set for durability, calendar life, and safety performance.

Industry analysts view the move as a litmus test for the broader solid‑state market. The higher energy density could reduce vehicle weight and cost by allowing smaller battery packs for the same range, while the solid electrolyte eliminates the flammable liquid component, potentially easing safety regulations. However, challenges remain, including long‑term cycle stability and the cost premium of sulfide electrolytes. CATL’s aggressive rollout suggests confidence that these issues can be mitigated through economies of scale and incremental material improvements. The Qilin debut therefore serves as a bellwether for whether solid‑state batteries can transition from niche prototypes to mainstream automotive power sources. [Source: CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/24/catl-qilin-battery-nio-firefly-60-kwh/)

## 02 ProLogium Introduces ‘DCN’ Third‑State Electrolyte at the Paris Motor Show
At the 2026 Paris Motor Show, ProLogium unveiled a new electrolyte family dubbed “DCN,” which the company describes as a “third‑state” material bridging the gap between liquid and solid electrolytes. The DCN formulation combines a polymer matrix with nano‑engineered inorganic particles, delivering an ionic conductivity of 10 mS cm⁻¹ at room temperature—an order of magnitude higher than conventional sulfide or oxide solid electrolytes. In addition, the hybrid design suppresses dendrite formation on lithium metal anodes, a critical safety concern for all‑solid‑state cells.

ProLogium’s CEO highlighted that the DCN electrolyte can be processed using existing roll‑to‑roll coating lines, enabling a “dry‑electrode” manufacturing approach that eliminates the need for solvent‑based slurry steps. This could cut production costs by up to 30 % and reduce environmental impact, addressing two of the most cited barriers to solid‑state commercialization. Early prototype cells using DCN have demonstrated a specific energy of 380 Wh/kg and a discharge capacity retention of 95 % after 1,000 cycles at 45 °C, figures that rival current lithium‑ion technology while offering the safety benefits of a solid electrolyte.

The announcement positions ProLogium to compete not only with other solid‑state pioneers such as QuantumScape but also with incumbent lithium‑ion manufacturers that are exploring hybrid electrolyte solutions. By targeting both high performance and manufacturability, the DCN electrolyte could accelerate the rollout of solid‑state packs in premium EVs and stationary storage applications within the next two to three years. ProLogium plans to begin pilot‑scale production of DCN‑based cells in its Taiwan facility by early 2027, with a view to scaling to a 5 GWh annual capacity by 2030. [Source: ProLogium](https://prologium.com/prologium-unveils-dcn-opening-a-third-state-electrolyte-universe-at-paris-motor-show/)

## 03 ProLogium Starts Mass Production of 381 Wh/kg All‑Solid‑State Batteries
ProLogium announced that it has entered mass‑production mode for its latest generation of all‑solid‑state batteries, achieving a gravimetric energy density of 381 Wh/kg and a volumetric density of 903 Wh/L. The cells employ a sulfide‑based solid electrolyte paired with a lithium‑metal anode, delivering a flat‑plate design that eliminates the need for a separator and reduces cell thickness by roughly 20 % compared with conventional lithium‑ion packs. According to the company, the new batteries can sustain a 0‑100 km/h acceleration in under 3.5 seconds for a midsize sedan, while maintaining a cycle life of over 1,200 full charge‑discharge cycles at 80 % depth of discharge.

The production line, located in ProLogium’s Hsinchu “Solid‑State Hub,” utilizes a dry‑electrode coating process that deposits active material directly onto a current collector without solvents, cutting drying time and solvent‑recovery costs. The company reports a unit cost of $120 kWh⁻¹ for the first‑generation mass‑produced cells, a figure that narrows the gap with high‑performance lithium‑ion batteries and makes solid‑state packs more attractive for volume EV manufacturers.

ProLogium’s rollout targets two primary markets: premium electric vehicles and stationary energy storage for renewable integration. Early adopters include a European EV startup that plans to launch a limited‑run sports coupe powered exclusively by ProLogium’s solid‑state pack in 2028, and a utility in Taiwan that will trial the batteries for grid‑balancing services. The company also announced a partnership with a major automotive OEM to co‑develop a 70 kWh pack for a forthcoming flagship sedan, with volume production slated for 2029.

Industry observers view the mass‑production milestone as a decisive proof point that solid‑state technology can move beyond small‑batch pilot cells. The combination of high energy density, improved safety, and a scalable dry‑electrode process suggests that solid‑state batteries could soon compete on cost and performance with incumbent lithium‑ion chemistries, potentially reshaping the EV powertrain landscape. [Source: ProLogium](https://prologium.com/prologium-begins-mass-production-of-high-energy-density-all-solid-state-battery-reaching-381-wh-kg-and-903-wh-l/)
