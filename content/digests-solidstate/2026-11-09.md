## 01 ProLogium and Rimac Advance Next‑Generation Solid‑State EV Battery
ProLogium Technology Co., a Taiwanese leader in solid‑state electrolytes, announced a joint development program with Rimac Technology, the high‑performance EV subsidiary of Croatian hypercar maker Rimac Automobili. The partnership will focus on a next‑generation all‑solid‑state lithium‑metal cell that targets an energy density exceeding 500 Wh/kg—well above the 250‑300 Wh/kg typical of today’s commercial lithium‑ion packs [ProLogium Press Release](https://prologium.com/prologium-and-rimac-technology-advance-next-generation-ev-battery-development/).

The collaboration leverages ProLogium’s proprietary sulfide‑based solid electrolyte, which offers high ionic conductivity (≈10 mS cm⁻¹ at room temperature) and excellent mechanical stability, paired with Rimac’s expertise in high‑power cell architecture and thermal management. Early laboratory prototypes have demonstrated a specific capacity of 350 mAh g⁻¹ at a current density of 1 C, while maintaining a cycle life of more than 500 full charge‑discharge cycles without significant capacity fade [Technical Brief, Solid State Battery Review, 2025](https://www.ssbreview.com/2025/solid-electrolyte-performance).

Why this matters: achieving 500 Wh/kg would enable electric vehicles to match or surpass the range‑per‑weight of internal‑combustion cars while reducing battery pack mass by roughly 30 %. The higher energy density also opens the door for lighter, more efficient powertrains in performance models—a segment where Rimac already excels. Moreover, the solid‑state chemistry eliminates the need for liquid electrolytes, improving safety by mitigating thermal runaway risks.

The joint roadmap outlines a three‑phase timeline: (1) scale‑up of electrolyte production to 10 tonnes per year by 2027; (2) pilot‑line cell fabrication targeting 200 Ah modules by early 2028; and (3) integration into a limited‑run hypercar platform for road testing in 2029. If successful, the technology could cascade to mainstream OEMs through licensing agreements, accelerating the broader industry shift toward solid‑state batteries.

Industry observers note that the partnership underscores a growing trend of traditional EV manufacturers teaming with specialist solid‑state firms to bridge the “valley of death” between lab breakthroughs and mass production [Electrochemical Society Outlook, 2026](https://www.electrochem.org/outlook/2026/solid-state-commercialization). ProLogium’s move to align with a high‑performance OEM like Rimac signals confidence that its electrolyte platform can meet the demanding power and durability requirements of next‑generation electric vehicles.
